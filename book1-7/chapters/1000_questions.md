I was once challenged by someone asking: "What if just one number in fundamental physics was different?" In my mind, this was as if they were asking what if one number of pi was different of if the angle of a right triangle was different. However, they continued "Why, then life and chemical bonds themselves would be impossible!" There are two related problems with this line of thinking.

First, constant numbers in physics and geometry are descriptors; even fundamental constants where humans have yet to fully understand the underlying physics, there is no magical calculator somewhere that holds these number in; they are a description of something physical. It is always incorrect to say "What if this or that number was different?" The problem with that style of question, as stated before, is that numbers are descriptors of reality; there is a physical structural process that numbers may describe; the most basic of all descriptors are ratios, such as musical harmonics and pi (ratio of diameter to circumference). So to state such a question in a valid way you would need to state what physical or geometric process you are actually suggested be different.

The second problem is that if the physical or geometric process was different, it would not be different alone. This is an important concept; the ratio we call pi, the numbers in fundamental physics, the musical harmonic ratios, are all particular mechanisms from a large interconnected set of mechanisms. These mechanisms are not isolates, they cannot be plucked up and altered without altering other more fundamental mechanisms.

While I mulled over how how fundamental mechanisms are related and particular, another statement was made: "Without divine authority there is not basis for morality." This is an interesting statement, one that I grappled with, and one that we will come back to. For now let's step to a question many people have asked "What if two people switched minds?" or "How would you respond if you were someone in a distant country? or even "What would you want if you were someone in much worse off then yourself?" The problem with these statements, by themselves, is that man is himself particular. Each person has an inheritance of a particular biology, a particular family, and a particular social group they grew up in, and a particular mind which is a part of the rest our our biological body. Just like with physics or geometry, you cannot in isolation ask "What if this number were different?" you must instead ask "What if this mechanism was different, and what else would it affect?"
