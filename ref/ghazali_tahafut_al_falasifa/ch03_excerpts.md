## 1. The first of four points of natural science in dispute

Introduction to the natural sciences, before the Seventeenth Discussion. Shamela line 1391; Dunya p. 233; Kamali p. 181.

> الأولى حكمهم بأن هذا الاقتران المشاهد في الوجود بين الأسباب والمسببات اقتران تلازم بالضرورة، فليس في المقدور ولا في الإمكان إيجاد السبب دون المسبب ولا وجود المسبب دون السبب.

The first is their judgment that this connection observed in existence between causes and effects is a connection of necessary concomitance, so that it is neither within [divine] power nor possible to bring the cause into existence without the effect, or the effect without the cause.

Just before this, al-Ghazali writes that religion does not require opposing the philosophers in any of the natural sciences except on four points (line 1388: "وليس يلزم مخالفتهم شرعاً في شيء من هذه العلوم وإنما نخالفهم من جملة هذه العلوم في أربعة مسائل"). The other three concern the soul and the return of souls to bodies.

## 2. Why the first point must be disputed: the miracles

Shamela line 1396; Dunya p. 234; Kamali pp. 181–182.

> وإنما يلزم النزاع في الأولى من حيث أنه ينبنى عليها إثبات المعجزات الخارقة للعادة من قلب العصا ثعباناً وإحياء الموتى وشق القمر. ومن جعل مجاري العادات لازمة لزوماً ضرورياً أحال جميع ذلك. وأولوا ما في القرآن من إحياء الموتى وقالوا: أراد به إزالة موت الجهل بحياة العلم. وأولوا تلقف العصا سحر السحرة على إبطال الحجة الإلهية الظاهرة على يد موسى شبهات المنكرين، وأما شق القمر فربما أنكروا وجوده وزعموا أنه لم يتواتر.

The dispute over the first [point] is necessary only because the affirmation of the miracles that break the habitual course depends on it (literally, "is built upon it"): the turning of the staff into a serpent, the bringing of the dead to life, and the splitting of the moon. Whoever makes the habitual courses of things necessary, with a necessary concomitance, has made all of that impossible. They have interpreted what is in the Qur'an about bringing the dead to life, saying that it means the removal of the death of ignorance by the life of knowledge. They have interpreted the staff's swallowing of the sorcerers' sorcery as the divine proof, made manifest by the hand of Moses, overturning the doubts of those who denied. As for the splitting of the moon, they often deny that it happened and claim that it was not transmitted by mass transmission (*tawatur*).

Reading: Shamela has "لو يتواتر". The 9th printing (OCR line 7728) has "لم يتواتر", and Kamali translates it the same way ("has not been continuous and trustworthy"). "لم" is correct.

The words *thu'ban* (serpent) and *talaqquf* (swallowing) are the words of Qur'an 7:107 / 26:32 and 7:117 / 20:69 / 26:45. See `ext-ref/quran/ch03_verses.md` (repo root; not committed).

## 3. The two reasons for the inquiry

End of the introduction. Shamela line 1416; Dunya p. 236; Kamali p. 184.

> فهذا مذهبهم في المعجزات ونحن لا ننكر شيئاً مما ذكروه وأن ذلك مما يكون للأنبياء وإنما ننكر اقتصارهم عليه ومنعهم قلب العصا ثعباناً وإحياء الموتى وغيره فلزم الخوض في هذه المسألة لإثبات المعجزات ولأمر آخر وهو نصرة ما أطبق عليه المسلمون من أن الله قادر على كل شيء.

This, then, is their doctrine of miracles. We deny nothing they have mentioned, and such things do belong to prophets. What we deny is their stopping there, and their refusal [to allow] the turning of the staff into a serpent, the bringing of the dead to life, and the like. So it became necessary to take up this question: to affirm the miracles, and for another matter, namely to support what the Muslims have agreed upon, that God has power over all things.

## 4. Opening of the Seventeenth Discussion

Shamela line 1419; Dunya p. 237; Bouyges p. 277 (Griffel n. 145); Kamali p. 185.

> الاقتران بين ما يعتقد في العادة سبباً وما يعتقد مسبباً ليس ضرورياً عندنا بل كل شيئين ليس هذا ذاك ولا ذاك هذا، ولا إثبات أحدهما متضمن لإثبات الآخر ولا نفيه متضمن لنفي الآخر، فليس من ضرورة وجود أحدهما وجود الآخر ولا من ضرورة عدم أحدهما عدم الآخر

The connection between what is habitually believed to be a cause and what is believed to be an effect is not, in our view, necessary. Of any two things, this one is not that one and that one is not this one. The affirmation of one does not include the affirmation of the other, and the denial of one does not include the denial of the other. The existence of one does not necessitate the existence of the other, and the nonexistence of one does not necessitate the nonexistence of the other.

Same paragraph, continued:

> وإن اقترانها لما سبق من تقدير الله سبحانه يخلقها على التساوق لا لكونه ضرورياً في نفسه غير قابل للفرق بل في المقدور خلق الشبع دون الأكل وخلق الموت دون جز الرقبة وإدامة الحيوة مع جز الرقبة

Their connection is due to the prior decree of God, who creates them side by side, not to its being necessary in itself and incapable of separation. On the contrary, it is within [God's] power to create satiety without eating, to create death without the cutting of the neck, and to continue life after the neck is cut.

Griffel (n. 145) notes that the manuscripts read *hazz al-raqaba* (an incision in the neck), not *jazz* (cutting off). Translations that say "decapitation" follow the misreading.

## 5. Fire and cotton

Shamela lines 1429 and 1432; Dunya p. 238; Kamali p. 186.

> فاعل الاحتراق بخلق السواد في القطن والتفرق في أجزائه وجعله حراقاً أو رماداً هو الله إما بواسطة الملائكة أو بغير واسطة، فأما النار وهي جماد فلا فعل لها.

The agent of the burning, by creating blackness in the cotton and the separation of its parts, and by making it a burnt mass or ash, is God, either through the mediation of angels or without mediation. As for fire, it is inanimate and has no action.

> فما الدليل على أنها الفاعل وليس له دليل إلا مشاهدة حصول الاحتراق عند ملاقاة النار؟ والمشاهدة تدل على الحصول عنده ولا تدل على الحصول به وأنه لا علة سواه

What is the proof that it [fire] is the agent? He [the opponent] has no proof except the observation that burning occurs upon contact with the fire. Observation shows that it occurs with it [the contact], and does not show that it occurs by it, or that there is no cause other than it.

Note for the chapter's first part (line 32): the chapter says God "directly wills and creates" the burning. The text says "either through the mediation of angels or without mediation".

## 6. Abraham in the fire

The philosophers' position, as al-Ghazali reports it, in the "second standpoint" of the discussion. Shamela line 1446; Dunya p. 241; Kamali p. 188.

> وعن هذا المعنى أنكروا وقوع إبراهيم في النار مع عدم الاحتراق وبقاء النار ناراً وزعموا أن ذلك لا يمكن إلا بسلب الحرارة من النار وذلك يخرجه عن كونه ناراً أو بقلب ذات إبراهيم وبدنه حجراً أو شيئاً لا يؤثر فيه النار، ولا هذا ممكن ولا ذاك ممكن.

On this ground they denied that Abraham fell into the fire without being burned while the fire remained fire. They claimed that this could happen only by taking the heat away from the fire, which would make it no longer fire, or by changing Abraham's essence and body into stone or something on which fire has no effect; and that neither this nor that is possible.

Al-Ghazali gives two answers. In the first (line 1449), God acts by will, so it is possible in reason that He not create the burning when the contact occurs. In the second (line 1467 onward), he grants that fire was created so that it burns two identical pieces of cotton alike, and holds that God or the angels may change an attribute of the fire or of the prophet so that the prophet is not burned. As evidence that such an attribute is possible, he says that a man who coats himself with talc can sit in a lit oven unharmed (line 1470).

## 7. The objection that nothing could be known, and the reply

Objection (the book that may have become a horse): Shamela line 1455; Dunya pp. 241–244; Kamali p. 189.

> ومن وضع كتاباً في بيته فليجوز أن يكون قد انقلب عند رجوعه إلى بيته غلاماً أمرد عاقلاً متصرفاً أو انقلب حيواناً

Whoever leaves a book in his house should allow that it may have turned, by the time he returns home, into a beardless slave youth, intelligent and busy at his work, or into an animal.

Reply: Shamela line 1460; Dunya p. 245; Kamali p. 189.

> فإن الله خلق لنا علماً بأن هذه الممكنات لم يفعلها، ولم ندع أن هذه الأمور واجبة بل هي ممكنة يجوز أن تقع ويجوز أن لا تقع، واستمرار العادة بها مرة بعد أخرى يرسخ في أذهاننا جريانها على وفق العادة الماضية ترسخاً لا تنفك عنه.

God has created in us the knowledge that He has not done these possible things. We have not claimed that these things are necessary. They are possible: they may happen and may not happen. The continuation of the habit, time after time, fixes firmly in our minds that they will run according to the past habit, so firmly that we cannot free ourselves of it.

Shamela line 1463; Dunya p. 245; Bouyges 286.7–8 / Marmura 171.7–8 (Griffel n. 105); Kamali p. 190.

> فإن خرق الله العادة بإيقاعها في زمان خرق العادات فيها انسلت هذه العلوم عن القلوب ولم يخلقها.

If God breaks the habit by making [such a thing] happen at a time when habits are broken, these cognitions slip away from the hearts, and He does not create them.

## 8. The Twentieth Discussion (bodily resurrection): the Qur'an cited against him

The philosophers' objection, as al-Ghazali states it. Shamela line 1985; Dunya p. 304; Kamali p. 246.

> فإن قيل: الفعل الإلهي له مجرى واحد مضروب لا يتغير ولذلك قال تعالى: "وما أمرنا إلا واحدة كلمح بالبصر" وقال: "ولن تجد لسنة الله تبديلا"

If it is said: the divine act has one fixed course that does not change, and for that reason God, exalted, said, "And Our command is but one, like a glance of the eye" [Qur'an 54:50], and said, "You will never find any change in the way of God" [Qur'an 33:62; 48:23; compare 35:43] ...

His answer (lines 2004–2005) is that the objection relies on the eternity of the world, already refuted, and that reason does not exclude God creating the present order and then beginning a second one.

Close of the Twentieth Discussion. Shamela line 2008; Dunya p. 306; Kamali p. 248.

> وهذه المسألة كيف ما رددت تنبنى على مسئلتين إحديهما حدث العالم وجواز حصول حادث من قديم الثانية خرق العادات بخلق المسببات دون الأسباب أو إحداث أسباب على منهج آخر غير معتاد، وقد فرغنا عن المسئلتين جميعاً.

However this question is turned, it rests on two questions. The first is the origination of the world in time and the possibility that a temporal thing comes from an eternal one. The second is the breaking of habits, by creating effects without causes or by bringing causes into being in another, unaccustomed manner. We have finished with both questions.

## 9. Conclusion of the book: the three points of unbelief

Shamela lines 2011–2017; Dunya pp. 307–308; Kamali p. 249.

> فإن قال قائل: قد فصلتم مذاهب هؤلاء أفتقطعون القول بكفرهم ووجوب القتل لمن يعتقد اعتقادهم؟ قلنا: تكفيرهم لا بد منه في ثلث مسائل إحديها مسألة قدم العالم وقولهم إن الجواهر كلها قديمة والثانية قولهم إن الله لا يحيط علماً بالجزئيات الحادثة من الأشخاص والثالثة في إنكارهم بعث الأجساد وحشرها، فهذه المسائل الثلث لا تلائم الإسلام بوجه ومعتقدها معتقد كذب الأنبياء

If someone says, "You have set out the doctrines of these men in detail. Do you then pronounce definitively that they are unbelievers, and that killing is obligatory for whoever holds their belief?" we say: pronouncing them unbelievers is unavoidable in three questions. The first is the question of the eternity of the world and their saying that all substances are eternal. The second is their saying that God's knowledge does not encompass the temporal particulars among individual things. The third is their denial of the resurrection and gathering of bodies. These three questions do not agree with Islam in any respect, and whoever believes them believes that the prophets lied.

Shamela line 2021; Dunya p. 309; Kamali p. 249.

> فأما ما عدا هذه المسائل الثلث من تصرفهم في الصفات الإلهية واعتقاد التوحيد فيها فمذهبهم قريب من مذاهب المعتزلة ومذهبهم في تلازم الأسباب الطبيعية هو الذي صرح المعتزلة به في التولد

As for what lies outside these three questions, in their treatment of the divine attributes and their belief about God's unity, their doctrine is close to the doctrines of the Mu'tazila. Their doctrine of the necessary concomitance of natural causes is the one the Mu'tazila stated openly in [their doctrine of] generation (*tawallud*).

Note: the necessity of natural causes is not one of the three points of unbelief. It supports the third (bodily resurrection), as section 8 shows.

# Ibn Rushd, *Tahafut al-Tahafut*: reply to the Seventeenth Discussion

File: `ext-ref/ibn_rushd_tahafut_al_tahafut/tahafut_al_tahafut_jabiri_ar_ocr.txt` (repo root; not committed), OCR of the edition published by the Centre for Arab Unity Studies (Markaz Dirasat al-Wahda al-'Arabiyya), Beirut, with an introduction by Muhammad 'Abid al-Jabiri, archive.org item `ar119phil21`. Lines 18612–18636, paragraphs marked [ز] and, in the OCR, [1] (the editor's next paragraph letter). Bouyges's edition of *Tahafut al-Tahafut* (Beirut, 1930), p. 522.8, per Griffel n. 198.

> والعقل ليس هو شيء أكثر من إدراكه الموجودات بأسبابها ... فمن رفع الأسباب فقد رفع العقل. وصناعة المنطق تضع وضعا أن هاهنا أسبابا ومسببات، وأن المعرفة بتلك المسببات لا تكون على التمام إلا بمعرفة أسبابها. فرفع هذه الأشياء هو مبطل للعلم ورافع له.

The intellect is nothing more than its perception of existing things through their causes ... Whoever removes the causes removes the intellect. The art of logic lays down that there are causes and effects, and that knowledge of those effects is complete only through knowledge of their causes. To remove these things nullifies knowledge and does away with it.

The ellipsis covers one clause that the OCR garbles (line 18612). It probably reads "وبه يفترق من سائر القوى المدركة" (by this it is distinguished from the other faculties of perception), but this has not been checked against a clean text.

> ومحال أن يكون لله تعالى عادة: فإن العادة ملكة يكتسبها الفاعل توجب تكرر الفعل منه على الأكثر، والله عز وجل يقول: "ولن تجد لسنة الله تبديلا"، "ولن تجد لسنة الله تحويلا".

It is impossible that God, exalted, should have a habit. A habit is a disposition that an agent acquires, which causes him to repeat an act for the most part. And God, mighty and majestic, says: "You will never find any change in the way of God" [33:62; 48:23], and "You will never find any alteration in the way of God" [35:43].

The OCR shows the editor's verse references as "(الأحزاب 57)" and "(فاطر .)4٠". The verified locations are 33:62 and 35:43 (see `ext-ref/quran/ch03_verses.md`).
