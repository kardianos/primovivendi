## 1. The good (Principle 3): the five necessities and their ranking

### 1.1 Al-Ghazali (d. 505/1111), al-Mustasfa: Shafi'i and Ash'ari, mainstream

> وَمَقْصُودُ الشَّرْعِ مِنْ الْخَلْقِ خَمْسَةٌ: وَهُوَ أَنْ يَحْفَظَ عَلَيْهِمْ دِينَهُمْ وَنَفْسَهُمْ وَعَقْلَهُمْ وَنَسْلَهُمْ وَمَالَهُمْ، فَكُلُّ مَا يَتَضَمَّنُ حِفْظَ هَذِهِ الْأُصُولِ الْخَمْسَةِ فَهُوَ مَصْلَحَةٌ، وَكُلُّ مَا يُفَوِّتُ هَذِهِ الْأُصُولَ فَهُوَ مَفْسَدَةٌ وَدَفْعُهَا مَصْلَحَةٌ ... وَهَذِهِ الْأُصُولُ الْخَمْسَةُ حِفْظُهَا وَاقِعٌ فِي رُتْبَةِ الضَّرُورَاتِ، فَهِيَ أَقْوَى الْمَرَاتِبِ فِي الْمَصَالِحِ وَمِثَالُهُ: قَضَاءُ الشَّرْعِ بِقَتْلِ الْكَافِرِ الْمُضِلِّ وَعُقُوبَةِ الْمُبْتَدِعِ الدَّاعِي إلَى بِدْعَتِهِ، فَإِنَّ هَذَا يُفَوِّتُ عَلَى الْخَلْقِ دِينَهُمْ، وَقَضَاؤُهُ بِإِيجَابِ الْقِصَاصِ أَدَبُهُ حِفْظُ النُّفُوسِ، وَإِيجَابُ حَدِّ الشُّرْبِ إذْ بِهِ حِفْظُ الْعُقُولِ الَّتِي هِيَ مِلَاكُ التَّكْلِيفِ وَإِيجَابُ حَدِّ الزِّنَا إذْ بِهِ حِفْظُ النَّسْلِ وَالْأَنْسَابِ، وَإِيجَابُ زَجْرِ الْغُصَّابِ وَالسُّرَّاقِ إذْ بِهِ يَحْصُلُ حِفْظُ الْأَمْوَالِ الَّتِي هِيَ مَعَاشُ الْخَلْقِ وَهُمْ مُضْطَرُّونَ إلَيْهَا.

Translation: "The Law's aim for creatures is five things: that it preserve for them their religion, their life, their intellect, their offspring, and their property. Whatever secures the preservation of these five foundations is a benefit (maslaha). Whatever causes them to be lost is a harm (mafsada), and repelling that harm is a benefit. ... The preservation of these five foundations stands at the rank of necessities, the strongest rank of benefits. Examples: the Law's ruling that the misleading unbeliever be killed and that the innovator who calls others to his innovation be punished, because these would cause people to lose their religion. Its imposing retaliation (qisas), by which lives are preserved. The penalty for drinking, by which intellects are preserved, and the intellect is the basis of legal responsibility. The penalty for zina, by which offspring and lineages are preserved. And the deterring of usurpers and thieves, by which property is preserved, property being the livelihood of creatures, which they cannot do without."

- **Order as written**: religion (din), life (nafs), intellect (aql), offspring (nasl), property (mal).
- **Text problem**: the printed text reads:

  > أَدَبُهُ حِفْظُ النُّفُوسِ

  It reads this way in both the Wikisource and the Shamela texts. The parallel clauses read "idh bihi" ("since by it"), so the printed word is probably a misprint for that. The translation above follows that reading.
- **Source**: al-Mustasfa, ed. Muhammad Abd al-Salam Abd al-Shafi (Beirut: Dar al-Kutub al-Ilmiyya, 1993), **p. 174**. Read in the Shamela export at https://archive.org/details/2641Htm and cross-checked against https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/المستصفى/الجزء_الأول (the Wikisource text has no page markers).
- **Status**: verified (primary, digital text of the named edition).

**Does al-Ghazali rank religion above life?** In this passage he lists religion first, and his first example of protecting religion is a death penalty: killing the "misleading unbeliever." He does not state a ranking rule for when religion and life conflict. In the same section, on human shields (tatarrus), he weighs life against life rather than religion against life. He allows shooting through Muslim captives used as shields only when three conditions all hold: the necessity is real (daruriyya), it is certain (qat'iyya), and it concerns the whole community (kulliyya).

> وَلَوْ كَفَفْنَا لَسَلَّطْنَا الْكُفَّارَ عَلَى جَمِيعِ الْمُسْلِمِينَ ... لِأَنَّا نَعْلَمُ قَطْعًا أَنَّ مَقْصُودَ الشَّرْعِ تَقْلِيلُ الْقَتْلِ كَمَا يَقْصِدُ حَسْمَ سَبِيلِهِ عِنْدَ الْإِمْكَانِ ... وَانْقَدَحَ اعْتِبَارُهَا بِاعْتِبَارِ ثَلَاثَةِ أَوْصَافٍ أَنَّهَا ضَرُورَةٌ قَطْعِيَّةٌ كُلِّيَّةٌ

Translation: "If we held back, we would hand all the Muslims over to the unbelievers. ... We know with certainty that the Law aims to reduce killing, just as it aims to cut off the way to killing where that is possible. ... Taking this benefit into account becomes clear through three qualities: it is a necessity, it is certain, and it is general."

The same section rejects throwing one person off a ship to save the rest, and rejects starving people eating one of their number chosen by lot. Neither case is "general" (kulliyya). Source: al-Mustasfa p. 175. A related line is on p. 177:

> نَحْنُ نَعْلَمُ أَنَّ الشَّرْعَ يُؤْثِرُ الْكُلِّيَّ عَلَى الْجُزْئِيِّ

Translation: "We know that the Law prefers the general to the particular." Status: verified (primary).

### 1.2 Al-Amidi (d. 631/1233), al-Ihkam fi Usul al-Ahkam: Ash'ari and Shafi'i, mainstream

This is the explicit mainstream ranking. Among the grounds for preferring one legal cause over another, his seventeenth is:

> السابع عشر: أن يكون مقصود إحدى العلتين حفظ أصل الدين ومقصود الأخرى ما سواه من المقاصد الضرورية فما مقصوده حفظ أصل الدين يكون أولى نظراً إلى مقصوده وثمرته من نيل السعادة الأبدية في جوار رب العالمين وما سواه من حفظ الأنفس والعقل والمال وغيره فإنما كان مقصوداً من أجله

Translation: "Seventeenth: that one of two causes aims at preserving the foundation of religion and the other aims at one of the other necessary aims. The one aimed at preserving the foundation of religion takes precedence, because of its aim and its fruit, which is attaining eternal happiness near the Lord of the worlds. Everything besides it, the preservation of lives, intellect, property and so on, is aimed at only for its sake."

Al-Amidi then answers an objection that life should come first because human rights are strict and God's rights are lenient. His answers include two notable claims:

> فإنا نقتله قصاصاً لا بكفره

Translation: when a man has both disbelieved and committed murder, "we kill him in retaliation, not for his unbelief." He means the human claim is paid first, while the claim of religion survives in the afterlife.

> وبقاء الذمي بين أظهر المسلمين معصوم الدم والمال ليس لمصلحة المسلمين بل لأجل اطلاعه على محاسن الشريعة وقواعد الدين ليسهل انقياده ويتيسر استرشاده وذلك من مصلحة الدين

Translation: "The dhimmi's remaining among the Muslims with his blood and property protected is not for the Muslims' benefit. It is so that he may come to know the merits of the Law and the principles of the religion, so that his submission becomes easier and his guidance more likely. That is a benefit of religion."

His full order of precedence is religion, then life, then lineage, then intellect, then property:

> فكذلك ما يتعلق به من مقصود النفس يكون مقدماً على غيره من المقاصد الضرورية ... وعلى هذا أيضاً يكون المقصود في حفظ النسب أولى من المقصود في حفظ العقل ومقدم على ما يفضي إلى حفظ المال

Translation: "Likewise, what concerns the aim of life comes before the other necessary aims. ... On this basis too, the aim of preserving lineage takes precedence over the aim of preserving intellect, and comes before what leads to preserving property."

- **Source**: https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/إحكام_الآمدي_-_الجزء_السادس. This is Wikisource's sixth part, on preference (tarjih), and it has no print page markers.
- **Status**: verified (primary). The printed volume and page were not verified.

### 1.3 Al-Shatibi (d. 790/1388), al-Muwafaqat: Maliki, mainstream

> فأما الضرورية فمعناها أنها لا بد منها في قيام مصالح الدين والدنيا بحيث إذا فقدت لم تجر مصالح الدنيا على استقامة بل على فساد وتهارج وفوت حياة وفي الأخرى فوت النجاة والنعيم والرجوع بالخسران المبين

Translation: "The necessary [aims] are those that cannot be done without for the interests of religion and of this world to stand. If they are lost, the interests of this world do not run straight but run to corruption, chaos and loss of life, and in the next world there is loss of salvation and bliss and a return in manifest loss."

> ومجموع الضروريات خمسة وهي حفظ الدين والنفس والنسل والمال والعقل وقد قالوا إنها مراعاة في كل ملة

Translation: "The necessities total five: preservation of religion, life, offspring, property and intellect. They [the scholars] have said these are observed in every religion."

He explains how the five depend on one another:

> فلو عدم الدين عدم ترتب الجزاء المرتجى ولو عدم المكلف لعدم من يتدين ولو عدم العقل لارتفع التدين ولو عدم النسل لم يكن في العادة بقاء ولو عدم المال لم يبق عيش

Translation: "If religion were gone, the hoped-for reward would not follow. If the responsible person were gone, there would be no one to practise religion. If intellect were gone, religious practice would cease. If offspring were gone, there would ordinarily be no continuation. If property were gone, there would be no living."

- **Order as written here**: religion, life, offspring, property, intellect. This differs from al-Ghazali's order. The lists are not fixed orderings in every text.
- **Source**: https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/موافقات_الشاطبي_-_الجزء_الثاني. The Wikisource page markers are pp. 8, 10 and 17 of part 2, which matches the commonly cited "al-Muwafaqat 2/8–10". The edition is not named on the page.
- **Status**: verified (primary).

### 1.4 Ibn Taymiyya (d. 728/1328): Salafi reference point

**(a) He objects that the five are too narrow.**

> وجعلوا الدنيوية ما تضمن حفظ الدماء والأموال والفروج والعقول والدين الظاهر، وأعرضوا عما في العبادات الباطنة والظاهرة من أنواع المعارف بالله تعالى ... وكذلك فيما شرعه الشارع من الوفاء بالعهود وصلة الأرحام، وحقوق المماليك والجيران، وحقوق المسلمين بعضهم على بعض

Translation: "They made the worldly [benefits] whatever secures the preservation of blood, property, sexual honour, intellects and outward religion. They turned away from the kinds of knowledge of God in inward and outward worship ... and likewise from what the Lawgiver laid down: keeping covenants, maintaining ties of kinship, the rights of slaves and of neighbours, and the rights Muslims have over one another."

Source: Majmu' al-Fatawa vol. 32, in his answer on playing chess, at https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/مجموع_الفتاوى/المجلد_الثاني_والثلاثون. It is commonly cited as 32:234; that page number was not verified.

> لكن بعض الناس يخص المصالح المرسلة بحفظ النفوس والأموال والأعراض والعقول والأديان. وليس كذلك، بل المصالح المرسلة في جلب المنافع وفي دفع المضار، وما ذكروه من دفع المضار عن هذه الأمور الخمسة فهو أحد القسمين

Translation: "Some people restrict unregulated benefits (al-masalih al-mursala) to preserving lives, property, honour, intellects and religions. That is not so. Such benefits lie both in bringing about goods and in warding off harms. What they mention, warding off harm from these five things, is only one of the two parts."

Source: Majmu' al-Fatawa vol. 11, "فصل في العلم بالكائنات", at https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/مجموع_الفتاوى/المجلد_الحادي_عشر/فصل_في_العلم_بالكائنات. Page not verified.

Status of both passages: verified (primary). Note that his list of omitted goods includes kinship ties and the rights of neighbours, which bears on Principle 4.

**(b) Religion above life in jihad.** From al-Siyasa al-Shar'iyya:

> وإذا كان أصل القتال المشروع هو الجهاد ومقصوده هو أن يكون الدين كله لله وأن تكون كلمة الله هي العليا فمن منع هذا قوتل باتفاق المسلمين وأما من لم يكن من أهل الممانعة والمقاتلة كالنساء والصبيان والراهب والشيخ الكبير والأعمى والزمن ونحوهم فلا يقتل عند جمهور العلماء ... وذلك أن الله تعالى أباح من قتل النفوس ما يحتاج إليه في صلاح الخلق كما قال الله تعالى: {والفتنة أكبر من القتل} أي أن القتل وإن كان فيه شر وفساد ففي فتنة الكفار من الشر والفساد ما هو أكبر منه فمن لم يمنع المسلمين من إقامة دين الله لم تكن مضرة كفره إلا على نفسه

Translation: "Since the basis of lawful fighting is jihad, and its aim is that religion be wholly for God and that God's word be uppermost, whoever obstructs this is fought, by agreement of the Muslims. Those who are not people of resistance and fighting, such as women, children, monks, the very old, the blind, the chronically ill and the like, are not killed according to the majority of scholars. ... This is because God permitted the killing of souls as far as it is needed for the welfare of creation, as He said: 'and fitna is greater than killing' [2:217]. That is, killing contains evil and corruption, but the fitna of the unbelievers contains greater evil and corruption. So whoever does not prevent the Muslims from establishing God's religion, the harm of his unbelief falls on himself alone."

- **Source**: https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/السياسة_الشرعية_في_إصلاح_الراعي_والرعية/4 (no page markers). Status: verified (primary).
- **Qur'an**: both verses below were verified three ways.

  > والفتنة أكبر من القتل

  2:217, "fitna is greater than killing."

  > والفتنة أشد من القتل

  2:191, "fitna is more severe than killing."

### 1.5 What follows in law from ranking religion first

- **Apostasy, the hadith**: Bukhari 6922 has Ibn Abbas citing the following, with the same words in Bukhari 3017. Verified in hadith-api.

  > مَنْ بَدَّلَ دِينَهُ فَاقْتُلُوهُ

  Translation: "Whoever changes his religion, kill him."
- **Apostasy, Salafi**: Ibn Baz, introducing Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab's list of the nullifiers of Islam (source details in §2.3), wrote:

  > المسلم قد يرتد عن دينه بأنواع كثيرة من النواقض التي تحل دمه وماله

  Translation: "A Muslim may apostatize from his religion through many kinds of nullifiers that make his blood and property lawful." Verified (primary), https://binbaz.org.sa/articles/20.
- **Apostasy, mainstream today (Dar al-Ifta, Egypt, research paper dated 27 April 2016)**:

  > لم تعنِ الخروج من الإسلام بقدر ما عنت الخروج على الإسلام الذي يُعدُّ جرمًا ضد النظام العام في الدولة ... ويُعتَبر حينذاك مرادفًا لجريمة الخيانة العظمى

  Translation: the severe texts "did not mean leaving Islam so much as rising against Islam, which counts as a crime against the public order of the state ... and was then the equivalent of the crime of high treason."

  The paper quotes Mahmud Shaltut (Shaykh al-Azhar 1958–1963):

  > وأن الكفر بنفسه ليس مبيحًا للدم، وإنما المبيح هو محاربة المسلمين والعدوان عليهم ومحاولة فتنتهم عن دينهم

  Translation: "Unbelief in itself does not make blood lawful; what makes it lawful is waging war on the Muslims, aggressing against them, and trying to turn them from their religion."

  Source: https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwaresearch/details/2312/. Status: verified (primary, official site).
- **Jihad**: Ibn Taymiyya (above) bases lawful killing on removing the obstruction of religion, while sparing non-combatants. Al-Amidi says a dhimmi's protection is justified by the interest of religion.
- **Where they agree and differ**:
  - The classical mainstream (al-Ghazali, al-Amidi) and the Salafi reference author (Ibn Taymiyya) all put religion first. Al-Amidi states it as an explicit rule.
  - The Salafi distinctive here is not the ranking itself. It is Ibn Taymiyya's complaint that the five-list leaves out worship, covenants, kinship and neighbours.
  - Today they diverge on apostasy. Dar al-Ifta reads the death penalty as a penalty for treason-like hostility. The Salafi texts tie it to the act of nullification itself.

### 1.6 Pro-natal teaching

**The hadith of the "loving, fertile" woman (Abu Dawud 2050)**

> جَاءَ رَجُلٌ إِلَى النَّبِيِّ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَقَالَ إِنِّي أَصَبْتُ امْرَأَةً ذَاتَ حَسَبٍ وَجَمَالٍ وَإِنَّهَا لاَ تَلِدُ أَفَأَتَزَوَّجُهَا قَالَ " لاَ " . ثُمَّ أَتَاهُ الثَّانِيَةَ فَنَهَاهُ ثُمَّ أَتَاهُ الثَّالِثَةَ فَقَالَ " تَزَوَّجُوا الْوَدُودَ الْوَلُودَ فَإِنِّي مُكَاثِرٌ بِكُمُ الأُمَمَ "

Translation: "A man came to the Prophet and said: I have found a woman of rank and beauty, but she does not bear children; shall I marry her? He said: No. He came a second time and he forbade him; he came a third time and he said: Marry the loving, fertile woman, for I will outnumber the nations through you."

- **Source**: Sunan Abi Dawud 2050, narrated by Ma'qil b. Yasar.
- **Gradings, as given in hadith-api**: al-Albani "hasan sahih"; Shu'ayb al-Arna'ut "sahih"; Muhammad Muhyi al-Din Abd al-Hamid "hasan sahih"; Zubair Ali Zai "hasan".
- **Status**: text and number verified in hadith-api. Dar al-Ifta fatwa 4713 (below) quotes the same text and credits it to Abu Dawud. It also cites a version in Ahmad's Musnad that ends:

  > مكاثر بكم الأنبياء يوم القيامة

  Translation: "[I will] outnumber the prophets through you on the Day of Resurrection."

**Mainstream: Dar al-Ifta, Egypt, fatwa no. 4713, 5 February 2019 (Shawqi Allam)**

> عدمُ الإنجابِ هو حقٌّ للزوجين معًا، ويجوز لهما الاتفاقُ عليه إذا كان في ذلك مصلحة تخصُّهما، ولا يجوز لأحدهما دون موافقة الآخر، وهذا الجواز على المستوى الفردي، أَمَّا على مستوى الأمة فلا يجوزُ المنْعُ المطلق من الإنجاب؛ لما فيه من الإخلال بنسبة التوازن التي أقام الله الخلق عليها، ولا يدخل فيها ما تقوم به الدول من إجراءات للعمل على تحديد النسل طلبًا للحياة الكريمة لشعوبها ... فتصرف ولي الأمر منوط بالمصلحة.

Translation: "Not having children is a right of the two spouses together. They may agree on it if it serves an interest of theirs, and neither may do it without the other's consent. This permission is at the individual level. At the level of the community (umma), an absolute prevention of childbearing is not permitted, because it upsets the balance on which God established creation. This does not cover measures states take to limit births in pursuit of a dignified life for their peoples ... for the ruler's action is bound to the public interest."

The fatwa also says:

> لم يوجب الشرع على كل من تزوَّج أن ينجب أولادًا

Translation: "The Law did not oblige everyone who marries to have children." It reasons by analogy from coitus interruptus (azl).

Source: https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/14993/. Status: verified (primary, official site).

An earlier Dar al-Ifta fatwa, no. 2422 of 7 February 2004 (Ali Gomaa), holds that a couple's agreement not to conceive is permissible. A further agreement to abort if a pregnancy occurs is not permissible. Source: https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/13077/. Verified (primary).

**Salafi: Ibn Baz, binbaz.org.sa fatwa 6751 ("Nur ala al-Darb", undated)**

> لا يجوز لهما ما دامت المرأة قادرة فليس لهما ذلك؛ لأن الشريعة تريد من الناس العناية بالأولاد وتكثير الأمة ... ولأن في ذلك أيضاً تكثير الأمة حتى تكون الأقوى ضد أعدائها، فليس للرجل أن يدع الإنجاب خوفاً من تبعة المئونة ومشقة النفقة، أو من أجل التلذذ بالمرأة ونحو ذلك

Translation: "It is not permitted for them as long as the woman is able [to bear]. They have no right to it, because the Law wants people to care for children and to multiply the community ... and because multiplying the community makes it strongest against its enemies. A man may not give up having children for fear of the burden of provision and the hardship of maintenance, or for the sake of pleasure with the woman and the like."

He allows exceptions: harm to the woman, for example when she can deliver only by an operation that harms her, and temporary spacing of a year or two when the children have come close together.

Source: https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/6751/. Status: verified (primary, official site).

**Ibn Baz on the Council of Senior Scholars decision (binbaz.org.sa audio 1099, undated)**

> وصدر في القرار المنع من تعاطي المرأة ما يمنع الحبل من حبوب أو لولب أو غير ذلك ... ولكن إذا دعت الحاجة إلى ذلك أو الضرورة إلى ذلك بطريقة خاصة فلا بأس، ويسمى تنظيمًا ... أما التحديد فممنوع، لا يجوز لهما أن يحددا ولدين أو ثلاثة أو أربعة، لا يجوز هذا ولا للدولة أيضًا

Translation: "The decision prohibited a woman's use of what prevents pregnancy, pills, an IUD or otherwise ... but if need or necessity calls for it in a particular way there is no harm, and that is called 'regulation' (tanzim) ... As for 'limitation' (tahdid), it is forbidden. They may not fix [the number at] two, three or four children; that is not permitted, nor is it permitted to a state either."

Source: https://binbaz.org.sa/audios/1099/. Status: Ibn Baz's statement is verified (primary). The decision itself (Council of Senior Scholars Decision no. 42, dated 13/4/1396 AH, about April 1976) is **reported, not verified**: its number and date appear only in secondary pages, and its text was not found.

IslamQA no. 166680 (26 April 2011) says that a couple's agreement never to have children "is disputed among scholars":

> محل خلاف بين العلماء

It holds that such a condition in a marriage contract is void while the contract stands, because:

> المنع من الإنجاب بالكلية مخالف للشرع الذي أوصى بالتكاثر والتناسل

Translation: "Preventing childbearing altogether opposes the Law, which enjoined multiplying and procreation." Source: https://islamqa.info/ar/answers/166680. Verified (primary, site text).

**Agreement and difference on childbearing**

- Both traditions encourage childbearing on the same hadith.
- Dar al-Ifta (2019) permits a couple's lifelong childlessness by mutual consent, forbids it only at the level of the whole community, and allows the state to limit births.
- Ibn Baz forbids a couple's permanent childlessness while the wife can bear, forbids any state from limiting births, and gives the strength of the community against its enemies as one reason.

## 2. Tiers and loyalty (Principle 4)

### 2.1 The ordered-giving hadith (Sahih Muslim 997)

> ابْدَأْ بِنَفْسِكَ فَتَصَدَّقْ عَلَيْهَا فَإِنْ فَضَلَ شَىْءٌ فَلأَهْلِكَ فَإِنْ فَضَلَ عَنْ أَهْلِكَ شَىْءٌ فَلِذِي قَرَابَتِكَ فَإِنْ فَضَلَ عَنْ ذِي قَرَابَتِكَ شَىْءٌ فَهَكَذَا وَهَكَذَا ‏.‏ يَقُولُ فَبَيْنَ يَدَيْكَ وَعَنْ يَمِينِكَ وَعَنْ شِمَالِكَ

Translation: "Begin with yourself and give it in charity to yourself. If anything is left over, it is for your family. If anything is left over after your family, it is for your relatives. If anything is left over after your relatives, then this way and this way, meaning in front of you, to your right and to your left."

- **Context**: Jabir reports that a man of Banu Udhra had freed his only slave, effective at the man's own death. The Prophet sold the slave for 800 dirhams, handed the money to the owner, and said these words.
- **Source**: Sahih Muslim, Kitab al-Zakat, no. 997 (997a) in the Abd al-Baqi numbering. hadith-api lists it as sequence 2313, "arabicnumber 997.01", book 12, hadith 50.
- **Status**: verified (digital text).

### 2.2 The order of obligatory maintenance (nafaqa) in a standard manual

The manual used is al-Nawawi (d. 676), Minhaj al-Talibin, the standard Shafi'i text, ed. Awad Qasim Ahmad Awad (Dar al-Fikr, 2005).

> فصل يلزمه نفقة الوالد وإن علا والولد وإن سفل وإن اختلف دينهما بشرط يسار المنفق بفاضل عن قوته وقوت عياله في يومه ... ومن له أصل وفرع ففي الأصح على الفرع وإن بعد أو محتاجون يقدم زوجته ثم الأقرب وقيل: الوارث وقيل: الولي.

Translation: "Section. He must maintain his parent, however far up, and his child, however far down, **even if their religion differs**, on condition that the provider has means: a surplus beyond his own food and his household's food for his day. ... If a needy person has both an ascendant and a descendant, the most correct view is that the descendant pays, even if more distant. If [one provider faces several] needy dependants, he puts his wife first and then the nearest relative. Some say the heir comes first; some say the guardian."

- **Source**: pp. 265–266. Read in the Shamela export at https://archive.org/details/6195Htm.
- **Status**: verified (primary, digital text of the named edition).
- **What it shows**:
  - The provider's own food and his household's come first; only a surplus is owed to others.
  - Among dependants, the wife comes before blood relatives.
  - Then come the nearest relatives.
  - The duty to parents and children binds across religion.
- **Related**: the Minhaj's order for paying the end-of-Ramadan alms (zakat al-fitr) when means are short is the self, then the wife, then the young child, then the father, then the mother, then the adult child (p. 70).

### 2.3 Loyalty and disavowal (al-wala' wa'l-bara'): the Qur'anic texts

All four verses were verified three ways.

> قد كانت لكم أسوة حسنة في إبراهيم والذين معه إذ قالوا لقومهم إنا برآء منكم ومما تعبدون من دون الله كفرنا بكم وبدا بيننا وبينكم العداوة والبغضاء أبدا حتى تؤمنوا بالله وحده إلا قول إبراهيم لأبيه لأستغفرن لك وما أملك لك من الله من شيء ۖ ربنا عليك توكلنا وإليك أنبنا وإليك المصير

60:4. Translation: "There has been for you an excellent example in Abraham and those with him, when they said to their people: We are disassociated from you and from what you worship besides God. We have rejected you, and enmity and hatred have appeared between us and you forever until you believe in God alone. Except Abraham's saying to his father: I will ask forgiveness for you, though I have no power to do anything for you against God. Our Lord, upon You we rely, to You we turn, and to You is the destination."

> يا أيها الذين آمنوا لا تتخذوا اليهود والنصارى أولياء ۘ بعضهم أولياء بعض ۚ ومن يتولهم منكم فإنه منهم ۗ إن الله لا يهدي القوم الظالمين

5:51. Translation: "O you who believe, do not take the Jews and the Christians as allies (awliya'). They are allies of one another. Whoever of you takes them as allies is one of them. God does not guide the wrongdoing people."

> لا تجد قوما يؤمنون بالله واليوم الآخر يوادون من حاد الله ورسوله ولو كانوا آباءهم أو أبناءهم أو إخوانهم أو عشيرتهم

58:22 (opening). Translation: "You will not find a people who believe in God and the Last Day showing affection to those who oppose God and His Messenger, even if they were their fathers, their sons, their brothers or their clan."

> لا يتخذ المؤمنون الكافرين أولياء من دون المؤمنين ۖ ومن يفعل ذلك فليس من الله في شيء إلا أن تتقوا منهم تقاة ۗ ويحذركم الله نفسه ۗ وإلى الله المصير

3:28. Translation: "Let not the believers take the unbelievers as allies instead of the believers. Whoever does that has nothing to do with God, unless you are guarding yourselves against them as a precaution. God warns you of Himself, and to God is the destination."

Two companion verses are cited by both traditions; both were verified:

- 60:8:

  > لا ينهاكم الله عن الذين لم يقاتلوكم في الدين ولم يخرجوكم من دياركم أن تبروهم وتقسطوا إليهم ۚ إن الله يحب المقسطين

  Translation: "God does not forbid you, regarding those who did not fight you over religion and did not expel you from your homes, from being kind to them and acting justly toward them. God loves those who act justly."
- 60:9:

  > إنما ينهاكم الله عن الذين قاتلوكم في الدين وأخرجوكم من دياركم وظاهروا على إخراجكم أن تولوهم ۚ ومن يتولهم فأولئك هم الظالمون

  Translation: "God forbids you only from taking as allies those who fought you over religion, expelled you from your homes and backed your expulsion. Whoever takes them as allies, those are the wrongdoers."

### 2.4 Salafi teaching

**Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, Nawaqid al-Islam (the ten nullifiers), as published by Ibn Baz**

> الثامن: مظاهرة المشركين ومعاونتهم على المسلمين، والدليل قوله تعالى: وَمَنْ يَتَوَلَّهُمْ مِنْكُمْ فَإِنَّهُ مِنْهُمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَهْدِي الْقَوْمََ الظَّالِمِينَ [المائدة:51]

Translation: "Eighth: backing the polytheists and helping them against the Muslims. The proof is His saying: 'Whoever of you takes them as allies is one of them; God does not guide the wrongdoing people' [5:51]."

> الثالث: من لم يكفر المشركين أو شك في كفرهم أو صحح مذهبهم كفر.

Translation: "Third: whoever does not declare the polytheists unbelievers, or doubts their unbelief, or holds their doctrine correct, has disbelieved."

> ولا فرق في جميع هذه النواقض بين الهازل والجاد والخائف، إلا المكره

Translation: "In all these nullifiers there is no difference between the one joking, the one in earnest, and the one afraid, except the one under compulsion."

- **Source**: https://binbaz.org.sa/articles/20. First published in Majallat al-Buhuth al-Islamiyya (Riyadh), no. 7, Rajab–Shawwal 1403 AH (1983); reprinted in Majmu' Fatawa wa Maqalat Ibn Baz 1/130, as the page itself states.
- **Status**: verified (primary, official Ibn Baz site).

**Ibn Baz on the eighth nullifier (binbaz.org.sa fatwa 11318, undated)**

> نعم، مظاهرة المشركين على المسلمين ردة عن الإسلام؛ لقوله -جل وعلا-: وَمَنْ يَتَوَلَّهُمْ مِنْكُمْ فَإِنَّهُ مِنْهُمْ ... كونه يظاهر الكفار من النصارى، أو اليهود، أو الشيوعيين، أو غيرهم ضد المسلمين، وينصرهم على المسلمين، هذا تول للكفار، وردة عن الإسلام

Translation: "Yes, backing the polytheists against the Muslims is apostasy from Islam, because of His saying, 'whoever of you takes them as allies is one of them.' His backing the unbelievers, Christians, Jews, communists or others, against the Muslims, and helping them against the Muslims, is alliance with the unbelievers and apostasy from Islam."

Source: https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/11318/. Verified (primary).

**Ibn Baz on gifts and kinship ties with non-Muslims (binbaz.org.sa fatwa 23930, undated lesson)**

> إن كانوا ليسوا حربًا لنا فلا بأس، ذاك الحربي لا يُعطى شيئًا لا قليل ولا كثير ... لأن هذا مظاهرة على المسلمين فيكون ردة عن الإسلام

Translation: "If they are not at war with us, there is no harm [in giving to them and maintaining ties]. The one at war (harbi) is given nothing, little or much ... because that is backing [them] against the Muslims, and so it is apostasy from Islam."

He bases the permission on 60:8 and on the Prophet's instruction to Asma' to keep ties with her polytheist mother. Source: https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/23930/. Verified (primary).

**Salih al-Fawzan, al-Wala' wa'l-Bara' fi al-Islam**

The copy used is a digital edition annotated by Adil Nassar (Dar al-Kitab wa'l-Sunna, Gaza; PDF dated 2013), at https://archive.org/details/ozkorallh_20170707_2251. The PDF's text layer garbles the lam-alif ligature. The quotes below restore it, and were checked against page images for pp. 2 and 13.

> فمن أصول العقيدة الإسلامية أنه يجب على كل مسلم يدين بهذه العقيدة أن يوالي أهلها ويعادي أعداءها فيحب أهل التوحيد والإخلاص ويواليهم، ويبغض أهل الإشراك ويعاديهم، وذلك من ملة إبراهيم والذين معه

Translation (p. 2): "Among the foundations of the Islamic creed is that every Muslim who holds this creed must be loyal to its people and hostile to its enemies: he loves the people of monotheism and sincerity and is loyal to them, and he hates the people of polytheism and is hostile to them. That is the way of Abraham and those with him."

On the same page he cites 60:4, 5:51, 60:1, 9:23 and 58:22, and writes:

> بل لقد حرّم على المؤمن موالاة الكفار ولو كانوا من أقرب الناس إليه نسباً

Translation: "Indeed He forbade the believer loyalty to unbelievers even if they are the nearest of people to him by lineage."

He also writes that he heard someone on an Arabic radio station say of Christians:

> إنهم إخواننا ويا لها من كلمة خطيرة

Translation: "They are our brothers. What a dangerous word that is!"

His list of the outward signs of loyalty to unbelievers (pp. 4–7) includes:

1. Imitating them in dress and speech.
2. Living in their lands without emigrating, when emigration is possible.
3. Travelling to their lands for pleasure.
4. Helping and backing them against the Muslims:

   > إعانتهم ومناصرتهم على المسلمين ... وهذا من نواقض الإسلام وأسباب الردة

   Translation: "Helping and backing them against the Muslims ... this is among the nullifiers of Islam and causes of apostasy."
5. Appointing them to posts that give access to Muslim secrets. Under this item he names as an instance of the problem the importing of non-Muslim workers and drivers into the lands of the two holy sanctuaries.
6. Dating by their calendar.
7. Taking part in, helping with, or congratulating them on their festivals.

His limiting note (p. 13, read on the page image):

> فالصلةُ والمكافأةُ الدنيويةُ شيءٌ، والمودةُ شيءٌ آخرُ ... وكذلك تحريمُ موالاةِ الكفارِ لا تَعني تحريمَ التعاملِ معهمْ بالتجارةِ المباحةِ واستيرادِ البضائعِ والمصنوعاتِ النافعةِ والاستفادةِ منْ خبراتِهم ومخترعاتِهم

Translation: "Maintaining ties and worldly reciprocity are one thing; affection is another. ... Likewise, forbidding loyalty to unbelievers does not mean forbidding dealing with them in permitted trade, importing useful goods and manufactures, or benefiting from their expertise and inventions."

- **Status**: verified (primary text in a reprint edition). The original first edition was not seen. A library catalogue entry found by search lists a Riyadh edition (Dar al-Watan, 1991); that was not checked.

**Ibn Uthaymeen on congratulating Christians at their festivals**

> تهنئة الكفار بعيد الكريسمس أو غيره من أعيادهم الدينية حرام بالاتفاق ... لأن فيها إقراراً لما هم عليه من شعائر الكفر، ورضى به لهم ... وإذا هنؤنا بأعيادهم فإننا لا نجيبهم على ذلك ... ومن فعل شيئاً من ذلك فهو آثم سواء فعله مجاملة أو توددا أو حياء

Translation: "Congratulating unbelievers on Christmas or their other religious festivals is forbidden by agreement ... because it implies approving the rites of unbelief they follow and being content with them for them ... If they congratulate us on our festivals, we do not reply to them in kind ... Whoever does any of this is sinful, whether he does it out of courtesy, affection or embarrassment."

- He bases the "agreement" on a quotation from Ibn al-Qayyim's Ahkam Ahl al-Dhimma.
- **Source**: IslamQA no. 947, posted 21 Dhu al-Hijja 1428 (31 December 2007), at https://islamqa.info/ar/answers/947. IslamQA cites Majmu' Fatawa wa Rasa'il Ibn Uthaymeen 3/369.
- **Status**: verified (reproduction). It was not checked against the printed Majmu' or binothaimeen.net. The Ibn al-Qayyim passage it relies on was not checked in Ahkam Ahl al-Dhimma: the Wikisource page for that book is empty.

### 2.5 Mainstream treatment of the same verses

**Al-Qurtubi (d. 671), al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an, on 5:51 (Maliki, classical mainstream)**

> {وَمَنْ يَتَوَلَّهُمْ مِنْكُمْ} أي يعضدهم على المسلمين {فَإِنَّهُ مِنْهُمْ} بين تعالى أن حكمه كحكمهم ... ثم هذا الحكم باق إلى يوم القيامة في قطع المولاة

Translation: "'Whoever of you takes them as allies' means whoever supports them against the Muslims. 'Is one of them': God made clear that his legal status is theirs. ... This ruling remains until the Day of Resurrection, in cutting off alliance."

Source: https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/الجامع_لأحكام_القرآن/سورة_المائدة/صفحة_واحدة. Status: verified (primary). This reading, "supports them against the Muslims", is the same act that Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab names as the eighth nullifier. The classical mainstream exegesis did not lack the doctrine.

**Dar al-Ifta, Egypt, fatwa no. 5997, 20 December 2015 (Shawqi Allam): against total separation**

> القول بمقاطعة المسلم لغير المسلمين مقاطعةً تامة عامةً قولٌ فاسد؛ فيجب على المسلم موالاة المسلمين ونصرتهم، وعدم معاداة غير المسلمين

Translation: "The view that a Muslim must cut off non-Muslims totally and generally is a corrupt view. The Muslim must be loyal to Muslims and support them, and not be hostile to non-Muslims."

On 3:28 it argues:

> فظاهر هذه الآية أن الموالاة المؤثرة المذكورة على سبيل الذم هي ما كانت لأجل أنهم كفار

Translation: "The apparent sense of this verse is that the blameworthy loyalty it mentions is loyalty *because* they are unbelievers."

It then quotes al-Tabari, citing Jami' al-Bayan 6/313 (Risala edition):

> لا تتخذوا، أيها المؤمنون، الكفارَ ظهرًا وأنصارًا توالونهم على دينهم، وتظاهرونهم على المسلمين

Translation: "Do not, believers, take the unbelievers as backers and helpers, siding with them on their religion and backing them against the Muslims." That al-Tabari quote was not checked independently.

It adds:

> المسلم -كشأن كل إنسان- له مجموعة من دوائر الانتماء غير المتعارضة فيما بينها؛ منها: الانتماء للأسرة، ومنها: الانتماء للوطن، ومنها: الانتماء للقومية التي يتبعها، ومنها الانتماء الأكبر للإنسانية

Translation: "The Muslim, like every human being, has a set of circles of belonging that do not conflict with one another: belonging to the family, belonging to the homeland, belonging to his nation, and the largest belonging, to humanity."

Source: https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/16588/. Status: verified (primary, official site).

**Al-Azhar Observatory (Marsad al-Azhar), "حكم موالاة غير المسلمين" ("The ruling on loyalty to non-Muslims"), 31 March 2016**

> عقيدة الولاء والبراء في الإسلام إنما هي مرتبطة بحال السِّلم والحرب ... فمن سالم المسلمين وتآلف معهم، ولم يعادهم ولم يخرجهم من ديارهم ولم يفتنهم عن دينهم، فله البر والقسط والولاء، حتى ولو كان غير مسلم

Translation: "The doctrine of loyalty and disavowal in Islam is tied to the state of peace and war ... Whoever is at peace with the Muslims and on good terms with them, and has not shown them hostility, expelled them from their homes or tried to turn them from their religion, is owed kindness, justice and loyalty, even if he is not a Muslim."

It goes on to say that when non-Muslims break covenants or mock Islam, "loyalty turns into disavowal." Source: https://www.azhar.eg/observer/replies/ArtMID/5814/ArticleID/75/. Status: verified (primary, Azhar site).

**Dar al-Ifta, Egypt, fatwa no. 3644, 24 August 2016 (Shawqi Allam), on congratulating non-Muslims**

> لا مانع شرعًا من ذلك ... ونص الفقهاء على جواز تهنئة غير المسلمين بأعيادهم مع مراعاة أن لا يقترن ذلك بطقوس دينية أو ممارسات تخالف ثوابت الإسلام

Translation: "There is no legal objection to it ... The jurists stated that congratulating non-Muslims on their festivals is permitted, provided it is not joined to religious rites or practices that contradict the fixed tenets of Islam."

On the older prohibitions it says:

> فهي أقوالٌ لها سياقاتها التاريخية وظروفها الاجتماعية المتعلقة بها ... ولا يخفى أن تغير الواقع يقتضي تغير الفتوى ... وهذا المعنى لم يعد قائمًا في عصر المواطَنة الحاضر

Translation: "They are views with their own historical contexts and social circumstances ... A change in reality requires a change in the fatwa ... and that reason no longer holds in the present age of citizenship."

Source: https://www.dar-alifta.org/ar/fatwa/details/13237/. Status: verified (primary).

**Agreement and difference on loyalty**

- **Both**:
  - Both affirm loyalty to Muslims and disavowal of those who fight Muslims or expel them (60:9).
  - Both treat backing non-Muslims in war against Muslims as gravely wrong. Al-Qurtubi and Ibn Baz both read 5:51 as "supporting them against the Muslims."
  - Both permit trade and kinship ties with non-Muslims who are not at war (60:8).
- **Differences**:
  1. **Scope of the prohibition.** The Salafi texts extend the disavowal to inner love, imitation, festivals, calendars, travel and residence in non-Muslim lands, and employment. Ibn Baz and Ibn Uthaymeen make backing non-Muslims against Muslims apostasy. Dar al-Ifta and the Azhar Observatory confine the prohibition to loyalty given *because of* unbelief or to those at war.
  2. **Greetings.** Ibn Uthaymeen forbids congratulating non-Muslims on their festivals. Dar al-Ifta permits it.
  3. **Admitting change.** Dar al-Ifta openly says that the classical prohibitions were tied to their circumstances and that the fatwa changes. The Salafi texts present the classical rulings as still binding.

## 3. Non-Muslims today (Principle 5)

### 3.1(a) Al-Azhar Declaration on Citizenship and Coexistence, 1 March 2017

Issued at the conference "Freedom and Citizenship: Diversity and Integration", held in Cairo from 28 February to 1 March 2017 by al-Azhar and the Muslim Council of Elders.

The page on the Muslim Council of Elders site titled "إعلان الأزهر الشريف للمواطنة والعيش المشترك" (https://www.muslim-elders.com/Home/DocumentDetails/6) actually holds Ahmad al-Tayyeb's opening speech, not the numbered declaration; its English PDF is the same speech. No copy on azhar.eg was found.

> أولًا: إنَّ مصطلح "المواطنة" هو مصطلحٌ أصيل في الإسلام، وقد شعَّت أنوارُه الأولى من دستور المدينة ... ويُبادر الإعلان إلى تأكيدِ أنَّ المواطنة ليست حلًّا مستوردًا، وإنَّما هو استدعاءٌ لأوَّل ممارسةٍ إسلاميَّةٍ لنظام الحُكمِ ... وأنَّ غير المسلمين لهم ما للمسلمين، وعليهم ما على المسلمين ... فإنَّ الأزهر ومجلس حكماء المسلمين ومسيحيِّي الشرق يَلتَقُون اليومَ من جديدٍ على الإيمان بالمساواة بين المسلمين والمسيحيين في الأوطان والحقوق والواجبات، باعتبارهم "أُمَّة واحدة؛ للمسلمين دِينُهم، وللمسيحيين دِينُهم"

Translation: "First: the term 'citizenship' is an authentic term in Islam. Its first light shone from the Constitution of Medina ... The declaration affirms that citizenship is not an imported solution but a recalling of the first Islamic practice of government ... and that non-Muslims have what Muslims have and owe what Muslims owe ... Al-Azhar, the Muslim Council of Elders and the Christians of the East meet again today on belief in equality between Muslims and Christians in homeland, rights and duties, as 'one community (umma): to the Muslims their religion and to the Christians their religion.'"

> ثانيًا: ... كما أنَّ تجاهُلَ مفهوم المواطنة ومُقتَضياته يُشجِّع على الحديث عن الأقليّات وحُقوقِها. ومن هذا المنطلق يتمنَّى الإعلان على المثقفين والمفكِّرين أن يتنبهوا لخطورة المضيِّ في استخدام مصطلح "الأقليات"، الذي يحمل في طياته معاني التمييز والانفصال بداعي التأكيد على الحقوق

Translation: "Second: ... ignoring the concept of citizenship and what it requires encourages talk of minorities and their rights. From this standpoint the declaration urges intellectuals and thinkers to be alert to the danger of continuing to use the term 'minorities', which carries within it meanings of discrimination and separation under the pretext of affirming rights."

Item 4 makes protecting citizens' "lives, freedoms, property and the other rights of their citizenship" the first duty of the national state.

**Use of "dhimma"**: neither the declaration text nor al-Tayyeb's speech uses the words dhimma, dhimmi or jizya. A search of both texts found none.

Al-Tayyeb's speech of the same date (Muslim Council of Elders page) says:

> والأزهر حين يدعو إلى نشر مفهوم «المواطنة» بديلًا عن مصطلح «الأقلية والأقليات»، فإنما يدعو إلى مبدأ دستوري طبقه نبي الإسلام -ﷺ-على أول مجتمع مسلم في التاريخ، وهو دولة المدينة

Translation: "When al-Azhar calls for spreading the concept of 'citizenship' in place of the term 'minority and minorities', it is calling for a constitutional principle that the Prophet of Islam applied to the first Muslim society in history, the state of Medina."

Status: verified (reproduction by Egyptian state media for the declaration; primary for the speech, on the co-issuing body's site).

### 3.1(b) Document on Human Fraternity, Abu Dhabi, 4 February 2019

Signed by Pope Francis and the Grand Imam of al-Azhar, Ahmad al-Tayyeb. Vatican site, Arabic and English:

- https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/travels/2019/outside/documents/papa-francesco_20190204_documento-fratellanza-umana.html

> أنَّ مفهومَ المواطنةِ يقومُ على المُساواةِ في الواجباتِ والحُقوقِ التي يَنعَمُ في ظِلالِها الجميعُ بالعدلِ؛ لذا يَجِبُ العملُ على ترسيخِ مفهومِ المواطنةِ الكاملةِ في مُجتَمَعاتِنا، والتخلِّي عن الاستخدام الإقصائيِّ لمصطلح «الأقليَّاتِ» الذي يَحمِلُ في طيَّاتِه الإحساسَ بالعُزْلَةِ والدُّونيَّة

Official English: "The concept of citizenship is based on the equality of rights and duties, under which all enjoy justice. It is therefore crucial to establish in our societies the concept of full citizenship and reject the discriminatory use of the term minorities which engenders feelings of isolation and inferiority."

> أنَّ الحريَّةَ حَقٌّ لكُلِّ إنسانٍ: اعتقادًا وفكرًا وتعبيرًا ومُمارَسةً ... وتجريمِ إكراهِ الناسِ على دِينٍ بعَيْنِه

Official English: "Freedom is a right of every person: each individual enjoys the freedom of belief, thought, expression and action ... Therefore, the fact that people are forced to adhere to a certain religion or culture must be rejected." The Arabic uses "تجريم", which means "criminalizing" the coercion.

> أنَّ حمايةَ دُورِ العبادةِ، من مَعابِدَ وكَنائِسَ ومَساجِدَ، واجبٌ تَكفُلُه كُلُّ الأديانِ

Official English: "The protection of places of worship – synagogues, churches and mosques – is a duty guaranteed by religions."

### 3.1(c) Saudi and Salafi fatwas on churches and on non-Muslims in the Arabian Peninsula

**Ibn Baz, interview with al-Islah magazine (Dubai), no. 70, Rabi' al-Awwal 1404 (December 1983), pp. 14–15; Majmu' Fatawa wa Maqalat Ibn Baz 3/282**

> والواجب على الحكام أن ينفذوا هذه الوصية ... فعلى الحكام في السعودية وفي الخليج وفي جميع أجزاء الجزيرة، عليهم جميعًا أن يجتهدوا كثيرًا في إخراج النصارى والبوذيين والوثنيين والهندوس وغيرهم من الكفرة وألا يستقدموا إلا المسلمين ... س- وفي حالة اقتضاء الضرورة، هل يصح أن تبنى لهم دور العبادة؟ ج- لا يجوز أن يبنى في الجزيرة معابد للكفرة، لا النصارى ولا غيرهم، وما بني فيها يجب أن يهدم مع القدرة، وعلى ولي الأمر أن يهدمها ويزيلها ... حتى لا يبقى فيها إلا المساجد والمسلمون.

Translation: "The rulers must carry out this testament ... The rulers in Saudi Arabia, the Gulf and all parts of the Peninsula must all strive hard to expel the Christians, Buddhists, idolaters, Hindus and other unbelievers, and to bring in only Muslims ... Question: if necessity requires it, may houses of worship be built for them? Answer: it is not permitted to build temples of unbelievers in the Peninsula, whether Christian or other. Whatever has been built there must be demolished when it is possible. The ruler must demolish and remove them ... so that nothing remains in it but mosques and Muslims."

He allows non-Muslims to be employed only in a legal necessity:

> عند الضرورة الشرعية ... التي يقدرها ولاة الأمر

Translation: "In a legal necessity ... as the rulers judge it." Source: https://binbaz.org.sa/discussions/28/. Status: verified (primary, official site).

IslamQA no. 104806 also quotes Ibn Baz (Majmu' 6/454), who defines the Peninsula as Saudi Arabia, Yemen and the Gulf states. It quotes Ibn Uthaymeen (Majmu' 3/41) as permitting non-Muslims to be brought in when needed, on condition that they are not given permanent residence. Verified (reproduction): https://islamqa.info/ar/answers/104806.

**Ibn Baz on churches in Muslim lands generally (binbaz.org.sa fatwa 25417, Fatawa al-Durus, undated)**

> لا. يُمنعون، إلا إذا فتحت على كنائس وأقرّهم ولي الأمر عليها، يُقَرُّون على كنائسهم التي بأيديهم. س: لا ينشؤون ابتداء؟ ج: لا ينشؤون ابتداء.

Translation: "No, they are prevented, unless [the land] was conquered with churches in it and the ruler let them keep them; then they are left with the churches in their hands. Question: they do not build new ones? Answer: they do not build new ones."

Source: https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/25417/. Verified (primary).

**Permanent Committee for Research and Fatwa (al-Lajna al-Da'ima)**

Signed by Abd al-Aziz Al al-Shaykh (chair), Abd Allah al-Ghudayyan, Bakr Abu Zayd and Salih al-Fawzan. It answers requests registered with the Council of Senior Scholars' secretariat on 5/1/1421 and 2/3/1421 AH (April and June 2000).

> كل دين غير دين الإسلام فهو كفر وضلال، وكل مكان يعدّ للعبادة على غير دين الإسلام فهو بيت كفر وضلال ... ولهذا أجمع العلماء على تحريم بناء المعابد الكفرية مثل: الكنائس في بلاد المسلمين ... وأجمعوا على وجوب هدم الكنائس وغيرها من المعابد الكفرية إذا أُحدثت في الإسلام ... فجزيرة العرب: حرمُ الإسلام وقاعدته التي لا يجوز السماح أو الإذن لكافر باختراقها، ولا التجنس بجنسيتها، ولا التملك فيها، فضلاً عن إقامة كنيسة فيها

Translation: "Every religion other than Islam is unbelief and error, and every place prepared for worship on a religion other than Islam is a house of unbelief and error ... Hence the scholars agreed by consensus that building temples of unbelief, such as churches, in Muslim lands is forbidden ... and agreed that churches and other temples of unbelief must be demolished if they were built after Islam [arrived] ... The Arabian Peninsula is the sanctuary of Islam and its base. It is not permitted to allow or license an unbeliever to penetrate it, to take its nationality, or to own property in it, let alone to set up a church in it."

- **Source**: reproduced at https://midad.com/article/201779 (a third-party site, posted 8 November 2007).
- **Status**: verified (reproduction) only. It was not checked on alifta.gov.sa, which is a JavaScript application that could not be read here, nor in the printed collection. The fatwa number often given for it, 21413, and the date 1/4/1421, are **not verified**.

**The underlying hadiths (all verified in digital texts)**

- Bukhari 3053, with the same order in Bukhari 3168 and Muslim 1637:

  > أَخْرِجُوا الْمُشْرِكِينَ مِنْ جَزِيرَةِ الْعَرَبِ

  Translation: "Expel the polytheists from the Arabian Peninsula."
- Muslim 1767:

  > لأُخْرِجَنَّ الْيَهُودَ وَالنَّصَارَى مِنْ جَزِيرَةِ الْعَرَبِ حَتَّى لاَ أَدَعَ إِلاَّ مُسْلِمًا

  Translation: "I will expel the Jews and Christians from the Arabian Peninsula until I leave none but Muslims."
- Muwatta, Kitab al-Jami', no. 2629 in Wikisource's numbering:

  > لاَ يَجْتَمِعُ دِينَانِ فِي جَزِيرَةِ الْعَرَبِ

  Translation: "Two religions shall not coexist in the Arabian Peninsula." It comes from Ibn Shihab al-Zuhri directly from the Prophet, with no Companion named. Malik adds that Umar verified the saying and expelled the Jews of Khaybar.
- On the other side of the ledger, Bukhari 3166:

  > مَنْ قَتَلَ مُعَاهَدًا لَمْ يَرَحْ رَائِحَةَ الْجَنَّةِ

  Translation: "Whoever kills a person under treaty will not smell the scent of Paradise."

**The classical mainstream rule for comparison (al-Nawawi, Minhaj, Shafi'i)**

> ويمنع كل كافر من استيطان الحجاز وهو مكة والمدينة واليمامة وقراها

Translation: "Every unbeliever is prevented from settling in the Hijaz, which is Mecca, Medina, al-Yamama and their villages." (p. 312)

> ونمنعهم إحداث كنيسة في بلد أحدثناه أو أسلم أهله عليه

Translation: "We prevent them from building a new church in a town we founded or whose people converted to Islam." (p. 314)

Status: verified (primary).

**Agreement and difference on churches and the Peninsula**

- **Churches in Muslim lands**: the classical Shafi'i rule (no new churches in towns founded by Muslims) and Ibn Baz's rule ("no new building; existing churches stay only if conquest found them and the ruler let them remain") are close to each other.
- **The Peninsula**: al-Nawawi limits the residence ban to the Hijaz. Ibn Baz and the Permanent Committee apply it to the whole Peninsula (Saudi Arabia, Yemen, the Gulf). The Committee adds bans on nationality and on owning property. That is a widening of the classical Shafi'i rule.
- **Today**: al-Azhar (2017), the Human Fraternity document (2019) and NU (2019) set the classical categories aside in favour of equal citizenship, and make protecting churches a religious duty.

### 3.1(d) Retaliation and blood money for non-Muslims: the legal-school split

**The hadith (Bukhari 111, and Bukhari 3047, 6903 and 6915), from Ali's written sheet (sahifa)**

> قُلْتُ فَمَا فِي هَذِهِ الصَّحِيفَةِ قَالَ الْعَقْلُ، وَفَكَاكُ الأَسِيرِ، وَلاَ يُقْتَلُ مُسْلِمٌ بِكَافِرٍ

Translation: "I said: What is on this sheet? He said: [the rules of] blood money, the ransoming of captives, and that a Muslim is not killed for an unbeliever." Verified in hadith-api (Bukhari 111, Kitab al-Ilm).

**Hanafi: al-Marghinani (d. 593), al-Hidaya, ed. Talal Yusuf (Dar Ihya' al-Turath)**

> قال: "والمسلم بالذمي" خلافا للشافعي له قوله عليه الصلاة والسلام "لا يقتل مؤمن بكافر" ... ولنا ما روي "أن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قتل مسلما بذمي" ولأن المساواة في العصمة ثابتة نظرا إلى التكليف والدار والمبيح كفر المحارب دون المسالم ... والمراد بما روى الحربي لسياقه "ولا ذو عهد في عهده"

Translation: "[The author of the base text] said: 'and the Muslim [is killed] for the dhimmi.' This is against al-Shafi'i, whose proof is the Prophet's saying 'a believer is not killed for an unbeliever' ... Our proof is the report 'that the Prophet killed a Muslim for a dhimmi', and that equality in protected status holds, given legal responsibility and [residence in the same] territory. What makes blood lawful is the unbelief of the one at war, not of the one at peace ... What is meant in the hadith they cite is the unbeliever at war, as its context shows: 'nor one under covenant during his covenant.'" (vol. 4 p. 444)

> قال: "ودية المسلم والذمي سواء" ... ولنا قوله عليه الصلاة والسلام: "دية كل ذي عهد في عهده ألف دينار" وكذلك قضى أبو بكر وعمر رضي الله عنهما

Translation: "He said: 'The blood money of the Muslim and of the dhimmi is the same' ... Our proof is the Prophet's saying 'The blood money of everyone under covenant during his covenant is a thousand dinars', and Abu Bakr and Umar judged so." (vol. 4 p. 461)

- **Source**: read in the Shamela export at https://archive.org/details/2977Htm.
- **Status**: verified (primary, digital text of the named edition). The qisas passage sits between two page markers both reading "(4/444)", so its page is given as 4:444.
- **The earlier Hanafi voice**: Muhammad b. al-Hasan al-Shaybani, as quoted in al-Shafi'i's Kitab al-Umm (Kitab al-Radd ala Muhammad b. al-Hasan, "باب دية أهل الذمة", the chapter on the blood money of dhimmis), at https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/كتاب_الأم/كتاب_الرد_على_محمد_بن_الحسن/باب_دية_أهل_الذمة:

  > قال أبو حنيفة رضي الله عنه ودية اليهودي والنصراني والمجوسي مثل دية الحر المسلم وعلى من قتله من المسلمين القود

  Translation: "Abu Hanifa said: the blood money of a Jew, a Christian or a Zoroastrian is the same as that of a free Muslim, and the Muslim who kills one of them is subject to retaliation."

  It argues from Qur'an 4:92. For a victim from a people bound to Muslims by treaty, that verse prescribes the same words it uses for a believer:

  > دية مسلمة إلى أهله

  Translation: "Blood money handed to his family." Verified (primary).

**Shafi'i: al-Nawawi, Minhaj al-Talibin**

> ومكافأة فلا يقتل مسلم بذمي ويقتل ذمي به وبذمي وإن اختلفت ملتهما

Translation: "[A condition is] parity: so a Muslim is not killed for a dhimmi, while a dhimmi is killed for [a Muslim] and for [another] dhimmi, even if their religions differ." (p. 271)

> والمرأة والخنثى كنصف رجل نفسا وجرحا ويهودي ونصراني ثلث مسلم ومجوسي ثلثا عشر مسلم

Translation: "A woman and an intersex person count as half a man, for a life and for a wound. A Jew or a Christian is one third of a Muslim, and a Zoroastrian is two-fifteenths (two-thirds of a tenth) of a Muslim." (p. 279)

Al-Shafi'i himself (al-Umm, "دية المعاهد", the chapter on the blood money of a person under treaty) bases the one-third figure on judgments by Umar and Uthman, and takes "the least that has been said." Verified (primary).

**Maliki: Malik, al-Muwatta (Yahya's recension), Kitab al-Uqul, chapter on the blood money of dhimmis (Wikisource nos. 2544–2545)**

> أَنَّ عُمَرَ بْنَ عَبْدِ الْعَزِيزِ قَضَى : أَنَّ دِيَةَ الْيَهُودِيِّ أَوِ النَّصْرَانِيِّ إِذَا قُتِلَ أَحَدُهُمَا، مِثْلُ نِصْفِ دِيَةِ الْحُرِّ الْمُسْلِمِ. قَالَ مَالِكٌ : الأَمْرُ عِنْدَنَا : أَنْهُ لاَ يُقْتَلَ مُسْلِمٌ بِكَافِرٍ، إِلاَّ أَنْ يَقْتُلَهُ مُسْلِمٌ قَتْلَ غِيْلَةٍ، فَيُقْتَلُ بِهِ.

Translation: "Umar b. Abd al-Aziz judged that the blood money of a Jew or a Christian, if either is killed, is half the blood money of a free Muslim. Malik said: The practice among us is that a Muslim is not killed for an unbeliever, unless a Muslim kills him by treacherous ambush (ghila), in which case he is killed for him."

Source: https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/موطأ_الإمام_مالك/كتاب_العقول. Verified (primary).

**Hanbali**

Al-Tirmidhi (d. 279), Jami', no. 1413, at https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/سنن_الترمذي/كتاب_الديات_(2):

> وهو قول سفيان الثوري ومالك بن أنس والشافعي وأحمد وإسحاق قالوا لا يقتل مؤمن بكافر ... وقال عمر بن عبد العزيز دية اليهودي والنصراني نصف دية المسلم وبهذا يقول أحمد بن حنبل

Translation: "That is the view of Sufyan al-Thawri, Malik, al-Shafi'i, Ahmad and Ishaq: a believer is not killed for an unbeliever ... Umar b. Abd al-Aziz said: the blood money of a Jew or a Christian is half that of a Muslim; and that is what Ahmad b. Hanbal holds."

Verified (primary). Note that al-Tirmidhi in the same passage credits Malik with the one-third figure. That conflicts with the Muwatta's half and with Ibn Rushd's account below; the Muwatta is Malik's own text.

IslamQA no. 243345 (1 May 2016) quotes Ahmad's Masa'il, as transmitted by al-Kawsaj, 7/3498:

> عن مسلم قتل معاهداً، قال: يُدرأ عنه القود، وتضاعف عليه الدية، وإن قتله خطأ فعليه دية المعاهد، وهو نصف دية المسلم

Translation: "[Asked] about a Muslim who killed a person under treaty, he said: retaliation is averted from him and the blood money is doubled against him. If he killed him by mistake, he owes the blood money of a person under treaty, which is half that of a Muslim."

IslamQA's own ruling:

> فلا يقتل المسلم بالكافر، سواء كان ذميا أو معاهدا أو مستأمنا

Translation: "A Muslim is not killed for an unbeliever, whether a dhimmi, a person under treaty, or one granted safe conduct." It adds:

> ولا يعني هذا إهدار دم الذمي

Translation: "This does not mean the dhimmi's blood counts for nothing." Source: https://islamqa.info/ar/answers/243345. Status: verified (primary for IslamQA's answer; the Masa'il quotation was not checked).

Ibn Baz (binbaz.org.sa fatwa 6413, a Bulugh al-Maram lesson) says that the hadith on a non-Muslim's blood money being half:

> جاء بحديث جيد لا بأس به

Translation: "comes in a good hadith with nothing wrong with it." Verified (primary). Al-Mughni of Ibn Qudama could not be checked here: those chapters are missing on Wikisource.

**Ibn Rushd (d. 595), Bidayat al-Mujtahid (a Maliki comparative survey), Kitab al-Jinayat**

> وأما قتل المؤمن بالكافر الذمي، فاختلف العلماء في ذلك على ثلاثة أقوال: فقال قوم: لا يقتل مؤمن بكافر، وممن قال به الشافعي والثوري وأحمد وداود وجماعة. وقال قوم: يقتل به، وممن قال بذلك أبو حنيفة وأصحابه وابن أبي ليلى. وقال مالك والليث: لا يقتل به إلا أن يقتله غيلة ... فسبب الخلاف تعارض الآثار والقياس.

Translation: "As for killing a believer for a dhimmi unbeliever, scholars hold three views. Some say a believer is not killed for an unbeliever: al-Shafi'i, al-Thawri, Ahmad, Dawud and others. Some say he is killed for him: Abu Hanifa and his school and Ibn Abi Layla. Malik and al-Layth say he is not killed for him unless he kills him by ambush ... The cause of the disagreement is the conflict between the reports and analogy."

He records the Hanafi analogy: since a Muslim's hand is cut off for stealing a dhimmi's property, the dhimmi's blood is as inviolable as his property. On blood money, three views: half (Malik, Umar b. Abd al-Aziz), one third (al-Shafi'i), equal (Abu Hanifa, al-Thawri). Source: https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/بداية_المجتهد_-_كتاب_الجنايات. Verified (primary).

**Summary table (classical rules for a dhimmi Jew or Christian victim)**

| School | Retaliation if a Muslim deliberately kills a dhimmi | Blood money of a Jewish or Christian dhimmi | Primary text read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hanafi | Yes | Equal to a Muslim's | al-Hidaya 4:444, 4:461; al-Shaybani in al-Umm |
| Maliki | No, except ambush killing (ghila) | Half | Muwatta 2544–2545; Bidaya |
| Shafi'i | No | One third (a Zoroastrian: two-fifteenths) | Minhaj pp. 271, 279; al-Umm |
| Hanbali | No | Half (doubled for deliberate killing, per Ahmad) | al-Tirmidhi 1413; Ahmad's Masa'il as quoted by IslamQA |

**Agreement and difference on retaliation and blood money**

- The modern Salafi position (IslamQA 2016, Ibn Baz) is the classical Hanbali one, which three of the four mainstream schools share in substance. On this question the Salafi position is not an innovation.
- The Hanafi school alone gave equal blood. The book's Chapter 9 already notes that the same school did not admit a dhimmi's testimony against a Muslim. So the most symmetric school on blood was asymmetric on evidence.
- The modern mainstream declarations (al-Azhar 2017, Human Fraternity 2019) state equality of rights and duties in general terms. No modern al-Azhar or Dar al-Ifta ruling that specifically revises the qisas or diya rules was located in this research. That is a gap, not a finding.

## 4. Human nature and particularity (Principle 2), briefly

**The fitra hadith (Bukhari 1385, Kitab al-Jana'iz)**

> كُلُّ مَوْلُودٍ يُولَدُ عَلَى الْفِطْرَةِ، فَأَبَوَاهُ يُهَوِّدَانِهِ أَوْ يُنَصِّرَانِهِ أَوْ يُمَجِّسَانِهِ، كَمَثَلِ الْبَهِيمَةِ تُنْتَجُ الْبَهِيمَةَ، هَلْ تَرَى فِيهَا جَدْعَاءَ

Translation: "Every child is born on the fitra (innate disposition); then his parents make him a Jew, a Christian or a Zoroastrian, just as an animal gives birth to a whole animal: do you see any mutilated one among them?"

Verified in hadith-api. Bukhari 1358 and 1359 carry a variant:

> مَا مِنْ مَوْلُودٍ إِلاَّ يُولَدُ عَلَى الْفِطْرَةِ

Translation: "There is no child but is born on the fitra." Abu Hurayra then recites 30:30 (verified three ways):

> فطرت الله التي فطر الناس عليها

Translation: "The fitra of God, on which He created people."

**Ibn Taymiyya's reading (Dar' Ta'arud al-Aql wa'l-Naql), the Salafi reference**

> فليس المراد به أنه حين خرج من بطن أمه يعلم هذا الدين ويريده ... ولكن فطرته مقتضية موجبة لدين الإسلام لمعرفته ومحبته

Translation: "It does not mean that when he leaves his mother's womb he knows and wants this religion ... but that his innate disposition calls for and requires the religion of Islam, knowing and loving it."

Source: https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/درء_تعارض_العقل_والنقل/41. Verified (primary).

**Qur'an 49:13 (verified three ways)**

> يا أيها الناس إنا خلقناكم من ذكر وأنثى وجعلناكم شعوبا وقبائل لتعارفوا ۚ إن أكرمكم عند الله أتقاكم ۚ إن الله عليم خبير

Translation: "O people, We created you from a male and a female and made you peoples and tribes so that you may know one another. The most honoured of you with God is the most God-fearing of you. God is knowing, aware."

- **Mainstream use**: al-Tayyeb quotes it in his 2017 citizenship speech as the basis for mutual acquaintance (ta'aruf) and cooperation between religions.
- **Salafi use**: Ibn Taymiyya quotes it in Iqtida' al-Sirat al-Mustaqim, together with the hadith "there is no merit of an Arab over a non-Arab except by piety." In the same work he also holds:

  > فإن الذي عليه أهل السنة والجماعة اعتقاد أن جنس العرب أفضل من جنس العجم

  Translation: "The position of Ahl al-Sunna wa'l-Jama'a is the belief that the Arabs as a genus are superior to the non-Arabs as a genus." Source: https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/اقتضاء_الصراط_المستقيم/7. Verified (primary).

**Agreement and difference**: both traditions use the hadith and the verse. Both affirm that people are born with one shared disposition, are formed by their parents, and are divided into peoples and tribes. Ibn Taymiyya ranks peoples by descent as well as by piety; the al-Azhar speech uses the verse only for mutual acquaintance.

## Bearing on Principles 2 to 5

**Principle 2 (humans are self-interested, limited, and particular)**

- Both traditions teach that every person is born with one shared disposition (fitra) and is then formed by his parents into a particular religion (Bukhari 1385).
- Both read Qur'an 49:13 as saying that God made people into distinct peoples and tribes. Particularity is treated as created, not as a fault.
- Self-interest is assumed in the law. Muslim 997 tells the giver to begin with himself. The Minhaj makes maintenance of relatives due only from what is left after the provider's own food and his household's.
- Ibn Taymiyya adds a ranking of peoples by descent, with Arabs as a genus above non-Arabs. Al-Azhar's 2017 use of 49:13 does not rank peoples.

**Principle 3 (goodness must include human life continuing, and that life kept and built)**

- Both traditions put life and offspring among the five things the Law exists to preserve.
- Al-Shatibi states the continuation point directly: without offspring "there would ordinarily be no continuation."
- Both encourage childbearing on the same hadith (Abu Dawud 2050).
- They differ on what follows:
  - Ibn Baz forbids a couple's permanent childlessness while the wife can bear, and forbids any state from limiting births.
  - Dar al-Ifta (2019) permits a couple's mutual choice of lifelong childlessness, forbids only an umma-wide halt, and lets the state limit births for welfare.
- Both traditions also rank religion above life. Al-Amidi states this as a rule. Ibn Taymiyya bases fighting on "fitna is greater than killing." Al-Ghazali's first example of preserving religion is a death penalty.
- So in both traditions, life is a necessary good but not the highest one. Taking life is lawful where religion is judged to require it: in war against those who obstruct religion and, classically, for apostasy.
- Today's mainstream (Dar al-Ifta 2016) narrows the apostasy penalty to treason-like hostility. The Salafi texts do not narrow it.

**Principle 4 (what you keep and build is self-anchored, tiered, and outward; enemies are real)**

- The tier order in the sources matches the book's order:
  - Muslim 997: self, household, relatives, then outward in every direction.
  - Minhaj: own sustenance, wife, then nearest kin, with the duty to parents and children binding across religion.
  - Dar al-Ifta 5997: family, homeland, nation, humanity, as non-conflicting circles.
  - Ibn Taymiyya lists kinship ties and neighbours' rights among the goods the five-list omits.
- Loyalty and disavowal (al-wala' wa'l-bara') add a religious tier that crosses the kin tiers:
  - 58:22 denies affection to opponents of God "even if they were their fathers, their sons, their brothers or their clan."
  - Al-Fawzan writes that loyalty to unbelievers is forbidden "even if they are the nearest of people by lineage."
  - Kindness and dealings stay permitted with kin and with non-Muslims who are not at war (60:8; Asma' and her mother; al-Fawzan p. 13; Ibn Baz 23930).
- Both traditions define the enemy the same way: those who fight Muslims over religion, expel them, or back their expulsion (60:9). Both treat backing non-Muslims in war against Muslims as gravely wrong. Salafis classify it as apostasy (the eighth nullifier). Al-Qurtubi reads 5:51 the same way.
- The Salafi texts go further. They extend the disavowal to inner affection, festivals, imitation, residence and employment. Dar al-Ifta and the Azhar Observatory confine it to loyalty given because of unbelief, or to those at war.
- On truthfulness: 3:28 contains an exception allowing outward precaution toward unbelievers when one fears them ("unless you are guarding yourselves against them"). How far that exception reaches was not researched here.

**Principle 5 (justice is scoped procedural symmetry)**

- **Classical law**:
  - Three of the four mainstream schools, and the Salafis who follow the Hanbali school, applied different outcomes to the same act within the same polity depending on the victim's religion.
  - A Muslim who killed a dhimmi was not killed in return; the Maliki school excepts ambush killing.
  - The dhimmi's blood money was one third (Shafi'i) or one half (Maliki, Hanbali) of a Muslim's.
  - The Hanafi school applied the same procedure, retaliation and equal blood money, to Muslim and dhimmi residents. It did so on the ground that both share protected status and the same territory, which is a scope argument.
  - The same Hanafi school refused a dhimmi's testimony against a Muslim (Chapter 9). Symmetry in one procedure did not carry over to the others.
- **Today, the two traditions apply different scopes**:
  - Al-Azhar's 2017 declaration, the 2019 Human Fraternity document and NU's 2019 decision make the national state the scope. Inside it they state equal rights and duties ("non-Muslims have what Muslims have and owe what Muslims owe"). They reject the term "minorities" and drop the dhimma categories without naming them. The Human Fraternity document makes protecting churches a duty.
  - The Saudi Salafi establishment makes the Arabian Peninsula a scope where non-Muslims may not build any house of worship, may not take nationality or own property (Permanent Committee), and may stay only by necessity (Ibn Baz). That is wider than al-Nawawi's Hijaz-only residence ban. Outside the Peninsula, Ibn Baz allows only churches that existed at conquest.
- **One open point**: no modern al-Azhar or Dar al-Ifta ruling was found that explicitly revises the classical retaliation and blood-money rules. The mainstream equality statements are general, and their effect on those specific rules remains unverified.
