## 1. Reason against revealed text when they appear to conflict

### 1a. Fakhr al-Din al-Razi (d. 606/1210), *Asas al-Taqdis*, chapter 32: the "universal rule" (al-qanun al-kulli)

Edition: *Asas al-Taqdis*, ed. Ahmad Hijazi al-Saqqa, Cairo: Maktabat al-Kulliyyat al-Azhariyya, 1406/1986 ("min turath al-Razi" no. 9). Source file: archive.org item `20260717_20260717_2035`. Chapter title, p. 220: "الفصل الثاني والثلاثون: في أن البراهين العقلية إذا صارت معارضة بالظواهر النقلية فكيف يكون الحال فيها؟" ("Chapter 32: when rational demonstrations are opposed by the apparent meanings of transmitted texts, how does the matter stand?"). **[V-scan]** pp. 220–221.

> اعلم: أن الدلائل القطعية العقلية إذا قامت على ثبوت شيء، ثم وجدنا أدلة نقلية يشعر ظاهرها بخلاف ذلك، فهناك لا يخلو الحال من أحد أمور أربعة: إما أن يصدق مقتضى العقل والنقل — فيلزم تصديق النقيضين وهو محال — وإما أن نبطلهما — فيلزم تكذيب النقيضين وهو محال — (وإما أن تكذب الظواهر النقلية، وتصدق الظواهر العقلية) وإما أن تصدق الظواهر النقلية وتكذب الظواهر العقلية — وذلك باطل — لأنه لا يمكننا أن نعرف صحة الظواهر النقلية، إلا إذا عرفنا بالدلائل العقلية: إثبات الصانع، وصفاته، وكيفية دلالة المعجزة على صدق الرسول ﷺ وظهور المعجزات على (يد) محمد ﷺ. ولو صار القدح في الدلائل العقلية القطعية، صار العقل متهما، غير مقبول القول. ولو كان كذلك لخرج عن أن يكون مقبول القول في هذه الأصول. وإذا لم تثبت هذه الأصول، خرجت الدلائل النقلية عن كونها مفيدة. فثبت: أن القدح في العقل لتصحيح النقل، يفضي إلى القدح في العقل والنقل معا. وإنه باطل. ولما بطلت الأقسام الأربعة لم يبق إلا أن (يقطع بمقتضى) الدلائل العقلية القاطعة: بأن هذه الدلائل النقلية إما أن يقال إنها غير صحيحة، أو (يقال: إنها صحيحة) إلا أن المراد منها غير ظواهرها. ثم إن جوزنا التأويل: اشتغلنا على سبيل التبرع بذكر تلك التأويلات على التفصيل. وإن لم نجوز التأويل فوضنا العلم بها إلى الله تعالى. فهذا هو القانون الكلي المرجوع إليه في جميع المتشابهات (وبالله التوفيق).

Know that when definitive rational proofs establish something, and we then find transmitted proofs whose apparent meaning suggests the opposite, the case must be one of four. (1) Both what reason requires and what the text requires are affirmed as true. That means affirming two contradictories, which is impossible. (2) Both are rejected. That means denying two contradictories, which is impossible. (3) The apparent meanings of the texts are judged false and the rational ones true. (4) The apparent meanings of the texts are judged true and the rational ones false. This last is invalid, because we can know that the apparent meanings of texts are sound only if we first know by rational proofs that the Maker exists, what His attributes are, how a miracle shows that the Messenger is truthful, and that miracles appeared at the hand of Muhammad. If definitive rational proofs could be impugned, reason would be suspect and its word could not be accepted. Then it could not be accepted on these foundations either. If these foundations were not established, the transmitted proofs would cease to yield anything. So it is established that impugning reason in order to vindicate the text leads to impugning reason and text together, and that is invalid. Once the [other] divisions are void, nothing remains except to decide, as definitive rational proofs require, about these transmitted proofs. Either they are said to be unsound, or they are said to be sound but meant otherwise than their apparent sense. Then, if we permit interpretation (ta'wil), we may, as an extra service, give those interpretations in detail. If we do not permit it, we consign knowledge of their meaning to God. This is the universal rule to be applied to all the ambiguous texts (mutashabihat).

The words in parentheses are the editor's manuscript variants. The clause "or the apparent meanings of the texts are judged false and the rational ones true" is marked "زيادة ح" (an addition found in manuscript H). Without it the passage names only three of its "four" cases.

The context is theological. The *Asas* deals with texts describing God (hand, face, settling on the throne) that al-Razi says reason shows cannot be meant literally. The rule is not stated as a rule about physical measurement.

**The editor's footnote (p. 221, n. 7)**, verified **[V-scan]**:

> واعلم: أن القانون الكلي الذي يقول به الإمام فخر الدين — وهو القانون الصحيح — لم يأخذه عن الغزالي والجويني وغيرهما من علماء المدرسة الأشعرية فحسب، بل هذا القانون مفصل في كتاب «الفصل» لابن حزم الأندلسي، في الجزء الأول.

Know that the universal rule that Imam Fakhr al-Din holds, and it is the correct rule, was not taken only from al-Ghazali, al-Juwayni, and other scholars of the Ash'ari school. This rule is also set out in detail in Ibn Hazm's *al-Fisal*, part one.

The note goes on to say that Ibn Taymiyya's own rule ("if revelation and reason conflict, revelation must be given precedence") is correct only for the reasons behind legal rulings.

### 1b. The Azhar creed textbook: when apparent meanings are set aside

The book is the same Azhar series as in `ref/azhar_curriculum/README.md`. The files are in the repo.

* **First year, p. 85 [V-scan]**. The *Jawhara* verse on texts that suggest anthropomorphism:

> وَكُلُّ نَصٍّ أَوْهَمَ التَّشْبِيهَا *** أَوِّلْهُ أَوْ فَوِّضْ وَرُمْ تَنْزِيهَا

Every text that suggests likeness [of God to creatures]: interpret it, or consign its meaning [to God], and aim at declaring Him transcendent.

The same page defines *ta'wil* as "حمل اللفظ على خلاف ظاهره، مع بيان المعنى المراد" (taking the word otherwise than its apparent meaning, while stating the intended meaning). It defines *tafwid* as "صرف اللفظ عن ظاهره مع عدم التعرض لبيان المعنى المراد منه" (turning the word away from its apparent meaning without attempting to state the intended meaning).

* **Third year, p. 78 [V-scan]**. On the Prophet's Pool (*hawd*), against the Mu'tazili reinterpretation:

> وليس هناك أيضًا ما يدعو إلى صرف هذه النصوص عن ظاهرها ما دامت الحقيقة ممكنة، وهذا هو الحق، وهو رأي أهل السنة.

Nor is there anything that calls for turning these texts away from their apparent meaning, so long as the literal reality is possible. This is the truth, and it is the view of Ahl al-Sunna.

So the textbook's operating rule is to keep the apparent meaning unless it is impossible. Its examples of "impossible" are about God, not the physical world.

### 1c. Ibn Taymiyya (d. 728/1328), *Dar' Ta'arud al-'Aql wa'l-Naql*

Edition: ed. Muhammad Rashad Salim, vol. 1. The title page was checked on the scan. Source file: archive.org item `dar-taarud-al-aql-wa-al-naql`, file "ج 1". The printed page numbers match the PDF leaf numbers. The same text is on Arabic Wikisource, page "درء تعارض العقل والنقل/1", which has no page numbers. The Wikisource text has typos (see the note under 1/147).

**The opening quotes the rule, 1/4 [V-scan]:**

> «إذا تعارضت الأدلة السمعية والعقلية، أو السمع والعقل، أو النقل والعقل، أو الظواهر النقلية والقواطع العقلية، أو نحو ذلك من العبارات، فإما أن يجمع بينهما، وهو محال، لأنه جمع بين النقيضين؛ وإما أن يردا جميعا؛ وإما أن يقدم السمع، وهو محال، لأن العقل أصل النقل، فلو قدمناه عليه كان ذلك قدحا في العقل الذي هو أصل النقل، والقدح في أصل الشيء قدح فيه، فكان تقديم النقل قدحا في النقل والعقل جميعا، فوجب تقديم العقل، ثم النقل إما أن يتأول، وإما أن يفوض. وأما إذا تعارضا تعارض الضدين امتنع الجمع بينهما، ولم يمتنع ارتفاعهما». وهذا الكلام قد جعله الرازي وأتباعه قانونا كليا فيما يستدل به من كتب الله تعالى وكلام أنبيائه عليهم السلام وما لا يستدل به

"If scriptural and rational proofs conflict (or 'hearing and reason', 'transmission and reason', 'the apparent meanings of texts and the definitive conclusions of reason', or similar phrases), then either they are combined, which is impossible because it combines contradictories; or both are rejected; or scripture is given precedence. That is impossible, because reason is the foundation of transmission. If we gave it precedence over reason, that would impugn reason, which is the foundation of transmission, and impugning the foundation of a thing impugns the thing. So giving precedence to transmission would impugn transmission and reason together. Therefore reason must be given precedence. Then the transmitted text is either interpreted or its meaning is consigned [to God]. If they conflict as contraries, combining them is impossible, but rejecting both is not." Al-Razi and his followers made this statement a universal rule for deciding which parts of God's books and His prophets' words may be used as proof and which may not.

The editor's note 2 on the same page points to *Asas al-Taqdis*, p. 210, in another edition. In the next paragraph (read in the Wikisource text; the page in the scan was not checked), Ibn Taymiyya says that "Abu Hamid" (al-Ghazali) set out a rule of this kind before al-Razi, and that Abu Bakr ibn al-'Arabi set out another one following al-Juwayni and al-Baqillani.

**His general reply, 1/79 [V-scan]:**

> فأما القطعيان فلا يجوز تعارضهما: سواء كانا عقليين أو سمعيين، أو أحدهما عقليا والآخر سمعيا، وهذا متفق عليه بين العقلاء، لأن الدليل القطعي هو الذي يجب ثبوت مدلوله، ولا يمكن أن تكون دلالته باطلة. … وإن كان أحد الدليلين المتعارضين قطعيا دون الآخر فإنه يجب تقديمه باتفاق العقلاء، سواء كان هو السمعي أو العقلي، فإن الظن لا يرفع اليقين. وأما إن كانا جميعا ظنيين: فإنه يصار إلى طلب ترجيح أحدهما، فأيهما ترجح كان هو المقدم، سواء كان سمعيا أو عقليا.

Two definitive proofs cannot conflict, whether both are rational, both scriptural, or one of each. All rational people agree on this, because a definitive proof is one whose conclusion must hold and whose evidential force cannot be void. … If one of two conflicting proofs is definitive and the other is not, the definitive one must be given precedence by the agreement of rational people, whether it is the scriptural or the rational one, for probability does not remove certainty. If both are probable, one seeks to determine which is stronger, and the stronger is given precedence, whether it is scriptural or rational.

**The first and second "aspects," 1/86–87 [V-scan]:**

> أن قوله: «إذا تعارض النقل والعقل». إما أن يريد به القطعيين، فلا نسلم إمكان التعارض حينئذ. وإما أن يريد به الظنيين، فالمقدم هو الراجح مطلقا. وإما أن يريد به ما أحدهما قطعي، فالقطعي هو المقدم مطلقا، وإذا قدر أن العقلي هو القطعي كان تقديمه لكونه قطعيا، لا لكونه عقليا. فعلم أن تقديم العقلي مطلقا خطأ، كما أن جعل جهة الترجيح كونه عقليا خطأ.

His statement "if transmission and reason conflict" means one of three things. If it means two definitive proofs, we do not grant that they can conflict. If it means two probable proofs, the stronger always takes precedence. If it means a case where one of them is definitive, the definitive one always takes precedence. If the rational one is assumed to be the definitive one, it takes precedence because it is definitive, not because it is rational. So giving the rational proof precedence in every case is a mistake, and so is making "being rational" the ground of preference.

**The third aspect, 1/87 [V-scan]** (the margin heading reads "نفي قاعدة أن العقل أصل النقل", "denial of the rule that reason is the foundation of transmission"):

> وذلك لأن قوله: «إن العقل أصل للنقل» إما أن يريد به: أنه أصل في ثبوته في نفس الأمر. أو أصل في علمنا بصحته. والأول لا يقوله عاقل، فإن ما هو ثابت في نفس الأمر بالسمع أو بغيره هو ثابت، سواء علمنا بالعقل أو بغير العقل ثبوته، أو لم نعلم ثبوته لا بعقل ولا بغيره، إذ عدم العلم ليس علما بالعدم

His statement "reason is the foundation of transmission" means either that reason is the foundation of the text's truth in reality, or that it is the foundation of our knowing that the text is sound. No rational person holds the first. Whatever is true in reality, through revelation or otherwise, is true whether we know it by reason, by something else, or not at all, because not knowing something is not knowing that it does not exist.

**His summary claim, 1/147 [V-scan]:**

> ولكن ما عُلم بصريح العقل لا يُتصور أن يعارضه الشرع ألبتة، بل المنقول الصحيح لا يعارضه معقول صريح قط. وقد تأملت ذلك في عامة ما تنازع الناس فيه، فوجدت ما خالف النصوص الصحيحة الصريحة شبهات فاسدة يُعلم بالعقل بطلانها، بل يُعلم بالعقل ثبوت نقيضها الموافق للشرع. وهذا تأملته في مسائل الأصول الكبار كمسائل التوحيد والصفات، ومسائل القدر والنبوات والمعاد وغير ذلك، ووجدت ما يُعلم بصريح العقل لم يخالفه سمع قط، بل السمع الذي يقال إنه يخالفه: إما حديث موضوع، أو دلالة ضعيفة، فلا يصلح أن يكون دليلا لو تجرد عن معارضة العقل الصريح، فكيف إذا خالفه صريح المعقول؟ ونحن نعلم أن الرسل لا يخبرون بمحالات العقول بل بمحارات العقول، فلا يخبرون بما يعلم العقل انتفاءه، بل يخبرون بما يعجز العقل عن معرفته.

But what is known by clear reason cannot possibly be contradicted by revelation. Rather, an authentic transmitted text is never contradicted by a clear rational truth. I have examined this in most of the questions people dispute. I found that whatever contradicts the authentic, explicit texts is specious argument whose falsity reason itself shows; reason in fact shows that the opposite, which agrees with revelation, is true. I examined this in the great questions of doctrine: God's unity and attributes, predestination, prophecy, the resurrection, and others. I found that what is known by clear reason has never been contradicted by scripture. The scripture said to contradict it is either a fabricated hadith or a weak inference [from a text]. Such a thing would not count as proof even with no rational objection against it, still less when clear reason contradicts it. We know that the messengers do not report what reason knows to be impossible (*muhalat al-'uqul*). They report what bewilders reason (*mahârât al-'uqul*): they do not report what reason knows to be false, but what reason cannot find out by itself.

Note: Arabic Wikisource has "بمجالات العقول بل بمجاراة العقول" here. The Salim scan reads "بمحالات … بمحارات", and the editor's footnote defines المحارات as "ما حارت العقول في فهمه" (what minds are bewildered in understanding).

IslamQA 238544 (already established) cites a later volume of the same work (9/29).

### 1d. Agreement and difference on Question 1

* **Agreement (verified in both texts):** Two definitive proofs cannot conflict. When an apparent textual meaning conflicts with a definitive rational proof, the text is not taken in its apparent sense. For al-Razi it is reinterpreted, consigned to God, or (for some reports) judged unsound. For Ibn Taymiyya it is "a fabricated hadith or a weak inference" (1/147).
* **Difference (verified):**
  * Al-Razi gives reason priority as a class, because reason grounds our knowledge that scripture is true (p. 220).
  * Ibn Taymiyya gives priority to whichever proof is definitive, whether rational or scriptural (1/79, 1/86–87). He denies that reason is the ground of scripture's truth in reality (1/87). He holds that apparent conflicts between real definitive reason and authentic texts do not occur, because the "rational" side turns out to be specious (1/147).
  * In practice the disagreement is over which claims count as "definitive reason." Al-Razi counted arguments that God is not a body as definitive. Ibn Taymiyya did not.
* **Scope:** Both texts are about doctrines concerning God. Neither text discusses physical measurement. Applying either rule to astronomy or biology is an extension. Section 2 documents one Salafi scholar (Ibn Uthaymeen) making that extension explicitly.

## 2. Cosmology: Abd al-Aziz Ibn Baz (1912–1999) on the sun and the earth

### 2a. The publication history

* **The 1385 AH newspaper article.** Ibn Baz himself dates it in the preface to the book, p. 17 **[V-scan]**: the article "نشر … في الصحف المحلية عام ١٣٨٥ هـ" (was published in the local newspapers in 1385 AH). His reply to *al-Musawwar*, on binbaz.org.sa, specifies "في رمضان 1385 هـ" (Ramadan 1385, about December 1965 to January 1966) **[V-site]**. I did not locate the original newspaper issue.
* **The *al-Musawwar* controversy.** The Egyptian magazine *al-Musawwar*, no. 2166, 15 April 1966, p. 15 (column by Ahmad Baha' al-Din), reported that the vice-president of the Islamic University had published an article declaring permissible the blood of anyone who says the earth is spherical and orbits the sun. Ibn Baz's reply, "تكذيب ونقد لبعض ما نشرته مجلة (المصور)", is at https://binbaz.org.sa/articles/82, citing *Majmu' Fatawa wa Maqalat Ibn Baz* 3/156 **[V-site]**:

> لقد نشر المقال الذي أشار إليه الكاتب في جميع الصحف المحلية في رمضان 1385 هـ، واطلع عليه القراء في الداخل والخارج، وليس فيه ذكر كروية الأرض بنفي ولا إثبات، فضلًا عن إهدار دم من قال بها … وإنما أهدرت في المقال دم من قال إن الشمس ثابتة لا جارية بعد استتابته، وما ذلك إلا لأن إنكار جري الشمس تكذيب لله سبحانه، وتكذيب لكتابه العظيم، وتكذيب لرسوله الكريم، وقد علم بالضرورة من دين الإسلام وبالأدلة القطعية وبإجماع أهل العلم أن من كذب الله أو رسوله أو كتابه فهو كافر حلال الدم والمال، ويستتاب، فإن تاب وإلا قتل، وليس في هذا بحمد الله نزاع بين أهل العلم.

The article the writer refers to was published in all the local newspapers in Ramadan 1385 and was read at home and abroad. It does not mention the sphericity of the earth, either to deny or to affirm it, let alone declare permissible the blood of those who hold it. … What I declared permissible in the article was the blood of whoever says that the sun is stationary and does not move, after he has been asked to repent. That is only because denying the sun's motion is calling God, His Book, and His Messenger liars. It is known necessarily in Islam, by definitive proofs and by the consensus of scholars, that whoever calls God, His Messenger, or His Book a liar is an unbeliever whose blood and property are lawful. He is asked to repent; if he repents [he is spared], and if not he is killed. Praise God, there is no dispute about this among scholars.

* **The book.** *al-Adilla al-Naqliyya wa'l-Hissiyya 'ala Imkan al-Su'ud ila al-Kawakib wa 'ala Jarayan al-Shams wa'l-Qamar wa Sukun al-Ard* (*The Transmitted and Sensory Proofs That Ascent to the Planets Is Possible, That the Sun and Moon Move, and That the Earth Is Still*). Second printing, 1402/1982, Riyadh: Maktabat al-Riyad al-Haditha (title page and copyright page **[V-scan]**). Source file: archive.org item `adilla_aql_naql` (single pages). The same scan is in item `Hassouni_5` (two-up). The preface (pp. 17–18) says the book reprints his two newspaper articles "من غير زيادة، ولا نقص" (without addition or omission). The one exception is a quotation from al-Baghdadi that he added to the second article. Contents:
  * Moon and planets: pp. 5–14.
  * First article, "الشمس جارية والأرض ثابتة" ("The sun moves and the earth is still"): pp. 19–30. It ends with his later title, General President of the Departments of Scholarly Research, Ifta, Da'wa and Guidance.
  * Second article, a reply to Shaykh Muhammad Mahmud al-Sawwaf, who had objected: pp. 31–73.
  * Secondary sources mention a 1395 AH Islamic University of Medina printing **[Reported]**. I did not see it.

### 2b. What he said: the first article (pp. 22–24) [V-scan]

> … فمن زعم خلاف ذلك وقال إن الشمس ثابتة لا جارية فقد كذب الله، وكذب كتابه الكريم الذي لا يأتيه الباطل من بين يديه ولا من خلفه تنزيل من حكيم حميد … وكل من قال هذا القول فقد قال كفرًا وضلالًا لأنه تكذيب لله، وتكذيب للقرآن، وتكذيب للرسول ﷺ لأنه عليه الصلاة والسلام قد صرح في الأحاديث الصحيحة أن الشمس جارية، وأنها إذا غربت تذهب وتسجد بين يدي ربها تحت العرش كما ثبت ذلك في الصحيحين من حديث أبي ذر رضي الله عنه — وكل من كذب الله سبحانه أو كذب كتابه الكريم أو كذب رسوله الأمين عليه الصلاة والسلام فهو كافر ضال مضل يستتاب فإن تاب وإلا قتل كافرًا مرتدًا ويكون ماله فيئًا لبيت مال المسلمين كما نص على مثل هذا أهل العلم، والإيمان في باب (حكم المرتد) وكما أن هذا القول الباطل مخالف للنصوص فهو مخالف للمشاهد المحسوس، ومكابرة للمعقول والواقع، فلم يزل الناس مسلمهم، وكافرهم يشاهدون الشمس جارية طالعة وغاربة، ويشاهدون الأرض قارة ثابتة، ويشاهدون كل بلد وكل جبل في جهته لم يتغير من ذلك شيء، ولو كانت الأرض تدور كما يزعمون لكانت البلدان، والجبال، والأشجار، والأنهار، والبحار لا قرار لها، ولشاهد الناس البلدان المغربية في المشرق، والمشرقية في المغرب، ولتغيرت القبلة على الناس حتى لا يقر لها قرار، وبالجملة فهذا القول فاسد من وجوه كثيرة يطول تعدادها.

… Whoever claims otherwise and says that the sun is stationary and does not move has called God a liar, and called His noble Book a liar … Everyone who says this has spoken unbelief and error, because it calls God, the Qur'an, and the Messenger liars. The Messenger stated in authentic hadiths that the sun moves, and that when it sets it goes and prostrates before its Lord beneath the Throne, as is established in the two *Sahih* collections from the hadith of Abu Dharr. Everyone who calls God, His Book, or His Messenger a liar is an unbeliever, astray and leading astray. He is asked to repent. If he repents [he is spared]; if not, he is killed as an apostate unbeliever, and his property becomes spoil for the Muslim treasury, as the scholars state in the chapter on the apostate. Just as this false view contradicts the texts, it also contradicts what is seen and sensed, and it defies reason and reality. People, Muslim and unbeliever, have always seen the sun moving, rising and setting. They see the earth settled and fixed, and every town and mountain in its direction, with nothing about that changing. If the earth turned, as they claim, towns, mountains, trees, rivers, and seas would have no fixed place. People would see western towns in the east and eastern towns in the west, and the direction of prayer would keep changing. In short, this view is false in more ways than can be listed.

On the earth's rotation (pp. 23–24):

> وأما من قال إن الأرض تدور، والشمس جارية فقوله أسهل من قول من قال بثبوت الشمس، ولكنه في نفس الأمر خطأ ظاهر مخالف للآيات المتقدمات، وللمحسوس، والواقع، ووسيلة للقول بعدم جري الشمس فقد أوضح الله في الآيات المذكورات آنفًا أنه ألقى الجبال في الأرض لئلا تميد بهم، والميد هو الحركة، والإضطراب، والدوران، كما نص على ذلك علماء التفسير، وأئمة اللغة وفي تكفير قائله نظر لأن الأدلة الواردة في ثبوت الأرض وسكونها، وعدم دورانها ليست في الصراحة كالأدلة الواردة في جري الشمس، وعدم سكونها؛ ولأن القائلين بدوران الأرض قد أوردوا شبهات توجب التوقف عن كفر من قال بذلك، وأما إنكار جريان الشمس فلا ريب في كفر قائله للأدلة الصريحة القطعية في ذلك.

As for whoever says that the earth turns and the sun also moves, his view is less grave than that of someone who says the sun is stationary. In reality, though, it is a plain error. It contradicts the verses cited above, the senses, and reality, and it is a step toward saying the sun does not move. In those verses God made clear that He cast the mountains onto the earth "lest it sway with them," and swaying (*mayd*) means motion, agitation, and turning (*dawaran*), as the exegetes and the authorities on the language state. Whether its proponent is an unbeliever is open to question. The proofs that the earth is fixed, still, and not turning are not as explicit as the proofs that the sun moves and is not still. Also, those who hold that the earth turns have raised doubts that require one to withhold judgment of unbelief from whoever holds it. As for denying that the sun moves, there is no doubt that whoever does so is an unbeliever, because the proofs of it are explicit and definitive.

**On the astronomers' agreement, p. 12 [V-scan].** This passage is about the classical planetary spheres, as reported by Ibn Kathir:

> ولو كانت لديهم أدلة قطعية على ما ذكروا لم يختلفوا، ولو فرضنا أنهم اتفقوا على ما ذكر فاتفاقهم ليس بحجة لأنه غير معصوم، وإنما الإجماع المعصوم هو إجماع علماء الإسلام الذين قد توافرت فيهم شروط الاجتهاد

If they had definitive proofs for what they said, they would not disagree. And if we suppose they did agree on it, their agreement is not a proof, because it is not protected from error. The only consensus protected from error is the consensus of Islamic scholars who meet the conditions for independent legal reasoning.

### 2c. The second article: what he did and did not call unbelief [V-scan]

**p. 36:**

> ولم أكفر من قال بدوران الأرض، ولا من قال إن الشمس تجري حول نفسها، وإنما صرحت بتكفير من قال إن الشمس ثابتة لا جارية هذا هو الموجود في المقال السابق … فالذي يقول إن الشمس ثابتة لا جارية مكذب لله تكذيبًا صريحًا ومعترض عليه، ومكذب لما فطر الله عليه العباد، ولما يشاهده الناس بأبصارهم فقد اجتمع في هذا الأمر العظيم النقل، والفطرة، وشاهد العيان فكيف لا يكون مثل هذا كافرًا

I did not declare an unbeliever whoever says the earth turns, nor whoever says the sun turns about itself. I explicitly declared an unbeliever only whoever says the sun is stationary and does not move. That is what the earlier article contains. … Whoever says the sun is stationary and does not move calls God a liar explicitly and objects to Him. He also denies the nature God gave His servants and what people see with their own eyes. In this great matter, transmitted text, innate nature, and eyewitness observation come together, so how could such a person not be an unbeliever?

**His closing summary, pp. 72–73:**

> ١) … أما القول بأنها ثابتة لا جارية فهو كفر وضلال، وقول باطل مخالف للأدلة النقلية، والحسية لا يجوز للمسلم اعتقاده …
> ٢) إن القول بأن الشمس ثابتة وجارية حول نفسها في آن واحد كما يقوله الكثير من علماء الهيئة قول باطل مخالف للأدلة السمعية، والحسية … وهذه المسألة لم أتعرضها في مقالي السابق بنفي ولا إثبات ولم أكفر من قال ذلك … وأنه لا يجوز للمسلم أن يقول إن الشمس ثابتة بوجه من الوجوه لأن ذلك يصادم نصوص القرآن الكريم، ونصوص السنة، وشواهد العيان وما كفر صاحبه ببعيد، والله المستعان.
> ٣) أوضحت فيما تقدم أن القول بدوران الأرض اليومي، والسنوي كله باطل … وبينت أني لم أكفر في المقال السابق من قال بدوران الأرض بل قلت أن في كفره نظرًا … فهذه العبارة صريحة في توقفي عن تكفير من قال بدوران الأرض للسبب الذي أوضحته في المقال السابق.
> ٤) بينت فيما تقدم أن ما نقله بعض الكتاب من إجماع علماء الهيئة على دوران الأرض لا صحة له بل المسألة لا تزال بينهم محل نزاع واختلاف لا محل اتفاق …

1. … The view that the sun is stationary and does not move is unbelief and error. It is a false view contrary to the transmitted and sensory proofs, and no Muslim may believe it.
2. The view that the sun is fixed while turning about itself, as many astronomers say, is false and contrary to the scriptural and sensory proofs. … I did not address this question in my earlier article, either to deny or to affirm it, and I did not declare its proponent an unbeliever. … No Muslim may say that the sun is fixed in any sense, because that collides with the texts of the Qur'an and Sunna and with eyewitness evidence, and judging its proponent an unbeliever is not far-fetched.
3. I have shown that the daily and yearly rotation of the earth are both false. … I made clear that in the earlier article I did not declare an unbeliever whoever holds that the earth turns. I said that his unbelief is open to question. … This statement is explicit that I withheld the judgment of unbelief from whoever holds that the earth turns, for the reason given in the earlier article.
4. I have shown that the claim by some writers that astronomers agree on the earth's rotation is incorrect. The question is still disputed among them, not agreed.

**So, precisely:**

* Unbelief, with the apostasy penalty after a demand for repentance, was declared for one position: "the sun is stationary and does not move" (ثابتة لا جارية).
* For "the sun is fixed but rotates on its axis," he called the view false and said judging its holder an unbeliever is "not far-fetched." He did not declare it unbelief.
* For the earth's daily and yearly rotation, he called it false and contrary to texts, senses, and reality, and withheld judgment on unbelief.
* He did not deny the earth's sphericity (binbaz.org.sa articles/82).
* For his reasoning about observation, see "Bearing on Principle 1," point 3.

### 2d. The moon landing (same book, p. 13) [V-scan]

> وبذلك يعلم أنه لا مانع من أن يكون هناك فضاء بين الكواكب والسماء الدنيا يمكن أن تسير فيه المركبات الفضائية، ويمكن أن تنزل على سطح القمر أو غيره من الكواكب، ولا يجوز أن يقال بامتناع ذلك إلا بدليل شرعي صريح يجب المصير إليه، كما أنه لا يجوز أن يصدق من قال إنه وصل إلى سطح القمر أو غيره من الكواكب إلا بأدلة علمية تدل على صدقه، ولا شك أن الناس بالنسبة إلى معلوماتهم عن الفضاء، ورواد الفضاء يتفاوتون، فمن كان لديه معلومات قد اقتنع بها بواسطة المراصد أو غيرها دلته على صحة ما ادعاه رواد الفضاء الأمريكيون وغيرهم من وصولهم إلى سطح القمر فهو معذور في تصديقه، ومن لم تتوافر لديه المعلومات الدالة على ذلك فالواجب عليه التوقف، والتثبت حتى يثبت لديه ما يقتضي التصديق أو التكذيب

From this it is known that nothing prevents there being space between the planets and the lowest heaven in which spacecraft can travel and land on the surface of the moon or another planet. It may not be said that this is impossible except on the basis of an explicit proof from revelation that must be followed. Likewise, no one who says he reached the surface of the moon or another planet may be believed except on the basis of scientific evidence (أدلة علمية) showing that he is truthful. People differ in their information about space and astronauts. Whoever has information that convinced him, through observatories or otherwise, that the American astronauts and others did reach the moon is excused in believing it. Whoever lacks such information must suspend judgment and verify until something establishes that the claim should be believed or rejected.

In His words "when it rose, it inclined" and "when it set, it passed them by" there is proof that it is the sun that moves, and that rising and setting come about through its movement. This is contrary to what people say today, that what turns is the earth while the sun is fixed. We have something from God's speech. We are obliged to take it in its apparent sense and not to move from that sense except on clear proof. If it is established for us by decisive proof that the alternation of night and day is caused by the earth's rotation, then we must interpret the verses to the meaning that matches reality, and say: "when it rose, as it appears to the eye; when it set, as it appears to the eye." … But until it becomes clear to us by decisive proof that the sun is fixed and the earth turns, and that night and day alternate by its turning, we will never accept this. … We know with certainty that God knows His creation best, and we do not accept a guess or a probability. But if we were certain that the sun is fixed in its place, that the earth turns around it, and that this produces night and day, then interpreting the verses would be obligatory, so that the Qur'an does not contradict what is known with certainty.

### 2g. Mainstream Sunni positions on cosmology

* **Abd al-Qahir al-Baghdadi (Ash'ari, d. 429/1037), *al-Farq bayn al-Firaq*,** in its list of doctrines on which Ahl al-Sunna agree (Arabic Wikisource, "الفرق بين الفرق") **[V-text]**:

> وأجمعوا على وقوف الأرض وسكونها وإن حركتها إنما تكون بعارض يعرض لها من زلزلة ونحوها خلاف قول من زعم من الدهرية أن الأرض تهوي أبدا، ولو كانت كذلك لوجب ألا يلحق الحجر الذي نلقيه من أيدينا الأرض أبدا لأن الخفيف لا يلحق ما هو أثقل منه في انحداره.

They agreed that the earth stands and is still, and that its motion comes only from an accident that befalls it, such as an earthquake. This is against the materialists (Dahriyya) who claimed that the earth falls forever. If it did, a stone we drop from our hands would never reach the earth, because the lighter does not catch up with what is heavier than it in its descent.

The "consensus" is stated against a specific claim, that the earth falls endlessly, and the argument offered is a physical one. Ibn Baz cites this consensus (book, pp. 18 and 73).

## 3. Evolution

### 3a. Saudi Permanent Committee (al-Lajna al-Da'ima)

*Fatawa al-Lajna al-Da'ima lil-Buhuth al-'Ilmiyya wa'l-Ifta'*, first collection, vol. 1, pp. 34–35. This is the printing of Mu'assasat al-Amira al-Anud bint Abd al-Aziz, Riyadh. Source file: archive.org item `Fataawa_Al-Lajnah_Ad-Daayeemah_Lil-Buhooth_Al-Ilmiyyah_Wal_Iftaa`, "جلد 01". Heading: "نظرية التطور والارتقاء" ("The theory of evolution and progress"). Fatwa no. 5167, question 5 **[V-scan]**:

> س٥: هناك من يقول: إن الإنسان منذ زمن بعيد كان قردًا وتطور، فهل هذا صحيح، وهل من دليل؟
> ج٥: هذا القول ليس بصحيح، والدليل على ذلك أن الله بين في القرآن أطوار خلق آدم فقال تعالى: ﴿إِنَّ مَثَلَ عِيسَىٰ عِندَ اللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ آدَمَ ۖ خَلَقَهُ مِن تُرَابٍ﴾، ثم إن هذا التراب بُلَّ حتى صار طينًا لازبًا يعلق بالأيدي … ثم صار حمأ مسنونًا … ثم لما يبس صار صلصالا كالفخار … وصوره الله على الصورة التي أرادها ونفخ فيه من روحه … هذه هي الأطوار التي مرت على خلق آدم من جهة القرآن …

Q5: Some say that long ago man was an ape and evolved. Is this correct, and is there proof?
A5: This statement is not correct. The proof is that God explained in the Qur'an the stages of Adam's creation: "the likeness of Jesus with God is as the likeness of Adam; He created him from dust" (3:59). Then this dust was moistened until it became sticky clay that clings to the hands, then altered black mud, then, when it dried, ringing clay like pottery. God formed him in the shape He willed and breathed into him of His spirit. … These are the stages that Adam's creation passed through according to the Qur'an.

The fatwa then describes the embryonic stages of Adam's descendants (23:12–14) and Eve's creation from Adam (4:1).

## 4. Maturidi position on causation

### 4a. Primary text: the Nasafi creed

*Al-'Aqa'id al-Nasafiyya* is by Najm al-Din Umar al-Nasafi (d. 537/1142), a Hanafi-Maturidi. The text below is as embedded in Sa'd al-Din al-Taftazani's *Sharh al-'Aqa'id al-Nasafiyya*, ed. Anas Muhammad Adnan al-Sharfawi, Damascus: Dar al-Taqwa, 1st ed. 1441/2020, p. 232. Source file: archive.org item `saad-din-taftazani`. The section is headed "الكلام في إبطال القول بالتولد" ("On refuting the doctrine of generated effects"). The editor takes this title from Abu al-Mu'in al-Nasafi's *Tabsirat al-Adilla* 2/680. In the edition, the creed's own words are in parentheses and the rest is al-Taftazani's commentary **[V-scan]**:

> (وَمَا يُوجَدُ مِنَ الْأَلَمِ فِي الْمَضْرُوبِ عَقِيبَ ضَرْبِ إِنْسَانٍ، وَالْإِنْكِسَارِ فِي الزُّجَاجِ عَقِيبَ كَسْرِ إِنْسَانٍ) قيَّد بذلك؛ ليصلحَ محلًا للخلاف في أنَّه: هل للعبدِ صنعٌ فيه أم لا، (وَمَا أَشْبَهَهُ) كالموتِ عقيبَ القتلِ.. (كُلُّ ذَلِكَ مَخْلُوقٌ لِلَّهِ تَعَالَى) لما مرَّ مِن أنَّ الخالقَ هو اللهُ تعالى وحدَه، وأنَّ كلَّ المُمكناتِ مُستندةٌ إليه بلا واسطةٍ. والمعتزلةُ لمَّا أسندوا بعضَ الأفعالِ إلى غيرِ اللهِ تعالى.. قالوا: إنْ كانَ الفعلُ صادرًا عن الفاعلِ لا بتوسُّطِ فعلٍ آخرَ فهو بطريقِ المباشرةِ، وإلا فبطريقِ التوليدِ … فالألمُ متولِّدٌ مِنَ الضربِ، والانكسارُ مِنَ الكَسْرِ، وليسا مخلوقينِ للهِ تعالى. وعندَنا: الكلُّ بخَلْقِ اللهِ تعالى (لَا صُنْعَ لِلْعَبْدِ فِي تَخْلِيقِهِ)

(The pain that occurs in someone struck, following a person's striking, and the breaking that occurs in glass, following a person's breaking it,) [al-Taftazani:] he added this qualification so that it would be a proper case for the dispute over whether the servant has any making in it; (and what resembles it,) such as death following killing, (all of that is created by God,) because, as said before, the Creator is God alone and every contingent thing depends on Him without intermediary. The Mu'tazila, because they ascribed some acts to beings other than God, said: if an act proceeds from the agent without the mediation of another act, it is by direct action; otherwise it is by generation. … So the pain is generated by the striking and the breaking by the breaking act, and neither is created by God. With us, all of it is by God's creation; (the servant has no making in creating it.)

### 4b. Comparison with the Azhar textbook

* **Same in substance (verified in both texts):** The Nasafi creed and the Azhar Ash'ari textbook both say that the effect following a created cause (pain after a blow, breakage after a strike, death after killing) is created by God directly, "without intermediary." The created cause does not produce it.
* **Not in the Nasafi text:** the Azhar textbook's four-way grading (by nature = unbelief; by a created power = innovation; necessary connection = ignorance; customary connection = the saved believer). The verified Nasafi passage says nothing about the status of those who hold the other views.
* **Secondary scholarship, not read by me:**
  * Nazif Muhtaroglu, "Al-Māturīdī's View of Causality," in *Occasionalism Revisited* (Lockwood Press, 2025), is reported by a Turkish article (DergiPark, file 3365345) to read al-Maturidi himself as an occasionalist **[Reported]**.
  * An Istanbul University item, "Natural Laws and Scientific Realism in al-Māturīdī," is described as saying al-Maturidi's position on natural laws is disputed **[Reported]**.
  * I did not verify Ramon Harvey's *Transcendent God, Rational World* (2021) on this point.
* **Conclusion for the author:** at the level of the standard school creed (al-Nasafi), the Maturidi position on secondary causes does not differ from the Ash'ari position in the Azhar textbook. Whether al-Maturidi's own *Kitab al-Tawhid* allows God-created natures is a live question in secondary scholarship. I did not resolve it from the primary text.

## 5. Other evidence: is the physical world consequential, and can observation correct a reading?

### 5a. Contagion (hadith "lā 'adwā")

* **Ibn Baz, "الجمع بين: «لا عدوى ولا طيرة» و «فر من المجذوم»",** https://binbaz.org.sa/fatwas/1553, citing *Majmu' Fatawa* 6/27 **[V-site]**:

> لا منافاة عند أهل العلم بين هذا وهذا … وذلك نفي لما يعتقده أهل الجاهلية من أن الأمراض كالجرب تعدي بطبعها، وأن من خالط المريض أصابه ما أصاب المريض، وهذا باطل، بل ذلك بقدر الله ومشيئته … لا يجوز أن يعتقد العدوى، ولكن يشرع له أن يتعاطى الأسباب الواقية من وقوع الشر، وذلك بالبعد عمن أصيب بمرض يخشى انتقاله منه إلى الصحيح بإذن الله كالجرب والجذام

That is false. It happens by God's decree and will. … One may not believe in [this kind of] contagion. But it is lawful to take the protective means against harm, by keeping away from someone with a disease that may be transmitted from him to the healthy, by God's permission, such as mange and leprosy.

## Bearing on Principle 1

Principle 1 requires agreement that there is one shared physical world with objective properties. It rejects letting a text, an authority, or a consensus override a measurement, and it rejects reclassifying a failed prediction after the fact. The sources above bear on it as follows.

1. **Both traditions affirm that the physical world is real and that it has consequences.** Neither tradition says that physical properties change when descriptions change. Both say that God acts in the world, which Principle 1's range of views allows.
   * The Ash'ari and Maturidi texts say that created things have no power of their own to produce effects, and that God creates each effect. The Azhar textbook says the connection between cause and effect is one of custom and "can fail to follow." It defines a miracle as a break in custom that accompanies a claim of prophecy (second year, p. 82). It treats the regular behavior of matter, such as metals expanding when heated, as the ordinary course, not as a miracle. Ash'ari creed also accepts wonders worked by saints (karamat); I did not check how this textbook treats them.
   * The Salafi texts say that God created real powers in things, that fire has a "power of burning," and that God can withdraw it.
   * Both positions predict the same observations in ordinary cases. They differ on what the regularity consists in, not on whether it holds.

2. **On the stated rules, both traditions allow established knowledge to override a reading of a text. Neither states the rule in terms of measurement.**
   * Al-Razi's rule says that when definitive reason conflicts with the apparent meaning of a text, the text is reinterpreted.
   * Ibn Taymiyya's rule says the definitive proof wins whichever side it is on, and that a clear rational truth is never contradicted by an authentic text. A text that seems to contradict one is fabricated or misread.
   * Both rules were written about statements concerning God. Both leave open what counts as "definitive." That question decides every actual case.

3. **In one documented Salafi case, a reading of scripture, a claimed consensus, and the senses without instruments were set against astronomical measurement.**
   * Ibn Baz (1965/66, reprinted in 1982, still referred to in an undated later lesson) rejected the earth's daily and yearly rotation.
   * He declared that anyone who says the sun is stationary is an unbeliever, to be killed if he does not repent.
   * He used observation as evidence: people see the sun move and the ground stay still.
   * He said agreement among astronomers is not proof, and that only the consensus of Muslim scholars is protected from error (p. 12).
   * He decided whether a heliocentrist is an unbeliever by how explicit the texts are, not by the physical evidence (pp. 23–24).
   * The same book made belief in the moon landing depend on "scientific evidence" (p. 13). He also accepted that the earth is spherical. Measurement was therefore accepted in some questions and rejected in others.

4. **Other Salafi scholars state the opposite procedure on the same question.**
   * Ibn Uthaymeen held the same reading of the verses. He wrote that if decisive proof shows that the earth turns, the verses must be reinterpreted "so that the Qur'an does not contradict what is known with certainty." He also wrote that the proof had not yet been given, so until then "we will never accept this."
   * This makes observation able to correct a reading, but only at a threshold the reader sets.
   * Al-Albani (reported) accepted rotation as fact.
   * IslamQA (2018) classifies the question as natural science, not doctrine.
   * The Salafi tradition is therefore not uniform on this question.

5. **On human evolution, the verified Salafi sources rank a reading of the Adam narrative above biological evidence.**
   * The Permanent Committee answers the question with Qur'anic verses only and calls the view "not correct."
   * Ibn Baz says that a person who holds it while knowing the texts is most likely an unbeliever.
   * IslamQA (2021) says Darwin's theory "contains unbelief." It still permits studying and teaching biology as a report of what others hold.
   * IslamQA (2009) also makes empirical claims, for example that no transformation of one creature into another has ever been observed. Such claims can be checked against the scientific record.
   * I did not find an official al-Azhar or Dar al-Ifta ruling. This report therefore does not say what the mainstream institutions hold on evolution.

6. **On contagion, both traditions accepted the medical evidence in practice.**
   * In 2020, al-Azhar's senior scholars stated that the virus spreads through crowding, on the word of physicians. They said that physicians and state institutions decide on quarantine.
   * The Saudi senior scholars suspended congregational prayer.
   * Ibn Baz's reading of "lā 'adwā" denies that disease spreads "by its nature." It affirms that disease may pass between people by God's will, and that avoiding the sick is lawful.
   * In this case the text was read so as to agree with observed transmission.

7. **On reclassifying failed predictions:** the Ash'ari doctrine that causes are custom "such that the effect can fail to follow" would, taken alone, allow any non-occurrence to be called God's choice. The textbook does not use it that way. Its examples of exceptions are miracles that confirm a claim to prophecy. I found no text in either tradition that applies this doctrine to explain away a failed physical prediction. I also did not search for one.
