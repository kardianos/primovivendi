# Mainstream Sunni and Salafi positions, compared against the five principles

Compiled 2026-11-10 from the three research reports in this folder. Each claim below is cited in full in the report named in brackets, with Arabic text, translation, location, and a verification label:

* `[K]` `p1_knowledge_causation.md`: reason against text, causes, cosmology, evolution, contagion.
* `[N]` `p2345_nonmuslims_tiers_goods.md`: the good, the tiers of obligation, non-Muslims, human nature.
* `[A]` `p5_apostasy_heresy.md`: apostasy and heresy, classical and modern.

The downloaded sources are in `ext-ref/islam_sunni_salafi/` (repo root; not committed): `dl_p1/`, `dl_p345/`, and `dl_apostasy/`.

"Mainstream Sunni" means the Ash'ari and Maturidi creeds, the four legal schools, and institutions such as al-Azhar and Egypt's Dar al-Ifta. "Salafi" means Ibn Taymiyya, Ibn al-Qayyim, Muhammad ibn 'Abd al-Wahhab, and the Saudi religious establishment (Ibn Baz, Ibn Uthaymeen, al-Fawzan, the Permanent Committee for Research and Fatwa), together with IslamQA.

Spot checks made against the primary text while compiling:

* Ibn Baz, *al-Adilla al-Naqliyya wa'l-Hissiyya*, p. 36 (scan).
* Ibn Baz, interview with *al-Islah*, 1404 AH, on binbaz.org.sa.
* Ibn Bishr, *'Unwan al-Majd*, 1:257 (scan).

## Principle 1: reality is real, knowable, and consequential

| Question | Mainstream Sunni | Salafi |
|---|---|---|
| Do created things produce effects? | No. God creates each effect, and the connection is custom that "can fail to follow." The al-Azhar textbook (2019–2020) calls belief in fire burning "by its nature" unbelief. The Maturidi Nasafi creed agrees on the core point. [K; `../azhar_curriculum/`] | Yes. God created real powers in things and can withdraw them. Ibn Taymiyya: "erasing causes so that they are no longer causes is a defect in reason." [`../salafi_causes/`] |
| Reason against text | Al-Razi: definitive reason prevails, and the text is reinterpreted. The rule concerns statements about God. [K] | Ibn Taymiyya: the definitive proof prevails, and clear reason never conflicts with an authentic text. [K] |
| Sun and earth | No modern institutional statement found. [K] | Ibn Baz (article 1965/66, book 1982): saying the sun is stationary is unbelief, punished as apostasy after a call to repent. The earth's rotation is false, but he withheld the charge of unbelief. He wrote that agreement among astronomers is not proof. Ibn Uthaymeen: the verses would be reinterpreted if decisive proof came, but it had not. Al-Albani (reported): rotation is fact. IslamQA (2018): not a matter of creed. [K] |
| Human evolution | No al-Azhar or Dar al-Ifta ruling found. [K] | Permanent Committee fatwa 5167: "not correct," on Qur'anic grounds. Ibn Baz: likely unbelief for one who knows the texts. IslamQA (2021): "contains unbelief." [K] |
| Contagion (2020) | Al-Azhar: physicians decide, and quarantine is obligatory. [K] | Saudi senior scholars suspended congregational prayer. Ibn Baz: disease passes by God's will, and avoiding the sick is lawful. [K] |

The Ash'ari creed denies consequence in things. The Salafi tradition affirms created causes. In the documented cases of cosmology and evolution, however, its leading scholars ranked their reading of the text above measurement.

## Principle 2: humans are self-interested, limited, and particular

Both traditions share:

* the hadith that every child is born with the innate disposition (*fitra*) (Bukhari 1385);
* Qur'an 49:13, "We made you peoples and tribes";
* the instruction to begin giving with oneself (Muslim 997).

Ibn Taymiyya also ranks Arabs, as a genus, above non-Arabs. [N]

## Principle 3: goodness must include human life continuing, kept and built

* **The five necessities:** religion, life, intellect, lineage, and property (al-Ghazali, *al-Mustasfa*, p. 174).
* **Religion above life:** both traditions rank it so. Al-Amidi states the rule explicitly. Ibn Taymiyya cites "fitna is greater than killing" (2:217). Life is therefore a necessary good but not the highest one. [N]
* **Children:** both encourage them, on the same hadith (Abu Dawud 2050). Ibn Baz forbids a couple's permanent childlessness and any state limit on births. Dar al-Ifta fatwa 4713 (2019) permits childlessness by a couple's mutual choice. [N]

## Principle 4: self-anchored, tiered, and outward; enemies are real

* **The tiers:** the order of obligation matches the book's order. Muslim 997 and al-Nawawi's *Minhaj* put the self first, then the wife, then the nearest kin. The duty to parents and children binds across religion. [N]
* **Loyalty and disavowal** (*al-wala' wa'l-bara'*, Qur'an 58:22) adds a religious tier that crosses the kin tiers. Both traditions define the enemy as one who fights or expels Muslims (60:8–9). [N]
* **How far the disavowal reaches:**
  * Salafi texts extend it to affection, festival greetings, residence, and employment.
  * Dar al-Ifta and al-Azhar confine it to loyalty given because of unbelief, or to those at war, and they permit festival greetings. [N]

## Principle 5: justice is scoped procedural symmetry

* **Classical retaliation and blood money for a non-Muslim killed by a Muslim.**

  | School | Retaliation | Blood money |
  |---|---|---|
  | Hanafi | yes | equal |
  | Maliki | none, except for a killing by ambush | half |
  | Shafi'i | none | one third |
  | Hanbali | none | half |

  The Salafi position is the classical majority position. No modern al-Azhar ruling revising these rules was found. [N]
* **Today, mainstream:**
  * Al-Azhar's 2017 Declaration on Citizenship and the 2019 Document on Human Fraternity state equal citizenship within the nation-state. They reject the term "minorities," and the Human Fraternity document makes the protection of churches a duty. [N]
* **Today, Saudi Salafi:**
  * Ibn Baz (1983): no non-Muslim house of worship may be built anywhere on the Arabian Peninsula, and existing ones "must be demolished when it is possible."
  * The Permanent Committee (2000): no nationality or property ownership for non-Muslims there.
  * Outside the Peninsula, Ibn Baz allows only churches that existed at the conquest. [N]

## Apostates

* **Classical law (all four schools).** The apostate is put to death after a call to repent.
  * The Hanafi school imprisons women instead, and treats the call to repent as recommended rather than required.
  * Execution belongs to the ruler alone. A private person who kills an apostate receives only a discretionary punishment and pays no blood money. [A]
* **Today, Salafi.** Ibn Baz (*Majmu'* 9/303) and the Permanent Committee (fatwa 21166) restate death for apostasy itself. Fatwa 4792 adds that one who has already repented is not punished. [A]
* **Today, Egypt.**
  * Ali Gomaa (2007): "merely rejecting faith" has "no worldly punishment."
  * Al-Tayeb (2016, as reported in the press): apostasy is a discretionary-punishment offense that can reach death, and publicly advocating it is "high treason." [A]
* **Lay opinion.** Pew (2013) found that 88% of all Muslims surveyed in Egypt favor death for leaving Islam, and 86% of those who want sharia as official law. Saudi Arabia was not surveyed. [A]

## Heretics

* **Historical: group verdicts on both sides.**
  * Al-Ghazali on the Batiniyya: "we assassinate them."
  * The Ottoman Shaykh al-Islam Ebussuud on the Kızılbaş: fighting them is the greater jihad, and their women may be enslaved.
  * Ibn Taymiyya on the Nusayris.
  * The Saudi-Wahhabi raid on Karbala in 1216 AH (1802): Ibn Bishr writes that "they killed most of its people in the markets and the houses," and about 2,000 men died.

  Each source also contains a limiting clause. [A]
* **Today.**
  * Salafi texts (the *Nawaqid*, *Kashf al-Shubuhat*, and Permanent Committee fatwa 21166) count calling on saints or the dead, and sacrificing at graves, as apostasy-level association of partners with God. [A]
  * The mainstream and Salafi camps dispute who holds the title "Ahl al-Sunna" (Grozny 2016). Ibn Baz: the Ash'aris are "not unbelievers." [A]
  * Pakistan's Ordinance XX (1984) makes it a crime for Ahmadis to call themselves Muslims. [A]

## Bearing on Chapter 3

The Salafi tradition affirms created causes. The same establishment, however, ruled on the sun and on evolution from the text against measurement, and it treats apostates and non-Muslims as set out above. Naming it in Chapter 3 as the tradition that teaches consequence would suggest an agreement with Principle 1 that the record does not support. Draft 3, now section 2 of `civ/chapters/03_ch03.md`, says only that "Islam has several major schools of theology, and not all of them teach this account of causes." That sentence is accurate and says nothing about any one school.
