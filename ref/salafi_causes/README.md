# Salafi texts on causes

Collected 2026-11-10 for `civ/chapters/03_ch03.md`, section 2. Ibn Taymiyya and Ibn al-Qayyim are the authorities of the Salafi tradition. IslamQA is a Salafi fatwa site.

## Ibn Taymiyya (1263–1328), *Majmu' al-Fatawa*, volume 8

Source: Arabic Wikisource, https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/مجموع_الفتاوى/المجلد_الثامن/سئل_عما_قاله_أبو_حامد_الغزالي_في_الرزق_المضمون_والمقسوم (fetched 2026-11-10; local copy removed). He is answering a question about a passage in al-Ghazali's *Minhaj al-'Abidin* on provision and reliance on God:

> فالالتفات إلى الأسباب شرك في التوحيد، ومحو الأسباب أن تكون أسبابًا نقص في العقل، والإعراض عن الأسباب المأمور بها قدح في الشرع، فعلى العبد أن يكون قلبه معتمدًا على الله، لا على سبب من الأسباب

Relying on causes is associating partners [with God] in the doctrine of His unity. Erasing causes so that they are no longer causes is a defect in reason. Turning away from the causes that are commanded is a fault against the revealed law. The servant's heart must rely on God, not on any one of the causes.

The same sentence appears, with small variations, at several other places in *Majmu' al-Fatawa* (volumes 1, 8, and 10). IslamQA cites one of them as 8/169.

## Ibn al-Qayyim (1292–1350), *I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in*, part 3

Source: Arabic Wikisource, https://ar.wikisource.org/wiki/إعلام_الموقعين/الجزء_الثالث (fetched 2026-11-10; local copy removed). The passage follows his list of Qur'anic texts "affirming causality" (المثبتة للسببية): 50:9, 7:57, 23:19, 9:14, 16:69, and 17:82, located by searching the Tanzil text. Against these, he says, the deniers of causes set ambiguous texts such as 35:3 ("Is there any creator other than God?") and 8:17.

> ونحو ذلك من المتشابه الذي إنما يدل على أن مالك السبب وخالقه يتصرف فيه بأن يسلبه سببيته إن شاء ويبقيها عليه إن شاء كما سلب النار قوة الإحراق عن الخليل ويالله العجب أترى من أثبت الأسباب وقال إن الله خالقها أثبت خالقا غير الله؟

... and similar ambiguous texts, which show only that the Owner and Creator of the cause disposes of it: He takes its causality away from it if He wills and leaves it with it if He wills, as He took away from the fire its power of burning for the Friend [Abraham]. How strange: does one who affirms causes, and says that God is their Creator, affirm a creator other than God?

## IslamQA, answer 238544, "حكم الالتفات إلى الأسباب" (The ruling on relying on causes)

Saved copy: `ext-ref/salafi_causes/islamqa_238544_2016-03-20.html` (repo root; not committed); live page https://islamqa.info/ar/answers/238544. Dated 11 Jumada II 1437 / 20 March 2016. The page showed 59,196 views when fetched on 2026-11-10. It quotes Ibn Taymiyya's sentence (as *Majmu' al-Fatawa* 8/169) and *Dar' Ta'arud al-'Aql wa'l-Naql* 9/29. Of a physician who tells a patient he will die of cancer:

> فإذا نظر الطبيب والمريض إلى أنّ الحس والمشاهدة والتجربة، قد بينت أنّ سنة الله في هذا المرض أنه من الأمراض الخطيرة التي تسبب الموت كثيرا، إذا انظرا إلى ذلك، فاعتبرا غلبة الظن هذه مع عدم الجزم، ومع الاعتقاد أنه تحت مشيئة الله تعالى فهذا الالتفات لا يقدح في توحيدهما

If the physician and the patient consider that sense, observation, and experiment have shown that God's way with this disease is that it is among the serious diseases that often cause death, and they treat this as a strong probability without certainty, while believing that it is under God's will, then this regard [for the cause] does not impair their belief in God's unity.

Its ruling on the other case: believing that the disease "acts independently" (مستقل بالتأثير) impairs that belief. Taking the means is, in general, obligatory. Eating and drinking are given as causes that preserve life, and someone who refuses them and starves dies a sinner.
