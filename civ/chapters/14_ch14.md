## Chapter 14: The Daily Practice of Civilization

A treatise of principles left unused on a shelf fails its own standard.

Chapter 1 described civilization as a **technology**. It has two parts, **Will** and **Method**, and together they answer how human life continues and flourishes across generations. A technology is judged by use. This chapter gives three instruments for using it:

1. **Diagnostic Tools for Public Debate and Intellectual Defense;**
2. **Seven Questions Before a Decision;** and
3. **The Order of One Ordinary Day.**

---

### 1. Diagnostic Tools for Public Debate and Intellectual Defense

In a public debate, a school board meeting, a university seminar, or a civic discussion, four diagnostic tests identify a defective argument and refute it systematically:

#### Tool 1: Refuting the Unfalsifiable Argument

Frequently, a speaker's belief system is constructed so that it can never be refuted by any event in the physical world. Such a belief is **sealed to proof in the world**. Sealed to proof means that the belief holds to a source of truth that no observation is allowed to correct (Chapter 1). The belief claims something about reality. No event can count against that claim. A belief about God is a different case. For the ultimate questions that produce no measurable distinction in the observable world, the principles issue no catechism and no anti-catechism (Chapter 3). Such a belief is not sealed to proof, because proof is a test against the world and no test reaches a claim about what lies beyond the world. This tool examines only how a belief treats reality. The tool reaches a belief about God only where that belief states something about the world.

If a socialist government nationalizes the Venezuelan petroleum industry and, for two years while oil prices are high, distributes subsidized consumer goods, the partisan declares: *"Socialism succeeds; credit that outcome to Socialism."*

Three years later, when the unmaintained oil equipment fails, the currency collapses in runaway inflation, and families suffer severe food shortages, one points to the same country and the same government, and the partisan replies: *"That is not the fault of Socialism; it resulted from foreign sabotage, hoarders, adverse weather, and an imperfect implementation of Socialism."*

The structural defect in that reasoning is evident: it applies asymmetrical accounting to historical outcomes. Every favorable result is credited to the theory, while every severe failure caused by executing the theory's own program is attributed to external interference. Three steps detect the defect.

**First, state the program in advance.** What concrete policies does the system prescribe (e.g., abolishing private property, reducing police enforcement, removing examination standards)? Principle 2 gives the reason. People find what they expect to find. The N-rays were falsely claimed in this way. A French physicist announced them in 1903, and only some observers could see them. In 1904 R. W. Wood visited the laboratory and found that the observer could not say when a hand entered the path of the rays or which way a prism was turned, and that a piece of wood in place of a steel file changed nothing (Wood, *Nature* 70, 1904, pp. 530 and 531). A test fixed after the result is fixed by what the observer expects. A program stated after its years is stated to fit them. Ask what is expected before the experiment is run.

**Second, apply the same standard of evaluation to arguments for and against.** This is procedural symmetry, and Principle 5 requires it. The partisan's excuse is a statement like any other. *"foreign sabotage, hoarders, adverse weather"* can be asked who did what, and when, and what it changed. A claim made for the doctrine is asked the same questions. If the successes are counted on the doctrine's word and the failures are excused on the partisan's word, two standards have been applied.

**Third, specify what observable fact would falsify the claim.** Ask directly: *"What concrete outcome in the physical world over the next five years would demonstrate to you that this policy is erroneous?"* Principle 1 gives the reason. Truth is correspondence to reality. A claim that no possible observation could prove false corresponds to none in particular. A small group of Americans once expected a flood on 21 December and a flying saucer to carry them away before it. The fact was named in advance, and it was specific. *"Midnight had passed and nothing had happened."* At about 4:45 in the morning the group received a message: *"The little group, sitting all night long, had spread so much light that God had saved the world from destruction"* (Festinger, Riecken, and Schachter, *When Prophecy Fails*, 1956, p. 68). The believers did not give up the belief. They changed the claim. The flood had been called off, they said, and the new claim stated nothing that an event could show to be false. A claim that fails its test does not cross to the metaphysical side afterward (Chapter 3). If the partisan cannot state a single falsifying condition, he is not conducting empirical inquiry or policy analysis; he is reciting an unfalsifiable dogma (**violating Principle 1**).

Claims about reality are always testable against reality. A doctrine is tested by all of its results, the losses as well as the gains. The three steps are that test: the program, the standard, and the fact that would count against it. By Principle 5, the speaker runs his own doctrine through the same three steps before he requires them of another.

#### Tool 2: The Narrow "Not Authentic Doctrine" Test

Related to asymmetrical accounting is the familiar "That was not authentic doctrine" evasion (*"The Soviet Union was not real Communism,"* *"The Inquisition was not real Christianity,"* *"Al-Qaeda is not real Islam"*). The defender removes every failed case from the ledger by denying that the people who produced it practiced the doctrine.

How does scoped procedural symmetry distinguish when *"That was not authentic doctrine"* is a **valid boundary distinction** versus when it is a **fallacious evasion**?

The guide is correspondence to reality. The test compares what the actors did with what the doctrine's own texts instruct, and it starts with the scope of the doctrine.

A doctrine that prescribes a political and social order is tested by its program. When rulers carry out what its texts instruct, their conduct corresponds to the doctrine, and the years that follow belong on the doctrine's ledger.

Lenin, Stalin, Mao, and Pol Pot followed Karl Marx's explicit command in the *Communist Manifesto* to *"abolish private property"* and concentrate all credit, transport, agriculture, and manufacturing in a central state monopoly. One cannot rightly claim afterward, "That was not authentic Marxism." The famine was not a departure from the doctrine; the famine was the direct consequence of executing those instructions in the physical world.

An ethic that explicitly leaves politics to another authority has no program to compare with a government's acts. The Gospels are the clearest case. Jesus says, *"give to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's"* (Matthew 22:21), and, *"My kingdom is not from this world"* (John 18:36). Men who bear that religion's name later held power and used it, as the Inquisition did. Their conduct cannot be read off the doctrine. The question is then whether the political act was a direct result of the doctrine. It was, if the doctrine's texts command the act, or if rules that the doctrine's own recognized authorities derive from those texts command it. It was not, if no text commands it and the texts forbid it. An act of the second kind belongs to the men who performed it and not to the doctrine they named.

A written rule of law is the simplest case, because its scope and its text are both explicit. Suppose a corrupt judge accepts a ten-thousand-dollar bribe to alter a verdict in an American courtroom. An American may legitimately state, *"That judge was violating American constitutional law."* Article II, Section 4 of the Constitution makes bribery a ground for removal from office, 18 U.S.C. § 201 makes it a crime, and the judicial oath binds the judge to administer justice without respect to persons (28 U.S.C. § 453).

Principle 5 requires the same test for every doctrine: the accuser's own, the defender's own, and the doctrine under examination alike.

#### Tool 3: Identifying Selective Logic and Tier Confusion

Two verification checks apply whenever someone advances a moral or political claim. The first is the **Label-Swap Test**: take the speaker's exact proposition, exchange the political, racial, or religious labels of the two parties, and restate the proposition to him. If he immediately objects when his own procedural rule is applied to his own faction, he has demonstrated that he rejects Principle 5 and seeks a partial tribunal.

The second is the **Tier-Scope Check**: ask whether the speaker is demanding Tier 2 household intimacy and unconditional generosity within Tier 4/5 public borders and anonymous bureaucracies, or conversely, demanding Tier 5 impersonal contractual calculation within Tier 2 marriage and parenting. Maintain every obligation within its proper tier and scope.

#### Tool 4: Free Speech as an Error-Correction Mechanism

Because reality is real, knowable, and consequential, and humans are limited, a civilization must defend free speech and open scientific inquiry against censors who claim that censorship "protects the public from dangerous errors":

> **The Walker in the Marsh:** Ten travelers are walking in single file through a fog-covered marsh at dusk. The lead walker steps onto what appears to be solid ground, and immediately sinks to his waist in quicksand.
>
> Naturally, he calls back to the nine walkers behind him: *"Stop. Do not step here. This ground is quicksand."*
>
> Now suppose that the expedition leader has enacted a rule of "Positive Speech Only," on the ground that warnings of hazards cause alarm, undermine confidence in the expedition plan, and disturb morale, and the lead walker is silenced.
>
> The quicksand does not become solid stone because no one is permitted to mention it. Walkers two through ten march blindly into the same hazard and perish.

**Free speech is not an indulgence granted merely for personal self-expression; free speech is a civilization's primary mechanism for detecting and correcting error.** Because every human magistrate, scientist, and planner is limited and fallible, silencing the citizen who reports that a medical policy, an economic plan, or an engineering design is failing in practice severs the transmission of information between the operator and the physical system.

### 2. Seven Questions Before a Decision

Seven questions can be put to any serious decision, whether in a household, an enterprise, a school board, or a vote:

1. **Reality and truth.** What are the verifiable physical and historical facts, stripped of wishful thinking and partisan distortion? What will happen in reality if we do this?
2. **Human limits and incentives.** Does this plan assume that people are selfless, or does it account for self-interest, limited knowledge, and attachment to particular people? Have I checked my own oversights?
3. **Keeping and building.** Does this decision keep what already works and build durable value, competence, and beauty, or does it consume capital for present comfort?
4. **Self-anchored tiers.** Are the self and the household provided for before scarce resources go to the outer tiers?
5. **Enemies.** How would a swindler, a free rider, or an enemy use or attack this arrangement? Are its boundaries and reserves guarded?
6. **Scoped procedural symmetry.** Is the rule stated in advance and applied alike to everyone in the same position inside this scope? Would I accept it if the labels were swapped?
7. **The generational horizon.** If everyone in our community followed this rule for eighty years, would our great-grandchildren be more numerous, more capable, and freer, or depleted and insolvent?

A father of three is offered a promotion that raises his salary by a quarter and keeps him in airports and hotels from Monday to Friday for six years. He already houses, feeds, and educates his children. His marriage is strained, and his ten-year-old son is struggling in school. The fourth and seventh questions decide it. If the family were in want, travel to feed them would be the father's duty to the household. A family that is already solvent gains goods it does not need and loses its father in the decade when the children's character is formed. No one at the company will remember the quarterly report in thirty years, and a broken marriage or a neglected son has consequences across generations.

The second, fifth, sixth, and seventh questions govern the charter when five families found a school, an apprenticeship guild, or a civic association. The families write the founding premises (objective standards, parental authority, and solvency) into the charter and the property trust before any money is spent. They place governance in the hands of people with a stake in the institution, parents and masters who contribute labor and dues, not distant grant offices with conditions attached. They also write in advance the rule by which a teacher or officer who lowers the standard or falsifies the results is removed.

### 3. Individuals as Vectors of Civilization: One Ordinary Day

We began with a newborn infant, naked, ignorant, and helpless against the elements, and a three-hundred-ton airliner crossing the ocean at thirty-five thousand feet. We asked how human beings bridge the distance between that helpless infant and a flourishing, multi-generational world.

We saw that the bridge is **Civilization**: the foundational technology of Will and Method by which human life and the ability to keep and build it are transmitted from generation to generation.

We saw that this foundational technology rests on Five Common Principles of Reality:

1. **Reality is real, knowable, and consequential. Truth is what corresponds to reality.**
2. **Humans are self-interested, limited, and particular.**
3. **Goodness must include human life continuing, and that life kept and built.**
4. **What you keep and build is self-anchored, tiered, and outward. Enemies are real.**
5. **Justice is scoped procedural symmetry.**

And we saw that when this technology is practiced by an individual human being, it takes the form of the **Seven Virtues of Civilization**:

1. **Align with truth**
2. **Take ownership, which is responsibility**
3. **Seek excellence**
4. **Admire beauty**
5. **Be humble**
6. **Have children**
7. **Form and listen**

In physics and engineering, a **vector** is a quantity that possesses both **magnitude** (strength) and **direction** (orientation).

A civilization of fifty million or three hundred million people is not an independent abstraction existing apart from its members; it is the vector sum of millions of individual human lives.

When millions of individuals direct their daily vectors toward falsehood, evasion of responsibility, negligent work, cynicism, childlessness, and the consumption of inherited capital, the resultant vector of the whole society points downward toward decline, and no prime minister or central bank can reverse that trajectory.

When men and women across a nation direct their daily vectors toward correspondence truth, self-anchored ownership, craft excellence, beauty, humility, raising children, and forming the younger while listening to those who formed them, under symmetrical laws, the resultant vector of that civilization points upward toward durability and growth. Even a resource-poor island or a damaged town after a storm can then become a productive center of human life.

Although every citizen's station, trade, and season of life differs, we may see how practical and orderly this technology is by tracing **One Ordinary Day** in the life of a man or woman who practices it.

At **6:00 a.m.** (Tier 1, Virtue 2), the alarm sounds before dawn. You rise, wash, exercise your body, and place your own mind and conduct in order first, establishing self-command so that everyone who depends upon you today encounters a steady, capable adult.

At **7:00 a.m.** (Tier 2, Virtues 6 and 7), in the kitchen, you prepare breakfast with your spouse and sit down with your children. The room reflects order: it is not a palace, but the floor is swept clean, the cabinet hinges are tight, and in the basement below stands the rotated winter pantry (Principle 3: Kept). Over breakfast, you review your ten-year-old's spelling words and listen to your seven-year-old's account, expending costly action-based love where it belongs, in the inner tier entrusted to your care.

From **8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m.** (Tiers 3 and 4), you step out your front door into the wider community. As you pass strangers on the sidewalk and travel to work, you extend dispositional love and politeness, obeying traffic laws, dealing fairly, and wishing your fellow citizens well. At your workplace, whether you are an aircraft mechanic, an engineer, a carpenter, a surgeon, a teacher, or a magistrate, you align with truth and seek excellence. When a test gauge indicates a crack in a fitting or an error in a calculation, you do not falsify the report to please a superior. You report the physical truth as it is, and you verify your work with checklists because you recognize that you are a fallible mortal (Principle 2, Virtue 5). When you complete the task, you leave the workbench or desk cleaner, more orderly, and more functional than you found it (Virtue 4). When you evaluate two applicants or two contracts in the afternoon, you apply the same written standard to both without regard to their faction or personal connections (Principle 5).

At **6:00 p.m.** (Principle 3, Virtue 7), at home before supper, you spend forty-five minutes with your son and daughter by the shed replacing a decayed fence post or splitting firewood, teaching them the proper use of a level and a wrench and the discipline of sustained labor. They listen. They test the method by using it, and they are permitted to practice, err, and master the tool (Chapter 10). You do not require them to agree with every word. You repay the grandfather who instructed you by forming the grandchildren who will inhabit this community eighty years hence.

At **8:00 p.m.** (Principle 4 outward), twice a month after supper, instead of spending the evening in passive distraction and complaining that "someone ought to reform the country," you attend the meeting of the town council, the school board, the volunteer fire company, or the parish vestry. You audit the actual budget ledger, defend the academic standards of the local school, and ensure that the municipal police and courts are supported in protecting property and maintaining public order.

At **10:00 p.m.**, before extinguishing the lights, you inspect the perimeter of your house, verify that the doors are locked (enemies are real), check on your sleeping children under a dry, sound roof, and converse quietly with your spouse.

That single day illustrates the complete mechanism. There was no public spectacle and no utopian illusion in it. Yet between six in the morning and ten at night, human life continued, reality was respected, decay was arrested, a household was sheltered, a municipality was strengthened, and the accumulated inheritance of five thousand years of human method was transmitted one day further into the future.

That is how civilization is built, and that is how civilization is kept, including on the difficult days when adversity, fatigue, or corrupt institutions test your resolve. Stand firm in your station, knowing that trials temper what is genuine and that millions of fellow builders across the world are holding the same line. May you keep a sober mind and a joyful heart through every test; may all that you keep and build be established on a sound foundation; and may your household and your descendants prosper in abundance for generations to come.
