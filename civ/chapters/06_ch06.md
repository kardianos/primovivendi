## Chapter 6: Principle 3: Goodness Must Include Human Life Continuing, and That Life Kept and Built.

### From What Is to What Ought to Be: Evaluating Moral Systems Across Time

In Principles 1 and 2, we described *what is*: we inhabit a real, knowable, consequential world where truth corresponds to reality, and we ourselves are self-interested, limited, particular human beings whose survival and cooperation depend on discovered arrangements and cultivated habits.

Now we reach the third foundational question of every society, the transition from *is* to *ought*: **What is the standard of the good? What ought human life and human society to aim at?**

Pure physical description by itself does not mechanically compel a person to care whether he lives or dies; a man who wishes to jump off a cliff can understand gravity perfectly while choosing death. Every moral and political system therefore begins by affirming a standard of goodness.

How do we test competing definitions of goodness in reality? By doing what an engineer does when he evaluates a system: **we project the operation of the competing moral codes forward across fifty, one hundred, and two hundred years, and determine what happens to the people who practice them.**

As soon as time runs forward, two continuous physical processes act against every human achievement:

1. **Material Entropy and Decay:** In physical reality, ordered structures never maintain themselves. Left alone, iron rusts, roof timbers rot, irrigation canals silt up, roads crack under winter frost, tools lose their edge, and houses accumulate filth and disease.
2. **Biological Aging and Death:** Every living human being who cleared the land, built the houses, wrote the laws, and learned the engineering skills grows old, loses strength and memory, and dies within roughly eight decades.

Over three generations, the outcome for any society whose definition of "the good" omits human survival, reproduction, maintenance, or building is invariable:

* If a group defines goodness in a way that leads to its own physical death, or promotes childlessness so that it fails to replace its retiring adults, or lets its farms, workshops, laws, and defenses fall into ruin, then within a century that code fails because its own commands produced the death or the ruin. Death at an enemy's hands is a different fact, and it belongs under Principle 4. A people can keep a sound code and still be destroyed by someone who has set out to destroy them.

A moral code that destroys the people who practice it eliminates itself from reality. Therefore, whatever else a religion or philosophy may value, we have derived our third foundational constraint of reality:

> **Principle 3 of Reality: Goodness must include human life continuing, and that life kept and built.**

We will now analyze each of the three parts of Principle 3 (*Goodness must include*, *human life continuing*, and *that life kept and built*) and see how they expose the structural flaws in several modern rival ethics.

### 1. "Goodness Must Include": A Necessary Minimum Condition, Not an Upper Limit

The exact phrasing of the first three words is essential: **Goodness must include...**

Principle 3 does not claim that bare biological survival and material maintenance are the *upper limit* or the exhaustive totality of all human aspiration. A devout Christian or Jew may hold that the highest good includes loving God, holiness, and eternal salvation; a contemplative philosopher may hold that wisdom and artistic creation complete a well-lived life. Principle 3 leaves full scope for those higher spiritual and intellectual aims, **provided they rest upon, and do not undermine, the necessary minimum condition of human life continuing, kept, and built.**

That minimum condition is mandatory because a dead man cannot pray, cannot contemplate philosophy, cannot paint a work of art, cannot keep a promise, and cannot show kindness to his neighbor. Any moral code that undermines the preconditions of survival, maintenance, and generational replacement in the name of a "higher" ideal is a prescription for self-destruction disguised as ethics.

Two widespread modern definitions of "the good," when carried to their logical conclusions, undermine that minimum condition:

#### Rival A: Dismantling the Moral Framework of "Consent as Morality"

One of the most pervasive moral reductionisms of the modern era, shared across progressive social theory, radical libertarianism, and popular ethics, is the claim that **Consent is the sole foundation and definition of morality**.

Stated in its standard form, the "Consent as Morality" framework asserts three propositions:

1. *Whatever consenting adults agree to do with or to one another is, by definition, moral and beyond judgment.*
2. *Nothing is immoral unless it violates someone's personal consent.*
3. *No human being bears any moral obligation toward anyone or anything to which he did not personally and voluntarily consent.*

At first glance, this formula appears simple, modern, and humane: no inherited commandments, no unchosen duties, no moral judgment over how neighbors live, and no obligations toward the unborn.

Now test **Consent as Morality** against physical and generational reality. When evaluated against the Five Common Principles, it fails on **five separate foundational points**:

1. **Failure Under Principle 1: Reality Does Not Ask Your Consent.**

    Physical, biological, and economic reality exists whether or not anyone perceives it correctly, and **it imposes consequences whether or not anyone consents to them.**

    - Two pilots in a cockpit can sign a notarized, unanimous contract agreeing to ignore the stall warning and raise the nose of the aircraft in an ice storm; the wing still stalls and the aircraft still crashes.
    - A legislature and fifty million voters can unanimously *consent* to print ten trillion dollars of paper currency while producing no new grain, steel, or electricity; the currency still hyperinflates and the markets still empty.
    - Consent is a state of subjective agreement between human minds; it has no power to repeal gravity, thermodynamics, biology, or arithmetic. Any moral system that equates *"everyone involved agreed"* with *"this corresponds to reality and will succeed"* has confused a vote with a physical law.

2. **Failure Under Principle 2: Limited, Appetitive, and Developing Humans Cannot Be Governed by Pure Consent.**

    Under Principle 2, human beings are *self-interested, limited, and particular*, and they begin life as helpless infants who require twenty years to mature.

    - **The Problem of Children:** A newborn infant, a three-year-old child, or a ten-year-old boy cannot give mature, informed consent. A three-year-old explicitly *refuses* to consent to eating vegetables, taking bitter medicine, going to sleep at eight o'clock, learning phonics, or being pulled by the collar out of the path of an oncoming truck. If consent is the sole foundation of morality, then either (a) all parental authority and education constitute "unconsented oppression" (paralyzing childhood formation), or (b) ideologues advance the destructive claim that children can "consent" to adult appetites or irreversible medical and ideological procedures. In reality, what protects and forms a child is **never** the fiction of the child's consent; it is **objective biological reality and the scoped, unchosen duty of parents and adults to keep and build the child's life (Principles 3 and 4).**
    - **The Exploitation of Human Limits and Appetites:** Even among adults past their eighteenth birthday, human beings have cognitive limitations, emotional vulnerabilities, and addictive appetites. When a narcotics dealer sells a lethal dose to a desperate addict, both parties "consented" to the transaction. When a predatory lender or gambling house strips a compulsive gambler of his family's house, both parties signed the contract. When two twenty-year-olds consent to a lethal duel, or when a disturbed youth consents to mutilate his healthy body or sell himself into degradation, the presence of a signature does not make the act good. **Consent cannot convert poison into nourishment.**

3. **Failure Under Principle 3: "The Empty Planet Problem" and Consensual Self-Destruction.**

    Follow a pure person-affecting, consent-only standard to its logical conclusion.

    - Suppose the currently living generation of human beings decides, by **wholly peaceful, unanimous consent**, to sterilize itself, burn the great libraries for winter heat, consume the remaining stores of food and fuel in comfort for fifty years, bear no children, and let the human race go completely extinct when the last eighty-year-old dies.
    - On a pure consent-only ethics, **no moral wrong whatsoever has occurred.** Nobody's consent was violated; no existing person on the ledger was forced against his will; and once the last human dies, there is nobody left on the ledger to object. Under that standard, **a dead planet of bare rock under a hot sun and a living planet populated by flourishing families, music, science, and cities are morally equivalent.**
    - That conclusion offends every sound human conscience because a morality that cannot distinguish between a living civilization and a lifeless rock has already chosen indifference to life. Under **Principle 3**, human life continuing, kept, and built is the objective moral minimum: **consensual destruction of life, health, competence, and posterity remains destruction.**

4. **Failure Under Principle 4 and Virtues 6–7: The Unchosen Obligations That Keep Every Human Alive.**

    Ask how many of the most important, life-sustaining moral duties in human existence originated in a revocable consent contract:

    - Did you sign a consent form before being born to your particular mother and father, in your particular country and century? No.
    - Once a mother and father bring a child into the world, can they wake up when the baby is six months old and say, *"We withdraw our consent to parenting today, so we are leaving the baby on the kitchen floor"*? If they do, the law rightly imprisons them for criminal neglect, because their obligation is fixed by objective reality and tier, not by ongoing subjective preference.
    - Does a grown son owe care and honor to the aging mother and father who fed him for twenty years only if he feels like consenting at age forty?
    - Does a citizen owe defense to the country whose laws and soldiers sheltered his childhood only if he feels like consenting on the morning the enemy crosses the border?
    - Does **Virtue 7 (Form and listen)** bind the younger to listen to and test the experience of the elders who formed them, and bind living adults to form those younger than themselves, including great-grandchildren not yet born? None of them can sign a consent contract today. The younger listen and test what they are told. Elders, including kin and those who lead, form the people younger than themselves.
    - Remove **unchosen, tiered obligations**, and every marriage, every household, every army, and every intergenerational trust dissolves the first afternoon that maintaining one's post becomes fatiguing or costly.

5. **Where Consent Actually Belongs: Inside Principle 5 as a Scoped Procedural Instrument Among Peers.**

    Once we set aside the misconception that consent is the *foundation of morality*, we can see immediately where consent *does* belong and why it is valuable there:

    - Consent is **not** Principle 3 (the definition of the good); consent is an operational instrument inside **Principle 5 (Scoped Procedural Symmetry)**.
    - Within the scope of **competent adult peers dealing with one another inside a lawful order**, such as buying and selling a workshop, signing an employment contract, taking marriage vows, or electing representatives under a constitution (*"the consent of the governed"*), requiring mutual consent is the **symmetrical procedural check** that prevents one citizen from robbing, enslaving, or defrauding another.
    - Keep the hierarchy clear: **Principle 3 (keeping and building human life) and Principle 4 (tiered obligation) establish the institutional order; consent is the procedural rule governing voluntary transactions among adult peers within that order.** One must not mistake a procedural rule among peers for the foundation of morality itself.

#### Rival B: Harm-Reduction and the Absence of Suffering as the Supreme Good

A second, even more widespread modern rival defines goodness as **the minimization of harm and suffering**.

Again, begin by recognizing harm-reduction as a legitimate *subordinate consideration*: installing guardrails on a high staircase, filtering cholera bacteria out of drinking water, and wearing seatbelts in automobiles reduce needless injury while helping human life continue and build. Under Principle 3, those are genuine improvements.

The failure occurs when **harm-reduction is elevated from a subordinate consideration to the supreme definition of the good**, because once "reducing suffering and risk of harm" becomes the supreme moral rule, **it has no limiting principle within living reality**:

* Working with heavy machinery, building bridges, and sailing ships carry a risk of painful injury; therefore, curtail them.
* Competing in an objective examination, starting a business, or proposing marriage carries a risk of failure, rejection, and emotional distress; therefore, abolish the examinations and discourage the risk.
* Having and raising children guarantees morning sickness, the pain of childbirth, sleepless nights, financial sacrifice, and the certainty that every child born will someday suffer grief, illness, and death; therefore, declare childbearing an unethical imposition of harm upon the unborn and promote childlessness ("anti-natalism") as the highest environmental and moral virtue.

Extend the proposition that "goodness is the reduction of suffering" to its mathematical limit, and what is the only state of the earth in which suffering, pain, inequality, and risk of harm are permanently eliminated? **Total extinction: lifeless rock under a sun, with not a single living mind remaining in the universe.**

When an ethical system's logical endpoint is the elimination of all living beings capable of acting, building, or caring, one is not looking at a higher form of compassion. One is looking at nihilism disguised as humanitarian concern.

### 2. "Human Life Continuing": Revealed Preference and Cultural Worthiness

The second element of Principle 3 is **human life continuing**.

Continuing has two inseparable time horizons:

1. **In the present:** Living human beings must stay alive, fed, sheltered, and defended against violence today.
2. **Across generations:** Living adults must bring the next generation of children into the world and raise them to maturity so that life and the civilization do not terminate when the current generation dies.

First, Principle 3 specifies **human** life continuing. Protecting soil, timber, and clean rivers is part of keeping the human habitat in working order, not a license to treat human beings as a blight upon the earth whose population should be reduced for the sake of undisturbed wilderness. Every living human stands at the front of an unbroken multi-millennial chain of parents who struggled through ice ages, plagues, and famines to pass life forward. That chain is **revealed preference demonstrated by action across generations**, rather than by academic rhetoric.

Second, a crucial truth governs the evaluation of cultures: **affirming that human life should continue and be kept and built is NOT a claim that every cultural system is equally worthy of continuance.**

A culture is a set of shared customs, beliefs, and methods. Like a mechanical design, a culture is evaluated by reality according to whether it enables the people living under it to survive, keep what they have, and build upward. If a society adopts customs that despise work, forbid inquiry, and leave its people living in squalor generation after generation, let no one be surprised when an iron-age or industrial civilization that masters metallurgy, law, and logistics overcomes it without difficulty. Survival is not a guarantee conferred unconditionally upon every culture regardless of performance; **prospering (keeping and building) is required for survival itself.**

### 3. "And That Life Kept and Built": Entropy, Property, and the Trajectory of Living Well

The final words of Principle 3 state: **and that life kept and built.**

Why is bare biological survival not sufficient? Why must Principle 3 join *continuing* with *kept* and *built*?

#### Kept: Maintaining Order Against Physical and Social Decay

**Kept** means maintaining what already exists in working order against the relentless pull of physical entropy and human neglect.

To see how quickly an unmaintained structure negates the work of its construction, trace the effect of a single displaced shingle on the roof of a house:

> **The Example of the Lifted Shingle:** A carpenter builds a sturdy timber-framed house and lays courses of cedar or slate shingles across the roof. The courses overlap so that rainwater drains by gravity from upper shingle to lower shingle, off the eaves, and into the gutter away from the walls.
>
> Five years later, a winter wind lifts a single shingle near the ridge and leaves a gap the size of a man's hand. The homeowner sees it from the yard in April, shrugs, and decides he is too busy to climb the ladder this week.
>
> All through a dry July, nothing appears to change; the living room remains warm and dry. Then comes a wet October. Rainwater penetrates the gap and collects on the roofing felt. By November, the felt deteriorates, the iron nails corrode, and the oak rafter beneath absorbs the moisture. By the second winter, fungal mycelium spreads through the damp timber. By the third winter, a brown stain spreads across the plaster ceiling of the bedroom, the air of the house smells of mold, and the structural beam that took a hundred years to grow in the forest and a week of a master carpenter's labor to frame is rotted through at the bearing point.

The builder's work was not destroyed by a hurricane in an afternoon. It was negated, season by season, by an owner who treated a constructed object as if it would maintain itself.

Every human technology is subject to this same physical progression:

* **A house or a floor:** Sweeping a floor so that grit, vermin, and filth do not accumulate in the living space is the fundamental act of *keeping*, and sweeping the floor is the same moral and operational act whether the floor is packed dirt in a poor peasant cottage or polished marble in a wealthy townhouse.
* **A skill or a language:** A surgeon who stops operating, a machinist who stops measuring, or a population that stops teaching grammar and arithmetic to its ten-year-olds loses the skill within a single generation.
* **A marriage, a law, or a border:** Trust left unmaintained and laws left unenforced deteriorate as surely as wet oak.

#### Built: Why Destroying Property Is Destroying Human Life

**Built** means creating new capacity, new structures, new tools, new knowledge, and new capable human beings that were not there before, enlarging the margin of survival above bare subsistence.

Here Principle 3 exposes a pernicious falsehood often heard during urban riots or socialist confiscations. When rioters loot and burn down a neighborhood grocery store, a machine shop, or an apartment building, apologists on television regularly excuse the destruction by saying: *"Calm down; nobody was killed. It is only property, and human life is more important than mere property."*

Evaluate that burned-out shop in terms of physical reality:

* What was that shop before the match was thrown?
* The shopkeeper, a mortal human being with a finite allotment of roughly twenty-five thousand adult days in his lifespan (Principle 2), spent **ten thousand irreplaceable hours of his one life** waking before dawn, saving coin by coin, laying bricks, wiring electrical circuits, bolting down shelves, and stocking food and tools so that the structure could feed his children and serve the neighborhood.
* While it is true that a burned building can be rebuilt by expending *another* ten thousand hours of human life whereas a murdered human being cannot be raised from the grave, **there is no separation between "human life" and "built property."** Built property (homes, farms, shops, tools, power grids, and savings) is expended human time and labor converted into durable material form, serving as the external support system that keeps human beings alive. To burn down a man's shop or confiscate his tools is to destroy ten thousand hours of his life and strip his family of the means by which their life is kept.

#### The Two Partial Failures: Consuming Inherited Capital vs. Neglecting Material Foundations

When a household or a civilization separates survival from keeping and building, it falls into one of two classic errors:

1. **Surviving Without Building (Consuming Inherited Capital):**

    Suppose three generations inhabit one farmhouse. The grandfather cleared the forest, built the stone house, dug the well, and filled the barn with tools and seed grain. The father kept the ledger accurate, repointed the stone walls when frost cracked the mortar, and repaired the tools. Then comes the son. The son finds fieldwork tedious. Every year, he eats from the storehouse, sells off one more parcel of pasture and one more chest of his grandfather's tools (which he never learned how to sharpen) to buy wine and ease, lets the roof leak in the back wing, and teaches his one child nothing of soil, accounts, or carpentry. If you visit that farmhouse in year fifteen of the son's tenure, everyone at the dinner table is still breathing and still eating well. They are "surviving" by consuming capital they did not build and cannot repair. Ten years later, the storehouse is empty, the roof collapses, and the family is ruined.

2. **Aesthetic Refinement Detached from the Preconditions of Survival:**

    The opposite failure occurs in a wealthy, late-stage city. Inside a grand concert hall, a trained choir sings exquisitely about art, universal justice, sensitivity, and the beauty of the hall. The voices are refined and the sentiments are noble. Meanwhile, beneath the stage, nobody has inspected the foundation pilings in forty years; the young men and women in the choir deem manual trades, policing, and military defense beneath their dignity; and the singers have an average of 0.6 children per couple because children would interrupt their rehearsals. The song is real, the harmony is sophisticated, yet the decay in the structural timbers proceeds regardless of both. Within a generation, the floor gives way beneath the choir, and the hall belongs to whoever still knows how to carry a rifle, lay brick, and raise children.

#### Living Well Is a Rate of Improvement, Not a Static Condition

Finally, Principle 3 prevents both utopian perfectionism and despair in difficult times.

"Keeping and building" is not a static destination where a person or a society arrives at a finished state, puts down its tools, and lives effortlessly forever. In a world of continuous entropy, there is no final state in which maintenance ceases to be required.

Instead, living well is a **directed effort** measured by the **positive rate of improvement between Time A and Time B**:

* At Time A (8:00 a.m.), the cottage floor is covered in tracked mud and the sink is full of encrusted dishes. You pick up the broom and the washcloth. At Time B (8:30 a.m.), the floor is clean and the dishes are stacked dry on the shelf. That positive improvement between 8:00 and 8:30 *is* keeping and building in microcosm: human will and method imposing order on a small area of disorder.
* Because living well is measured by the **rate of improvement** rather than the initial stock of wealth, this orientation is available to a poor man in a dirt-floored cabin or a prisoner in a cell (who keeps his body disciplined, his cell clean, and his mind learning) just as fully as to a wealthy industrialist, while a wealthy heir who lets his health, his family, and his inherited enterprise deteriorate into addiction and disorder exhibits a negative trajectory despite his financial balance.

We now see what goodness must include: human life continuing, and that life kept and built. Next, we turn to the individual builder and identify the personal virtues required to keep and build in daily life.
