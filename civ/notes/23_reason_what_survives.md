## What is wrong now

`ccp_sheet.md`, Knowable, "Why it is important," says:

> Reason and sensory contact with reality are linked. A man knows the world by sensing it and by reasoning on what he senses.

Read alone, that makes reason a source of knowledge paired with the senses. It can be heard as the claim that reason stands before the measurement.

That pairing was applied on purpose. `notes/07_ccp_sheet_calls.md` call 20, and `notes/09_chapter_review_instructions.md` under Knowable, locked "sensory contact with reality and reason are linked." The author reopened it on 2026-11-17. Those call logs stay as history. This note overrides them for the next edit.

The rest of Principle 1 already has the limit. Truth: a belief is justified when it has been tested and holds, and logic can tell what is congruent inside a model but cannot tell whether the story is real. The Knowable paragraph is the sentence that still over-pairs reason with sensation.

## Sheet

Replace those two sentences. Keep the proxy detail, the convergence of models, and the airplanes. Proposed paragraph:

> **Why it is important:** Humans can obtain an accurate, working understanding of the world. What a man knows is what has survived contact with the world. His senses are proxies: the eye turns bands of light into nerve signals, and the ear does the same with variations in air pressure. Instruments are proxies he builds, and he learns how far to trust each one by testing it in different ways. Logic and reason are shortcuts to a test. They tell him what a test should show if a theory is right, and they tell him when a theory contradicts itself. They do not, by themselves, confirm a claim about the shared world. The world does that, by pushing back. Knowledge grows as models are tested against reality again and again, until they converge. People fly in airplanes and use telephones because engineers have learned the properties of materials and built processes that work.

Leave the range of views and "What it cannot accept" unless a sentence there repeats the old pairing. Intuition may suggest a hypothesis and does not confirm one. That stays.

"Shortcut" is the author's word. The sheet does not use it yet. Use it here. It names the job: a deduction tells you the result of a test you have not run, and it is only as good as premises that have already survived.
