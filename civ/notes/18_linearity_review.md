This is an editing aid for Rule 10 of the editing guide, not a verdict. A parser reads each sentence, and the tool scores it against the parts of the rule and drafts a rewrite where a mechanical one is safe. A parser misreads some sentences, and a draft rewrite can lose emphasis the sentence needs, so each flag is a reason to read the sentence again.

## 6. Method and limits

- **Parser.** Stanford CoreNLP 4.6.0: tokens, parts of speech, lemmas, named entities, the shift-reduce constituency parser with Universal Dependencies converted from it, and neural coreference (C. Manning and others, "The Stanford CoreNLP Natural Language Processing Toolkit," ACL 2014; K. Clark and C. Manning, "Deep Reinforcement Learning for Mention-Ranking Coreference Models," EMNLP 2016). Attachment is right for most words of edited prose, and coreference is right for roughly four pronouns in five, so a flag about a pronoun deserves the most care.
- **Text.** Headings, block quotations, and tables are left out. Markup is removed. Inline quotations stay, because they are part of the sentence a reader meets.
- **"New before old"** compares the words before the main verb and the last five words with the content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives) of the previous sentence in the same paragraph. A pronoun, a connective, or a shared word at the start counts as a link. The first sentence of a paragraph is not checked.
- **Draft rewrites** are made by moving the parsed phrase and fixing the capital letter. They do not change wording, so a moved phrase can need a new connective, and a named pronoun can repeat a noun too often. A draft is offered only where the parse passes several checks: one main clause, the rest of the sentence starting with its subject, no pronoun left ahead of its noun, no quotation split, and no colon at the end. The other findings name the problem and leave the rewrite to the editor.
- **Parser errors.** The parser sometimes misreads a sentence, for example taking a whole clause for the subject (02:13). A finding that does not match the sentence is a parse error and can be ignored.
- **Not measured.** Whether a sentence ends on its new point, beyond the overlap test above, and whether the argument follows the order of events, beyond "because."
