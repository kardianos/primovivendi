Let *s* be the Muslim share of women in the childbearing years, *m* their children per woman, and *n* the rate for everyone else. The share of the next generation, with no migration and no difference in survival, is

*s′ = (s × m) / (s × m + (1 − s) × n).*

Put *s* = 0.065, *m* = 2.6, *n* = 1.6, and hold the gap fixed, which real populations do not:

- Births: about 10 percent.
- Next generation of women, same gap: about 16 percent.
- The one after: about 23 percent.
- The one after that: about 33 percent.

Four turns of a fixed gap is on the order of a century. It is an illustration, not a forecast. #### Layer C: How Scope Defines the Nature of a Polity

One can determine the character of any civilization immediately by asking how it defines the scope of full legal symmetry in its public courts:

* **Classical Islamic Law (*Fiqh*):** In the classical caliphates and the Ottoman Empire, the scope of legal symmetry was explicitly religious. Full legal symmetry in the public court applied among free male Muslims. Conquered Christian, Jewish, and Zoroastrian communities (*dhimmis*) were placed in a separate, subordinate legal scope under rules drawn from **Qur'an 9:29**, the **Pact of Umar**, and the legal schools. They were permitted to keep their lives, property, and houses of worship so long as they paid the poll tax (***jizya***) in submission, held no public authority over Muslims, observed public restrictions on the display of their faith, and accepted a lower standing in court. This distinction was not a treatise left on a shelf. In Hanafi courts a non-Muslim's testimony was not admitted against a Muslim, and the jizya was collected as the tax on remaining non-Muslim. The Ottoman state abolished that fiscal and legal class in 1855 and 1856, which is how one knows it had been operating law. Within its declared religious scope, classical *fiqh* was internally consistent and rule-bound. It possessed courts, judges (*qadis*), and legal treatises. Uneven mercy in administration did not make the two scopes one scope. By law, it divided inhabitants of the same land into superior and subordinate tiers by religion.
* **Constitutional Civic Citizenship:** By contrast, Western constitutional law, as it worked out the British and American settlements through the nineteenth-century removal of religious tests and oath disabilities, defined the public scope of the courtroom by a single law in which religion is not a legal position. Inside the courtroom of a modern constitutional state, whether the plaintiff and defendant are Protestant, Catholic, Jewish, Muslim, or atheist, wealthy merchant or poor laborer, they occupy the single position of citizen before the law, and the court applies the same rules of contract, property, and evidence to both.

Those two answers do not make one polity. A non-Muslim will not naturally want to live under the first rule. A person who truly holds that distinction to be morally right is not compatible with a country that refuses to draw it.
