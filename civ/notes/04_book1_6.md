## 1. Epistemology & Testable Truth: New Distinctions and Concrete Pictures (Ch. 1100, 1200)
- **The Church and State of Knowledge (Test Rule):**
  - One jurisdiction for claims that reality can check (bridges, harvests, birth rates, crime counts, incentives, beam strength); another for claims that leave no empirical trail (the ultimate will of the divine, final metaphysics).
  - If a claim admits of trial in the world, bring it to trial in the world. A non-testable commitment may guide the believer privately, but may never cancel a failed empirical test ("when the bridge falls, no conviction above measurement makes the fall a success; nobody's God is insulted by an inspection report").
- **Three Distinct Questions in Grading a Claim (Correspondence vs. Claim as Made vs. Credit):**
  1. *Correspondence:* Did the proposition match the world?
  2. *The Claim as Made:* What was actually asserted, at what precision, and upon what reasons fixed at the time?
  3. *Credit:* May the speaker's process be trusted next time a map is needed? Lucky agreement with the world under bad grounds does not repair bad reasoning or earn future trust.
- **No "Victory Lap on Vagueness" (The Coastal Weather Card):**
  - One man says "there will be some wind tomorrow" by the shore; any breeze confirms him. Another writes a card the night before naming the quay, the hour, the wind measure, and the direction—specific enough to fail. Confirmability tracks how far a claim could have failed. Refuses rewriting claims after the outcome so the speaker always appears to have won.
- **Culture Worthiness vs. Wallowing in the Mud:**
  - Affirming that human life and thriving are good is *not* a claim that every culture is equally worthy of continuance. A culture can be judged by whether it helps its people thrive. *"If a tribe wallows in the mud, let no one be surprised when an iron-age civilization rolls over it without a thought."*
- **The Map Drawn to Please the Patron:**
  - A mapmaker moves a river half a day's walk east because the patron likes symmetry and leaves off a marsh because marshes look ugly on vellum. A traveler trusts the map, finds black mud instead of water, drinks marsh water by the second evening, and dies of fever by the end of the week. Every step of that death was a step of the flattering map.

## 2. Survival & Thriving: New Concrete Illustrations (Ch. 1300, 1400, 1500)
- **Drawn-Down Ancestral Capital (Three Generations in One House):**
  - The grandfather built the house and filled the storehouse; the father kept the accounts and repaired what weather broke; the son eats the grain, sells the tools he never learned to use, and teaches his one daughter nothing of planting, accounts, or roofs. Alive this year; failing across decades.
- **Thriving Talk Over a Collapsing Floor (The Choir in the Hall):**
  - A choir sings beautifully of justice, art, and the beauty of the hall while nobody maintains the floorboards, defense, food, or children. The voices are trained and the song is real, and the crack in the boards does not care.
- **The Lifted Shingle (How Unmaintained Creation Cancels the Making):**
  - Shingles overlap so water walks downhill off the eaves. Lift one shingle and leave the gap. Nothing happens that summer. Then a wet autumn: felt darkens, nail rusts, beam drinks. By the third winter, a soft spot in the ceiling and a smell the whole house learns. The builder's work is undone not by weather in a day, but by an owner who treated a made thing as permanent.
- **The Workplace Kitchen Sponge and Dripping Tap (Ownership):**
  - In a shared workplace kitchen, the sponge no one owns rots and the tap drips for a year because everyone assumes someone else reported it. What everyone owns, no one maintains—and where neglect grows costly enough, someone eventually strips.
- **The Master's Cold Forge (Names vs. Ten-Year Hands):**
  - When the master dies, his oiled tools hang ready. His sons can recite the name of every cut and temper ("the names took a season to learn"), but none of them can do the cuts ("the hands took ten years each, and the ten years were never given"). Within five years the family buys its nails like everyone else.
- **The Shepherd's Paddock, Dog, and Staff (Strength Underlies Kindness):**
  - A shepherd's kindness to his lambs in the cold dawn is real because the fence is real and the dog is awake. Remove the dog and staff and call the removal "gentleness": in October the wolf finds the gap, and what dies in the dark is precisely the kindness, paid in lambs.
- **Allow Failure; Do Not Court Failure; Do Not Engineer Failure (The Child's Footrace):**
  - A daughter runs a chalked footrace and loses by ten strides, scraping her palms. That loss is information with a heartbeat. Her parent times her intervals all summer; in August she loses by four strides—learning that her own work moves the gap.
  - Contrast with three failures: (1) canceling the race so no child loses (destroys the information); (2) breaking the faster runners' legs so times come out equal (outcome symmetry where no one can run); (3) refusing to teach running because winning is "privilege" or telling the child outcomes are a rigged lottery (engineered failure).
  - Also contrast *allowing failure in training* (the apprentice planing a board crooked—budgeted splinters) with *courting ruin* (betting the rent, wiring the house untrained).
- **"One Day" Narrative:**
  - A complete morning-to-night walkthrough of a single ordinary day lived under the virtues (stepping on the scale, checking the drawer against the ledger, repairing the gutter bracket at midday while narrating to the apprentice, sitting with the daughter who failed a math exam over the unstudied chapter, helping the neighbor with a freezer, refusing to lend the truck to the unrepentant cheat who was forgiven personally a year ago, walking the fence line, doing ten push-ups, eating at the family table).
- **Fraud vs. Honest Error as an Attack on the Shared Instrument:**
  - Honest error wants to be right and feeds correction when shown the world. Fraud (the merchant with a thumb on the scale, or a coordinated lie that makes checking expensive and punishes checking) attacks the *measuring instrument itself*. Protect sharp dissent; punish fraud.
- **The Farmer's Test Plot (Bounded Social Experiment):**
  - A farmer stakes a small square beside the main field for new seed and watches it through sprout, storm, and harvest before expanding or killing it. Social policies require written terms of success *before* the trial begins, bounded scope, and the willingness to kill failed programs even when sponsors weep.

## 3. Two Parallel "Falls off the Boat": Cushions and Standards (Ch. 1600, 1700)
- **Fall 1: The Harmlessness Entitlement (From Good Cushion to Substitute Life):**
  - *The Good Cushion:* A time-bound bridge over a short, bridgable shock (the October-to-April rotated spare pantry; the brother's seed loan and Saturday barn-raising repaid at harvest; the highway emergency room where nobody proposes the patient move into the hospital; the planks laid over a flooded stream for crossing vs. the man who builds his house mid-stream on the planks and brings down the bridge).
  - *The Slide:* From "I should not die from a two-month layoff" → "I should not feel scarcity while others have more" → "I ought never be harmed, blocked, ranked, refused, or made uncomfortable."
  - *Protection vs. Frustration:* The locked door that protects the shopkeeper's stock frustrates the thief's will; the examiner failing the unprepared student harms his pride; the officer arresting the burglar harms his evening. Counting every frustrated will as an injustice outlawing all protection.
  - *Refusing the Reactionary False Fix:* The cruel overcorrection ("tear down every cushion, let the idle starve, might makes right") fails the good of life and mistakes cruelty for strength, throwing out the pantry, kin loan, and emergency room along with the entitlement.
  - *Four Cushion Design Tests:* (1) For a shock or a career of dependence? (2) Preserves life without killing work and formation? (3) Time-limited, reciprocal/work-conditioned where able, tiered (kin/neighbors first), fraud-policed? (4) When help ends after recovery, does the person stand?
- **Fall 2: Standards in Making (Time, Perverse Incentives, Second/Third-Order Effects):**
  - *The Joiner's Shop:* Master teaches eye, hand, tool, test (mark with grain, saw on waste side, pare with chisel, thumb-test joint). When the school closes and the son is raised on "measuring is servitude," the shop can still sell essays about chairs, but the chairs rock on uneven legs.
  - *First Breakers vs. Third Cohort:* The first cohort that rebels against convention still had the classical training in their hands; their followers inherit only the smashing. Grant juries and committees reward shock and essay-writing over skill (1st order), cut drafting/ear-training/shop hours from curricula (2nd order), and produce a public that can no longer tell a plumb wall from a leaning one (3rd order).
  - *Refusing the Museum Freeze:* Banning all new work after a chosen year and funding only copies also fails; a museum holds a standard but cannot put it into a living hand. The positive discipline is: **drill fundamentals first, copy excellence to build the hand, then make new work that answers to order, truth, and vitality.**

## 4. Three Long-Horizon Lenses & Red-Green Coordination Mechanics (Ch. 1800, 2600)
- **Three Lenses for Reading Premises Over Time:**
  1. *Generational lens (The Granary):* A son mocks the boring rule ("measure, dry, store, never eat seed grain") and still eats well for years from bins his father filled; his daughter inherits the mockery and an empty bin.
  2. *Scale lens (The Monastery vs. The State):* A small self-selected monastery can hold property in common under its rule; a state commanding the same for fifty million must conscript the unwilling and police the rest. Never grade a premise only by its founding generation of saints.
  3. *Interconnect lens:* Second- and third-order effects across linked systems (e.g., compassion that weakens enforcement multiplies victims; open-border talk breaks safety-net arithmetic).
- **Two Crews Pulling Down One Wall (Red-Green Coordination Mechanics):**
  - Two crews want incompatible buildings on the lot behind a wall, but the wall (neutral law, borders, free inquiry, family norms, merit) blocks both. Pulling the ropes together is cheap because stone is a concrete target; once the wall falls, the crew with more hands and fewer scruples builds alone.
  - Specific mechanics: **Shield and Sword** (progressive partner supplies narrative cover, institutional access, and two-tier scrutiny; hard sacral partner supplies street numbers, discipline, and readiness to dominate), **Dual Codes**, **Humiliation Politics**, **Speech Sacralization** (explicit and implicit blasphemy codes), and **Entryism**.
- **Purity Procedure vs. Reality Procedure (Ch. 2600):**
  - *Purity procedure:* Sorts people into clean and unclean by membership, class, sex, or creed, and loads moral law from the sort ("who you are, not what you did, decides your standing").
  - *Reality procedure:* The same basic bans (force, fraud, predation) and duties bind everyone in the same position under the facts, and virtue is a cultivated capacity in both sexes—neither a birth halo nor a mere checklist of avoided taboos.
- **Both "Self-Made Marble" and "Pure Luck Lottery" Erase Formed Agency (Ch. 2600):**
  - The self-made fiction claims a person sculpts himself from nothing (erasing debt/gratitude to household and lineage); the luck-lottery fiction claims circumstance decides everything (erasing earned ownership and duty). Reality: persons are *formed agents*, owing uncontracted gratitude to the chain that raised them and deserving what their own labor adds.

## 5. Vivid Parables for Rigged Tests, Feuds, Leniency, and Rupture (Ch. 2100–2500)
- **The Reinvention Test (Ch. 2100):** Ban the official form and watch what people do. Costume stays banned; substance comes back illegally wearing whatever costume is still legal (black-market money, informal watchman ownership of the common orchard, grandmothers becoming mothers again when family forms are abolished on paper).
- **The One-Way Check Valve vs. The Shopkeeper's Ledger (Ch. 2200):** Hermetic arguments work like a hinged pipe flap that swings open when upstream credit flows in and slams shut against downstream blame. The fix is the shopkeeper's single ledger: list three claimed successes and three failures of a package and ask if one theory of cause explains all six.
- **The Feud Book That Never Closes (Ch. 2300):** Under value symmetry ("eye for eye"), each household records the latest wounding as the first entry in its own ledger, never agrees on the exchange rate between a dead son and a burned barn, and teaches its sons the outstanding balance before the plow while the fields between them go unplanted.
- **The Hill Town Grain Storehouse and the Rats (Ch. 2400):** A town council votes that rat traps are cruel because rats did not choose to be rats. The rats do not attend council meetings; they keep eating. Only the address changes: the miller's stone bins and innkeeper's iron-banded bins hold, so the rats eat through the thin plank bins of the widow and day laborer. Leniency never deletes harm; it moves the bill to the weakest bin.
- **The Peasant's Cow Stall in 1930s Collectivization (Ch. 2500):** An instruction arrives that the cow now belongs to the collective. The peasant has a choice, not a cow: hand it over and watch the milk go to someone else's child, or slaughter it and feed his own. He slaughters, his neighbor slaughters, and a country of stalls empties in a season—no livestock, no manure, no draft power, no harvest, and the 1932–33 famine.
