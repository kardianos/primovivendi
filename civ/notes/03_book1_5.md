## 1. Public Test for "Refining" vs. "Inverting" the Principles (Ch. 0101)
- Disagreement over *application* (how far a duty reaches in a hard case, how thriving is measured) is not the same as abandonar or inverting the principles.
- **Public test:** Does the person still affirm a knowable world, permanent human limits (including that virtue is cultivated), life continuing and kept/built as the good, ranked real duties, and the same rules within an honest scope?
  - If **yes**: the dispute is application inside the shared form. Correction inside the form is humility.
  - If **no**: they have left the form and stand on a different foundation.

## 2. Refutation of "Person-Affecting" / Consent-Only Ethics (Ch. 0201)
- **The Rival:** A seemingly rigorous, humane rival starts from consenting individuals: duties run *only* to existing subjects who can agree, refuse, or be harmed ("no ghost claims, no abstract humanity, no forcing the unborn into a ledger").
- **Follow it to the end:** On a pure person-affecting or consent-only standard, **a planet with no life and a planet thick with flourishing people come out morally identical**. No one is wronged by an empty world; no one is benefited by fullness who would otherwise have been an existing subject on the ledger.
- **Refutation:** A morality that cannot say a living world is better than a dead rock has already chosen indifference to life. Consent and peer procedure are load-bearing *inside* a living order (Principle 5), not the foundation of the good (Principle 3). Flourishing human life is the standard that makes every ledger possible.

## 3. Pattern Recognition Instead of Rebuilding a Full Rival Creed (Ch. 0201, 0503)
- You do not need to invent a complete five-pillar twin philosophy for every critic who holds a fragment of error.
- **Pattern Recognition:** Map the move to the specific principle it contradicts (e.g., narrative truth vs. Principle 1; virtue as factory default vs. Principle 2; anti-natalism vs. Principle 3; kin preference as bigotry vs. Principle 4; unequal results as proof of unjust rules or "eye for eye" vs. Principle 5).
- Holding one bad pattern does not entail holding all of them, though patterns often travel in clusters (campus constructivist dialect, hard political-theological package, reactionary anti-procedure package). Refuse the specific move without steelmanning an entire imaginary creed for the speaker.

## 4. Singapore (1965–1967) Expanded Primary Touchpoints (Ch. 0301, 0302, 0303)
- **December 1965 speech ("The world does not owe us a living"):** Lee Kuan Yew begins not with a theory of justice or a petition to history, but with an inventory of reality after separation from Malaysia (August 1965): a small island, almost no hinterland or natural resources, uncertain British base, unsentimental neighbors.
- **1966 speech ("Not a digestible morsel"):** Facing hard scarcity and hard neighbors, Lee asks whether Singapore will raise a soft, pleasure-loving society or a "rugged, robust, disciplined effective society," so that "small though we may be, this place is not a digestible morsel."
- **1967 speech on Rule of Law:** Lee calls it cause for "quiet congratulations" that no one expects execution "at the whim and fancy of somebody else," contrasting Singapore with regimes where chief justices were detained for acquitting the wrong defendant or where relatives were appointed to the bench so the *ratio decidendi* would be understood in advance. Bounded power is survival equipment, not a luxury of large continents.
- **1967 speech on Uneven Talent ("Yeast, Ferment, Catalyst"):** Human capability is uneven and time is scarce; concentrating formation on the top 5–10% (while also insisting that character, grit, and integrity are not guaranteed by test scores) acts as "yeast, ferment, catalyst" to lift the whole society.
- **Methodological boundary ("Take the premise, not every tool"):** Singapore also used hard measures under siege (detention powers, corporal punishment) that free polities may reject while keeping the underlying premise: the world does not owe a living, human limits are design inputs, and competence/order are required for survival.

## 5. Public Square Rule: Inspectable Grounds vs. Uncheckable Authority (Ch. 0301)
- **Two engineers arguing about a bridge:** If one says "build it this way because my book says so" and the other says "I don't accept that book," they are not yet arguing about spans, loads, or flood seasons. Until both agree that steel, wind, and water get the final veto, no calculation can settle the dispute.
- **Capital punishment / public policy parallel:** Saying "my holy book requires it" stops debate at the premise for anyone who does not share the book. Saying "enemies are real, and a criminal trial's public work is to protect victims and the living order" gives inspectable grounds (Do enemies exist here? What happens where this practice is used or abandoned?) that citizens of different faiths can test and reality can grade.

## 6. Historical Contrasts in Law, Property, and Rupture (Ch. 0303, 0401, 0403, 0404)
- **Bound Law vs. Redesigned Man in Antiquity (Solon & Medes/Persians vs. Lycurgus's Sparta):**
  - *Solon's Athens:* Put written, public law in place of blood-feud and factional whim so limited, self-interested citizens could live together without eating the city.
  - *Law of the Medes and Persians:* Remembered as law that bound even the king's casual whim—power under a known order, not power as the order.
  - *Lycurgus's Sparta:* Equal outcomes and shared mess among the peer caste of full Spartiates, resting on a helot underclass outside that equality. Spartan boys in training were taught to steal to survive, where disgrace was not the theft but being caught—rewarding stealth rather than civic procedural symmetry. Modern rupture regimes repeat Sparta without the bronze: official outcome equality among the elect, an underclass that feeds them, and gray markets/favors as the real rule when stated law cannot be lived.
- **Russia: Reform Interrupted by Rupture (Stolypin & Durnovo vs. Lenin & Collectivization):**
  - *Stolypin's land reforms:* However incomplete, wagered on property-holding peasants—channeling self-interest through ownership rather than abolishing it.
  - *Durnovo's 1914 Memorandum:* Warned that a great European war risked "social revolution in its most extreme form."
  - *Lenin's April Theses (1917):* Refused support for the Provisional Government and demanded a second stage transferring power to the proletariat and poorest peasants—treat the existing machine as illusion to smash rather than a discovered constraint to improve.
  - *Soviet Collectivization (Cheremukhin et al. economic reconstruction):* Banning private agricultural markets and forcing peasants into collectives met reality's veto: peasants slaughtered livestock, agricultural productivity collapsed, and famine followed.
- **Iran (1978–1983): Tactical Alliance of Incompatible Packages, Then Purge ("Red and Black"):**
  - Under Mohammad Reza Pahlavi (White Revolution), top-down modernization pushed literacy, industrial capacity, and legal reforms while remaining authoritarian.
  - Leftists (Tudeh, guerrilla groups) and Islamists formed what the Shah called an "unholy alliance of Red and Black" against the reforming order.
  - Khomeini's *Islamic Government* (1970) had already stated the package openly: the ruler's task "is not legislation, but the implementation of the divine laws."
  - Once the shared enemy fell in 1979, the Islamist faction consolidated power and purged its former leftist allies through arrest, trial, and execution in the early 1980s.
  - **General Law:** Rupture coalitions of incompatible premises are temporary alignments, not friendships. When the shared enemy falls, one package eats the other.

## 7. Naturalization as Roman Civic Adoption (*Pietas*) (Ch. 0303)
- Naturalization is neither a thin commercial contract nor pure blood mysticism.
- **Classical Roman Adoption Analogy:** A naturalized citizen is like an adopted child entering a civic family. Membership confers citizen rights and demands *pietas*—loyalty and reverence toward the actual people, history, laws, and institutions that opened the door (not merely abstract universals).
- Too many adoptions at once overwhelm a household; an adoptee who holds the family's public order in contempt violates the adoption. Blood is not required for civic membership, but reciprocal loyalty to the concrete constitutional order is.

## 8. Islamic Intellectual Strands: Secondary Causes vs. Occasionalism (Ch. 0405)
- Nuance added to the critique of Ash'arite occasionalism and political Islam: the Islamic intellectual tradition is not a single mind.
- Strands that affirm secondary causes and a stable created order—parts of the *falsafa* tradition, **Mu'tazilite rationalism**, and **Ibn Rushd (Averroes)** insisting on necessary causal connection—can sit with correspondence truth and inquiry in the same way Two-Books Christianity does.
- The conflict is with **doctrinal and political packages** (strict occasionalism, *taqiyya* boundary management, *jizya* two-tier subordination, Khomeini's divine-law sovereignty) that sever truth from testable regularity or subordinate civic procedure to sacral monopoly—never a collective suspicion of persons by ancestry or surname.

## 9. Detailed Critique of Alexander Dugin and Anti-Success "Conservatism" (Ch. 0406)
- **The Surface-Aligned Rival:** Alexander Dugin (*The Fourth Political Theory*, *Foundations of Geopolitics*) wears the label "conservative" against thin late-liberalism, consumerism, and atomized individualism, proposing a collective Eurasian civilizational subject, a "Conservative Revolution," and the moral claim: *"The very concept of the success is deeply immoral. If you are successful the other is loser. We shouldn't accept it. We should prosper or suffer together. Otherwise it is satanic."*
- **Point-by-Point Refutation via the Principles:**
  1. *"Success is immoral because another must lose":* False zero-sum picture of reality (violates Principles 1 & 3). A harvest coming in, a bridge holding, or a baker prospering by baking good bread does not make another a "loser." Voluntary exchange under specialization raises both sides (as shown from Bronze Age Assur to Singapore). Calling competence and thriving "satanic" moralizes a false model of reality.
  2. *"We must prosper or suffer together":* This is **outcome symmetry** in sacred/collectivist dress (violates Principles 3 & 5). Equal misery is not goodness; choosing shared suffering as moral form is choosing decline and calling it communion.
  3. *"Replace the individual political subject with a collective-civilizational subject":* Dissolving the individual moral agent—who knows, acts, owns, parents, and answers under law—into empire-by-destiny removes the unit that can be held responsible under procedural symmetry (violates Principles 2 & 5), handing unchecked license to whoever claims to speak for the collective.
  4. *"Cohesion by common enemy against market-liberal values":* Conserving tested arrangements is Burkean; defining a civilization primarily by hatred of an external enemy rather than knowable reality and reciprocal procedure among citizens trains a polity for war and internal purge.
