## 7. Method and limits

- **Tokens.** Words are folded to lower case. Numbers are dropped. Phrases never cross punctuation, because Google counts punctuation marks as tokens. A hyphen stays in a phrase as its own token, as in Google ("self - interest"). An apostrophe ends a phrase and its suffix is dropped, because the Ngram Viewer does not look up possessives reliably.
- **Google Books.** Dates and OCR are imperfect, and the collection's mix shifts toward scientific and technical books across the period (Pechenick, Danforth, and Dodds, 2015). Every book counts once, however widely it was read. A phrase absent from the period is a reason to look at the phrase, not proof that nobody wrote it.
- **Reference corpus.** 36 works chosen for the treatise register the editing guide names: law, government, economics, industry, engineering, science, history, conduct, and essays. All are in the US public domain, so none is later than 1930; the Google counts cover 1931 to 1950. Front matter that survives the Gutenberg cleanup is counted.
- **Delta** measures distance in common-word habits. It does not measure quality, and a distance from the period mean is not by itself a fault.
- **Not measured.** Sentence order and linearity (editing guide, Rule 10) are read, not counted.
