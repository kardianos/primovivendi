## 5. Distance in common-word habits (Burrows's Delta)

Delta measures how far a text's rates for the 150 most frequent words of the reference corpus lie from the corpus mean, in standard deviations, averaged over the words. It reflects habits of grammar and connection more than topic. For scale, each reference work is measured against the mean of the other 35.

Cosine Delta compares the pattern of those standard deviations, word by word, with the pattern of one reference work, and ignores how large they are, so one or two extreme words move it less. It is 0 for the same pattern and 1 for an unrelated one. In tests of authorship it is the most reliable of the Delta measures (Evert and others, 2017). Works by different authors point in unrelated directions from the mean, so the measure here is the distance to the nearest reference work: how close the text comes to some writer of the period. For scale, each reference work is measured against the nearest of the other 35.

- **The book:** Delta 1.15; 35 of the 36 reference works lie closer to the mean. Cosine Delta to the nearest work 0.71; 32 of the 36 works have a nearer neighbor.
- **Reference works, Delta:** nearest 0.56 (Walter Lippmann, *Public Opinion*), median 0.80, farthest 1.60 (Michael Faraday, *The Chemical History of a Candle*).

## 8. Method and limits

- **Tokens.** Words are folded to lower case. Numbers are dropped. Phrases never cross punctuation, because Google counts punctuation marks as tokens. A hyphen stays in a phrase as its own token, as in Google ("self - interest"). An apostrophe ends a phrase and its suffix is dropped, because the Ngram Viewer does not look up possessives reliably.
- **Google Books.** Dates and OCR are imperfect, and the collection's mix shifts toward scientific and technical books across the period (Pechenick, Danforth, and Dodds, 2015). Every book counts once, however widely it was read. A phrase absent from the period is a reason to look at the phrase, not proof that nobody wrote it.
- **Reference corpus.** 36 works chosen for the treatise register the editing guide names: law, government, economics, industry, engineering, science, history, conduct, and essays. All are in the US public domain, so none is later than 1930; the Google counts cover 1931 to 1950. Front matter that survives the Gutenberg cleanup is counted.
- **Delta** measures distance in common-word habits. It does not measure quality, and a distance from the period mean is not by itself a fault.
- **Not measured.** Sentence order and linearity (editing guide, Rule 10) are read, not counted.
